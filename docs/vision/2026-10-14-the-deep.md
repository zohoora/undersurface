# The Deep: notes toward a magical UnderSurface

**Date:** 2026-10-14
**Status:** Creative vision. Nothing here is scoped or approved. Each idea below would need its own spec before it is built.
**Companion file:** `TECH_ISSUES_2026-10-14.md` (repo root) holds the technical findings for a coder agent.

---

## The one-sentence version

A chat box is all surface. UnderSurface should be the one place where your inner life has **depth**: things that sink and settle, things that live down there, weather, tides, sound, and a floor you can stand on.

---

## What I saw

The care in this codebase is extraordinary: the crisis stack, the grounding invariants, writer-governed memory, the "success includes needing less help" ethic, the refusal to let the model grade itself, Drift's no-return invariant. That ethic can't be copied by prompting ChatGPT. It is the best thing here.

The raw materials for magic are also already here: seven inner voices with their own memories, a heart-rate strap that streams chest motion, a voice channel, generated soundscapes, an SMS line into the day, a reflection engine that runs after every session, companion promises with dates, a pause detector, an ink-weight extension, a breathing background.

But the app has drifted from an art piece toward a **lab instrument**, and you can see it in three places.

1. **Measurement inside ritual moments.** The July "make personal science ambient" pass already pulled the worst of the lab out of view: the receptivity handshake ("Three signals help the lab compare like with like"), the outcome pulses, research moments, and the Field Manual are no longer reachable. That was the right move. What remains still measures at the wrong moment. An "Ambiguity prism" card asks "What was it like to sort these? easy to sort / took some work / created more work," and every return from a surface arc lands on a rating row: "softer / clearer · about the same · stronger / worse · not what I needed." These are the right instincts, but they put a clipboard in the room.
2. **Everything arrives as a card.** Up to eight kinds of card can attach to Talk: ambiguity prism, conductor offer, surface-arc guide and return, part proposal, value proposal, intention, field mosaic, recommendation segments. Each one is reasonable on its own. Together they feel like forms.
3. **The most poetic engines were orphaned by the pivot.** `letterEngine` still writes letters from the parts, but no user-facing component renders them; they appear only in the admin dashboard's "Letters" tab. Rituals, inner weather, echoes, fossils, and the body map are diary-era ideas that never found a home in the session world.

The landing page shows the same drift. It is tasteful and calm, and it could be a productivity tool. The name promises depth; what you see is beige paper.

One thing to keep exactly as it is: **the companion's plain voice.** `THERAPIST_CORE` bans poster-quote language, stacked metaphors, and "delve." That rule is correct. If you ask ChatGPT to be magical, it writes purple prose. UnderSurface should do the opposite.

## The governing image (it is literally true)

The deep-ocean floor is built from the skeletons of tiny creatures. Radiolarians and diatoms live near the light, die, and drift down as *marine snow*. Over millennia they become **radiolarian ooze**, the sediment that carpets the abyss. Ernst Haeckel drew them in *Kunstformen der Natur* (1904), and they are some of the most beautiful objects in biology.

So:

> Every session you have sinks, and becomes part of the ground you stand on.

That gives the product its metaphysics for free:

- **Grounding** (the crisis mode's real name) means reaching ground that you built yourself.
- **Memory** is sediment, not a database: layered, compacted, sometimes eroded, and sometimes it surfaces.
- **The parts** are what lives at different depths.
- **Time** is depth.

---

## Five laws

**I. Plain voice, magical world.** The words stay plain. Form, time, space, sound, and objects carry the magic. Metaphor lives in the visuals and in the names of things, never in the companion's prose, and buttons still say "Keep," not "Cast thy stone."

**II. Many voices, not one assistant.** ChatGPT is one voice. Here you are never talking to "an AI"; you are in a place with inhabitants, and some of them are yours.

**III. Time is a material.** Things ripen, arrive late, fade, return at the tide. Slowness and scarcity are features that an always-on chat box can't have.

**IV. The world is the interface.** State shows up as environment (light, water, weather, sound), not as cards and pills.

**V. It wants you to leave.** The transfer ladder already says "success includes needing less help." Make that the most beautiful part of the work: an ending that is designed.

And a safety law that overrides the other five: **Grounding brings you to shore.** When distress is detected, the deep closes: the light is warm, the horizon is level, there is land underfoot, and every flourish below is suppressed, exactly as every companion program is suppressed today.

## The map: surfaces become depths

The four surfaces already form a gradient from many words to none. Make it spatial.

| Depth | Today | What happens there |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface** | Talk | Daylight, conversation, the most words. |
| **Shallows** | Journal | Your words alone. Lights (the parts) glimmer below and sometimes rise. |
| **Midwater** | Speak | Voice. Text gives way to sound. |
| **The Deep** | Room | Sound and silence. Almost no words. |
| **The Floor** | History (now the sidebar) | Everything that has settled. |

The bottom-bar pills become a **depth gauge**. Moving between surfaces becomes descending and ascending: the light attenuates, sound muffles slightly, type grows larger and sparser as you go down, and it all reverses as you come up. The conductor's "surface arcs" become *currents*: "There's a current toward quiet. Follow it?" A session doesn't switch tabs. It goes somewhere.

## Specimens

Each idea notes what already exists in the repo and a rough size (S/M/L).

### The world

**1. Marine snow: every session leaves a specimen.** *(M)*
When a session closes, it leaves behind one radiolarian, generated deterministically from the session itself: radial symmetry from how many voices spoke, spine count from turn count, lattice density from how much silence there was, chambers from surfaces visited, and a hue from the inner-weather palette. No LLM and no image model, just seeded procedural geometry, so it is cheap, private, and never kitsch. You start collecting a cabinet without trying.
*Exists:* `weatherEngine`, `voiceFieldColors`, session records, reflection pass.
*Rule:* form encodes **process** (how many voices, how much quiet), never **valence** (good day or bad day). A collection of "dark spiky bad days" would turn mood into a scorecard.

**2. The floor: history as a sediment core.** *(M)*
Replace the sidebar list with a vertical core sample. Scrolling down goes back in time. Each session is a layer whose thickness reflects its length, with its specimen embedded in it. Recurring themes show up as strata that recur. Tap a layer to read that session. Older layers compact; layers you return to stay loose. The core is also **playable**: each specimen has a few tones derived from its geometry (Web Audio, synthesized locally), so dragging through a month sounds like a music box.
*Exists:* `EntriesList`, session notes, `memoryPruner` (compaction is already conceptually there).

**3. Weather is the water.** *(S/M)*
Inner weather stops being a widget and becomes the medium: fog on the surface, rain rings, swell, glassy calm. The emotional atmosphere variants in `atmosphere.css` are already halfway there.

**4. Breath tide.** *(S)*
With a Polar H10 (or webcam respiration), the waterline at the top of the page rises and falls with your actual breath. `getBreathingScale()` is already a stable rAF-pollable getter, and Relax already does this with the breath circle. Take it out of Relax and give it to the whole world.

**5. The undertext.** *(M, privacy-sensitive)*
When you delete a sentence in Journal, it doesn't vanish. It drifts down as faint silt beneath the page. Hold a key (or tilt the phone) and the palimpsest appears: what you almost said. The Watcher already cares about abandoned sentences, and this makes its subject visible. Kept local to the device by default, and opt-in.

### Many voices

**6. Lights in the dark.** *(M)*
The parts are bioluminescence at different depths. The Spark flickers near the surface, the Watcher is a slow lantern at the edge, and the Still is deep and steady. When a part is about to speak, its light rises toward the text. When you confirm one of *your* parts (the `userParts` program), a new light appears that you named. Over a year, the dark fills with your inner population.

**7. The Council.** *(M)*
For a hard decision, convene the parts. Each one speaks once, from its concern and in its own color. They're allowed to disagree (`disagreementEngine` exists for this). The Still speaks last. You sit at the head of the table and you speak the decision, not them. It is a structured ritual with a beginning and an end, not "give me pros and cons."

**8. A chorus in space.** *(L)*
Give each part its own Realtime voice and a position in stereo space: the Watcher a little to the left and far, the Tender close, the Still behind you. In Speak or the Deep, your interior becomes a quiet radio play. The panned-audio code from Drift's bilateral stimulus is the starting point. Nothing in a chat window can do this.

**9. Letters from the deep.** *(S, and the engine already exists)*
Resurrect `letterEngine`. Letters arrive sealed, in the writer's color, and **you choose when to open them**. A part that hasn't been heard from in weeks writes to say so. Later: parts write to *each other*, and you can read their correspondence about you. Today these letters are generated and then readable only by an admin. That should be fixed either way.

### Time

**10. Overnight.** *(M)*
Before sleep you hand the companion a question. It says it'll hold it overnight. At your morning field window, the answer arrives by SMS, short and plain and slept-on. This is the companion-promise mechanism (`#promise:YYYY-MM-DD`) joined to the field-window scheduler. A chat box answers instantly, always. Waiting is the magic.

**11. The tide.** *(S/M)*
`ritualEngine` already detects when you tend to come. Make that your high tide: at your hour the water is up and the deep is easiest to reach. At low tide, things wash up: a line from a month ago, a dormant part's light, an unopened letter. The app gets a rhythm that is yours, not a notification schedule.

**12. Dissolve.** *(S)*
A mode where what you write is **never kept**: no transcript, no reflection, no memory. You write, and when you finish it dissolves into the water while you watch. Fresh eyes made the companion forget the past; Dissolve lets the present go. It is a true ephemeral space, which no assistant that trains on or remembers your chats can promise. (L1 and L2 crisis checks still run transiently on the text before it is discarded.)

### The field

**13. The shoreline.** *(S, a reskin of Field Mosaic)*
What you texted or photographed during the day washes up as objects on a shoreline at the start of the next session: a photo, a line, a time. You beachcomb. You can pick one up and bring it into the water, or leave it. The companion never interprets the shoreline unless you hand it something.

### Leaving

**14. The tide goes out.** *(M)*
As the transfer ladder shows a writer doing more on their own, the companion's light dims and it speaks less. Eventually, when the evidence is there, it proposes a farewell: a letter that names what you built, the parts you met, your own words for things. You can always come back. But the product has an ending, and the ending is the most carefully made thing in it. No engagement-optimized product would ship this, and that is exactly why it belongs here.

**15. The plate and the book.** *(M/L)*
At a year, or at a farewell, your specimens are composed into a Haeckel-style plate, *Kunstformen* of one inner life, ready for large-format print. (The tiling and print pipeline you're building for MapForge is directly reusable here.) Alongside it there's a small book: your words (not the AI's), your glossary of coined phrases, and the letters. An export becomes an object you can hold.

### Others (optional, trust-sensitive)

**16. Distant lights.** *(S/M)*
Faint lights at the edge of the deep mark other people who are in a session right now. Counts only: no content, no location, no profiles, no interaction. At 3 a.m. it says one true thing that a chat box can't: *you are not the only one awake down here.*

**17. The empty chair.** *(M)*
The Gestalt technique as a spatial ritual. You speak to someone in the chair. The companion **never plays them**; instead you switch seats and answer as them yourself, and in voice the app pans your voice left or right as you move. This builds on the `user_first` grammar and respects the lesson of the removed Future Self mode: the AI doesn't impersonate people. You do the imagining.

## Transmutation: the lab becomes the myth

The research rails are good, so keep them. The idea is to keep **every data contract identical** and change only what the writer sees and does.

| Today (visible in the app now) | Tomorrow (world) | Data kept |
|---|---|---|
| Surface-arc return card ("softer / clearer · about the same · stronger / worse") | As you come back up, a small stone is in your hand. Keep it, or let it sink. | helped / nothing / worse / not wanted |
| Ambiguity prism, three hypotheses | Three lights at different depths. Bring up the one that fits. | fits / maybe / not this |
| Intention card | A bottle you throw forward to next time | intention text + value |
| Part proposal card | A new light appears and waits for a name | name / dismissed |
| Value proposal card | A light on the shore, the thing you steer by. You name it. | value name / dismissed |
| Companion promise | A lantern the companion carries until delivered | promise + due date |
| Conductor offer | A current: "There's a current toward quiet." | accepted / declined |
| Session note | What washed ashore | note text |
| Field Mosaic | The shoreline (specimen 13) | kept / dismissed / connected |

The outcome pulse, receptivity handshake, closing check-in and Field Manual are already out of the UI. If their data is ever wanted again, these are their forms: a stone, a drag of the waterline (calm, choppy, murky), surfacing ("What did you bring up?"), and an almanac of tide tables.

The metaphor goes into the object and the motion. The microcopy stays plain enough that a first-time writer never has to decode it.

## Subtractions

An art piece is defined as much by what it leaves out.

- **Measurement in ritual moments.** "Ambiguity prism," "easy to sort / created more work," and a rating row after every return. The July ambient-science pass started this; finish it. Keep the data, lose the clipboard.
- **Card sprawl.** Eight card types collapse into a single object grammar: stones, lights, bottles, lanterns, currents.
- **Flag sprawl and dead code.** About 40 feature flags, many from the diary era, plus roughly 4,500 lines of unreachable Relax, Drift and personal-science UI since Room landed. Give the ideas that serve the deep a home and delete the rest.
- **Ad trackers on the threshold.** The landing page loads Google Ads tags. On the doorway to a private reflection space that is tonally wrong, and the site's own CSP blocks them anyway (see the tech doc).

## A first descent

Here's the order that gives the most magic for the least work, and builds a foundation the rest can sit on.

1. **The depth axis.** Navigation as descent and ascent, plus "grounding brings you to shore." This is mostly CSS, transitions, and copy, and it reframes every existing surface at once.
2. **Marine snow and the floor.** Specimens plus the sediment-core history. Deterministic, cheap, private, and it gives every session a beautiful residue.
3. **Letters from the deep.** The engine exists; it needs a sealed-envelope UI and an "open when you choose."
4. **Overnight.** Promises, field windows, and SMS are all already built.

Then, in roughly this order: Lights in the dark, The Council, The tide, Dissolve, The tide goes out, the chorus, the plate and the book.

## Guardrails (this is a clinical-adjacent product)

- **The person never sinks.** Water, depth, and "going under" overlap with the language of suicidal ideation that `SAFETY_RULES` already watches for ("rest," "let go," "peace"). The rule: *words* sink and settle, while the *person* is buoyant, held, and lit. No drowning imagery, no "let go" or "sink" in copy anywhere near distress, and the deep is always luminous, never suffocating.
- **Grounding switches the world off.** Shore, horizon, warm light, fixed i18n copy. No specimens, lights, letters, or currents during grounding, and no Council or chorus.
- **No valence scorekeeping.** Specimens, strata, and plates encode process, not how good or bad a day was.
- **Dissolve still runs crisis checks**, transiently, before discarding.
- **Nobody is impersonated.** Not the writer's future self, not the person in the empty chair.
- **Distant lights carry counts only**, never content or identity.
- **The undertext stays local**, opt-in, and is never sent to the model unless the writer hands it over.

---

## Why this can't be copied by a chat box

ChatGPT's form is fixed by its universality: one voice, always on, the same white page for everyone, no body, no calendar, no ending. UnderSurface can have a point of view, a world with inhabitants, a body that breathes with yours, a tide that knows your hour, letters that take time to arrive, and an ending. **The moat isn't the model. It's the world.**
