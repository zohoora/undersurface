## 0. For reviewers

### 0.1 What this document is

A proposal for a second, separate way of using UnderSurface.

Today the app is a companion: you open it and a conversation starts. **Folio** is a writing tool: you open it and there is a quiet page. The companion still exists, but it only speaks when invited. The current system stays exactly as it is and stays one tap away. This document calls it **Sessions** (the owner calls it "legacy").

If you don't know the codebase, read Appendix C first. It explains the existing safety stack, surfaces and data model in a page.

### 0.2 Decisions the owner has already made

These aren't up for debate here, but tell us if one of them causes a problem.

1. Writing tool first, not companion first.
2. Folio is a complete, separate mode that lives alongside Sessions. Both stay usable.
3. The crisis-safety stack covers Folio just as it covers Sessions.

### 0.3 What we most want you to attack

1. Is the core gesture (lift a line, write beneath it) actually new and worth building, or is it an outline or comment feature in disguise?
2. Where does this plan quietly turn into a notes app?
3. **Safety.** Folio is a writing tool that doesn't answer you by default. Is the coverage in §5 enough? Is it too much, given the false positives that literary writing will produce?
4. **Editor architecture (§6.5).** Undersides are nodes in one ProseMirror document, and closed ones are hidden. Is there a simpler or more robust design?
5. **Data model and rules (§6.6–6.7).** Kept traces are made unchangeable in Firestore rules. Is that worth the complexity?
6. Is Phase 1 small enough to learn from, and can its acceptance criteria actually be tested?
7. Does anything in the vision contradict something else in it?
8. Is anything dishonest? Look for copy or naming that promises more privacy, meaning or care than the system delivers.
9. **Accessibility.** Can someone who doesn't use a mouse, can't tolerate motion, or uses a screen reader do everything?
10. What's missing that you'd expect in a writing tool, and should it stay missing?

For each finding, give its severity (blocker, major or minor), the section number, and a concrete fix.

## 1. Summary

> **The page has an underside, and the underside has a floor.**

Folio is a quiet writing page with one unusual property: any line can be lifted, and beneath it is room to write something else. The second line isn't a correction or a hidden truth. It's another thing that's also true, or funny, or not yet nameable.

A pair worth keeping can be kept. It becomes a small, unchangeable object with its date, and it settles onto the floor: a record of things you made. You can come back to it and add to it, but what you first wrote never changes. Later, if you allow it, kept things come back on their own.

The core loop, built in Phases 1–3:

> **Write → lift a line → write beneath it → step inside it (optional) → return → keep it if you want.**

**What Folio never does:**
- start a conversation or ask how you are
- interpret what you wrote or summarize you
- rate anything, count streaks, or measure how long you stay

**What it shares with Sessions:** your account, settings, language, and the full crisis-safety stack.

**The memory boundary:**
- Folio pages don't enter the companion's memory in Sessions.
- Sessions' memory doesn't enter Folio.
- The only way across is a deliberate action by the writer.

**Why a chat box can't do this.** A chat box turns everything into a reply. Folio's main material is your own words, in two layers, kept in the shape you left them and given back on a schedule a chat box doesn't have (§3.5).

## 3. Vision

### 3.1 The work

UnderSurface's name has always meant something under the surface. In Sessions, that's the conversation going deeper. In Folio, it's literal: a line of writing has an underside.

> It was nice to see everyone.

Lift it, and beneath:

> I felt absent the whole time.

The app doesn't say which is true. Both are on the page. Folio is a place where an unfinished experience can stay until your own language catches up with it.

### 3.2 Three tests

Every feature has to pass all three.

1. **If every generated reply disappeared, would this still be worth spending time in?** (from *Below the page*) Phases 1–3 contain no generated text at all.
2. **Does it become recognizably mine through what I do in it, without the system declaring who I am?** (from *Below the page*)
3. **Could a chat window do this?** If yes, it belongs in Sessions, or nowhere.

### 3.3 Laws

1. **The writer makes everything that lasts.** No automatic artifacts, summaries, collectibles or "insights". Only the writer creates a durable object.
2. **Neither layer is truer.** The app never ranks the surface against the underside. It never labels a meaning, a mood or a part, and never infers who the writer is from what they wrote.
3. **Plain words, strange world.** Metaphor lives in materials and motion. Labels, buttons and the companion's voice stay plain, so a first-time writer never has to decode anything.
4. **Time is a material.** Kept things keep their shape. New words sit beside old ones; they never replace them. Some things come back later.
5. **The exit survives every state.** Every view has a visible, immediate way back. Nothing ends with a completion ritual.
6. **It doesn't need you.** No streaks, no reminders, no withered garden after an absence, no measures of time spent. Coming back after months is ordinary.

**Safety law (overrides all six): grounding brings you to shore.** When distress is detected, the strange world switches off. The paper goes flat and warm, motion stops, stepping inside is unavailable, and nothing resurfaces. A plain door appears to the companion and to help. The writer's words always stay on the page.

### 3.4 One evening (illustrative; all text invented)

**Writing.** It's late. You open UnderSurface, and it opens on Folio because that's where you were last. The page is blank and nothing is asking you anything. You write a few lines about a dinner, then select "It was nice to see everyone." A small control appears beside the selection: **Lift**.

The line stays where it is. A crease opens under it and a strip of slightly different paper slides out, with the caret already there. You write "I felt absent the whole time." Then you close the fold. The line now has a quiet crease mark at its end. Nothing else changes.

**Stepping inside.** You open the fold again and choose **Step inside**. The page recedes, and the two lines stand in a soft field of light. You press **Farther** twice: the space opens and the light thins, and if sound is on, it goes distant. You press **Closer** and it gathers in again. You stay as long as you like, and **Back to the page** is always in the corner. When you go back, the page is exactly as you left it, caret and all.

**Keeping.** You choose **Keep**. The pair folds into a small slip and settles to the bottom edge. One word appears, *Kept.*, with nothing underneath it.

**Two weeks later.** You open **Kept**, where your slips are stacked newest first. You open the dinner one, choose **Add**, and write "I think I was waiting for someone to notice." It's added beneath with today's date, and the first two lines are unchanged. Nobody congratulates you.

**Delight.** Another night you write "We laughed until the soup went cold." Beneath it: "Nobody remembers what was funny." You keep it and name it *the soup incident*. A world made only of distress would teach people to look for distress; Folio has to hold this too.

**A hard night.** You write something heavier. A few seconds later the paper goes flat and still, with every word where you left it. A plain line appears at the top: you can talk with the companion now, or reach someone. For the next few days Folio won't step inside or bring anything back, and it doesn't make a show of any of this. If you choose **Talk now**, a Session opens in its grounded mode, and you're offered (not forced) the chance to bring the paragraph you just wrote.

**Return (Phase 6).** Months later you open Folio, and a slip sits at the top edge: *From before · 14 March*, "It was nice to see everyone." You had forgotten it. You open it, or choose **Not now** and it goes back.

### 3.5 Why a chat box can't be this

- **A chat box answers.** In Folio, by default nothing answers; your own second line is the response.
- **A chat box has one layer, the thread.** Folio gives each line two layers, and the relationship between them is yours.
- **A chat box forgets or summarizes.** Folio keeps the exact words, dated and unchangeable, and adds new layers beside them.
- **A chat box is always now.** Folio returns things later, and some answers arrive in the morning.
- **A chat box generates the artifact.** In Folio, the only artifacts are the ones you made.

An honest caveat, from *Below the page*: none of this is a technical moat, and another product could copy it. What can't be copied quickly is the quality of the interaction and the writer's own accumulated history.

### 3.6 What Folio is not

- **Not a notes app.** No folders, tags, formatting toolbar, tasks, or search in v1 (search is an open question, §10).
- **Not a prompt machine.** No prompts and no daily question.
- **Not an analysis tool.** No summaries, themes, mood charts or "patterns".
- **Not therapy, and not a replacement for Sessions.** Folio offers the door to Sessions; it doesn't try to be one.
- **Not private in the sense of unseen.** Pages are stored in the account like everything else, and short excerpts go to an AI service for safety checks (§5.7).

### 4.2 Living alongside Sessions

**What stays the same in Sessions:** everything. That includes Talk, Journal, Speak, Room, the opener, every companion program, the SMS channel and admin. The only visible change is a door to Folio in the sidebar, shown only to writers who have access.

**Home.** Opening the app (`/`) goes to wherever the writer was last, Folio or Sessions. This is remembered per device (`settings.lastHome`), the same way `lastSessionMode` is today. Writers who never open Folio see no change.

**Doors.**
- **Sessions → Folio:** a "Folio" item in the sidebar (`EntriesList`), above the session list.
- **Folio → Sessions:** a "Sessions" link in Folio's top bar. It goes to today's active session or a new one, exactly as opening the app does now.

**Memory boundary.** Folio pages, folds and traces never enter the companion's prompts in Sessions. That means no reflection, memories, profile, echoes or letters drawn from them. Sessions' memory never enters Folio either. Only the writer can carry something across:
- **Bring into a session** (Phase 5, plus a narrow Phase 1 version for shore, §5.3): from a selection, open a new Session with the text placed in the Talk composer, unsent. Once the writer sends it, it's a Session message like any other.
- Nothing flows the other way in v1.

**Shared:** account, consent, language, theme, timezone, the crisis stack and grounding state, the crisis resources card, data export and account deletion.

**Not shared:** the sidebar, the atmosphere effects (breathing background, cursor glow, bilateral pulse, inner weather), part thoughts, cards and analytics events.

**Access.** Folio sits behind a new experimental flag, `folioEnabled`, which is off by default. Admins can open Folio even while the flag is off, so the owner can use it in production before anyone else sees it. Turning the flag on opens Folio to everyone.

### 4.4 The page

```
 Pages                                         Kept   Sessions   •••
 ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

    We drove back late. The heater only worked on one side.

    It was nice to see everyone.⌟ Everyone asked about the job.
   ╭───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╮
   │ I felt absent the whole time.                                 │
   │                                  Close · Step inside · Keep   │
   ╰───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╯
    The soup was cold by the time anyone ate.

                                                              Saved
```

- **Blank state.** One placeholder line: "Write anything. Select a line to lift it." No prompt, no date header, no companion.
- **Writing.** Spectral serif, with the existing ink-weight and paragraph-settle extensions (`src/extensions/inkWeight.ts`, `paragraphSettle.ts`) and the same admin switches that turn them off in Sessions. Paragraphs only: no headings, lists, block quotes, code or links. Whether bold and italic stay is an open question (§10).
- **Saving.** Automatic. A quiet status in the top bar reads "Saved", "Saved on this device" (offline, or not yet confirmed by the server), or "Couldn't save. Your words are safe on this device." (§6.6).
- **Top bar.** Left: "Pages". Right: "Kept" (from Phase 3), "Sessions", and a "•••" menu with Plain view, New page, Delete page and Folio settings. The bar fades to 30% while you type and comes back on pointer movement or focus. It never disappears entirely.
- **Pages list.** Each page's first line and date, newest first. You can open or delete a page; deleting asks about any traces kept from it (§4.7). No folders, tags or sorting.
- **Plain view.** A read-only rendering with every underside shown, indented under its line and labelled "Beneath", with no motion. This is also the export and print format, and a full alternative for screen readers.

### 4.5 Lift and the underside

**Lifting.**
- Select text inside one paragraph of the page (not inside an underside). A **Lift** control appears near the selection on desktop, or in a bottom bar on touch screens (spike S2, §6.5).
- With no selection, the keyboard command lifts the sentence around the caret. Sentence boundaries come from `Intl.Segmenter`, falling back to the whole paragraph.
- A lift can cover 1 to 400 characters. A range that overlaps an existing fold can't be lifted; the app says "This line already has something beneath it" and offers an **Open** button.
- Lifting is a single undo step. It marks the line, inserts an open underside directly below its paragraph, and puts the caret inside it.

**The underside.**
- It's ordinary writing in paragraphs, in the same typeface, size and color as the page. The paper is different (§4.12); the words never are. Neither layer is styled as more important or more secret.
- Several lines in one paragraph can each be lifted. Their undersides stack below the paragraph in line order.
- In v1 there is only one level: undersides don't have undersides. Experiment E1 explores going deeper.

**Opening and closing.**
- A lifted line ends with a quiet crease mark (a small folded-corner glyph) and has a faint amber underline. Clicking or tapping either one toggles the underside.
- **Keyboard:** with the caret on a lifted line, `Mod+Alt+↓` opens it. Inside an underside, `Esc` or `Mod+Alt+↑` closes it and returns the caret to the line. These bindings are provisional (spike S5).
- Open or closed is view state, not content. Toggling doesn't save a new version of the page and isn't an undo step. The state is remembered per device and per page, together with scroll and caret position, so you come back to exactly where you were.
- Closing an empty underside removes the fold, so there are never empty folds.

**Editing the lifted line.**
- The mark follows normal editing, and typing at either end doesn't extend it.
- If the whole lifted line is deleted, its underside is never deleted with it. It becomes a **loose underside**, shown with the line it used to sit under ("Beneath a line you removed: '…'"). Two actions are offered:
  - **Keep it here:** it stays as an underside with no line.
  - **Turn into page text:** its paragraphs move into the page.
- Writer text is never silently deleted.

**Copy and paste.** Copying a lifted line copies its text. Pasting never creates folds, and pasted undersides become ordinary paragraphs. This prevents duplicate fold ids and surprise structure.

**Moving text.** If the paragraph containing a lifted line is split or merged, the underside moves with the line. It always sits directly after the paragraph that contains its mark.

### 4.6 Step inside (Phase 2)

```
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │  Back to the page                                     Sound  ○   │
 │                                                                  │
 │                    It was nice to see everyone.                  │
 │                                                                  │
 │                                                                  │
 │                    I felt absent the whole time.                 │
 │                                                                  │
 │                    Closer    ● ● ● ○ ○    Farther                │
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Entry.** From an open underside, choose **Step inside** (keyboard `Mod+Alt+Enter`). It's unavailable during shore.

**The space.** A full-screen layer over the page, with the lifted line above and the underside below, each in its own band of light. The words stay crisp and the same size at every distance. Distance changes the space around them, never how easy they are to read.

**One control: distance.** There are five stops: close, near, middle, far and distant. You can use the **Closer** and **Farther** buttons, a slider operated with the arrow keys, or a vertical drag on empty space on touch screens.

| Distance | Space | Light | Sound (if on) |
|---|---|---|---|
| close | narrow margins; the two bands nearly touch | warm, enveloping | full, present |
| near → far | margins widen; the bands separate | thinner, with a cooler edge | softer and duller (low-pass), more distant |
| distant | a wide field with a faint horizon line | a small oval of light | quiet and far |

Each movement changes one perceivable thing. Distance means nothing about the writer and is never recorded.

**Sound.** Off by default. Turning it on is a deliberate tap, which also satisfies browser autoplay rules. v1 reuses Room's `forest` and `drone` tracks; `ocean` is excluded under the water guardrail (§5.6). New tracks come later (§4.12). There's a volume control, and sound stops the instant you leave.

**No clock.** No timer, no arc, no ending ritual. After 90 seconds without interaction the controls dim to 30%, but **Back to the page** stays visible and focusable.

**Leaving.**
- **Ways out:** **Back to the page**, `Esc`, or the browser's back button.
- **On return:** the page has the same scroll, the fold is open, and the caret is at the end of the underside.
- **Forced exits** return to the page the same way: grounding, an audio interruption such as a phone call, or any navigation.

**Writing inside.** Not in v1; the space is for staying with the words. **Back and write** returns you with the caret in the underside. Reviewers: is that the right call? (§10)

### 4.7 Keep and the floor (Phase 3)

**Keeping.** From an open fold, choose **Keep**. The app makes an unchangeable copy of the lifted line and its underside as they are right now, with the date. In the animation, the pair folds into a slip and settles to the bottom edge (with reduced motion, a 120 ms fade). Then one word appears, "Kept.", with a "See kept" link. No praise, no caption.

**Keeping again.** If you keep a fold, edit it, and keep it again, the new trace is linked to the earlier one (`previousTraceId`), and the two sit together on the floor. Kept text is never edited in place.

**A trace** offers:
- The original lifted line and underside, with their date, read-only.
- **Add:** write something new beneath. It's added as a layer with today's date. A layer can't be edited once added, but it can be deleted.
- **Name it:** an optional title in your own words, such as "the soup incident". The title can be changed.
- **Go to the page:** opens the source page at the fold, or says that page was deleted.
- **Let this come back on its own** (Phase 6): on by default. Turning it off means Return never picks this trace.
- **Delete:** removes the trace and its layers.

```
 Kept
 ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   the soup incident                            2 Nov
 ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════   (2 layers added)
   It was nice to see everyone.                14 Oct
 ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   The dog waited by the door again.            9 Sep
 ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

**The floor (the Kept view).**
- Traces are stacked like layers of sediment: newest at the top, older further down. Scrolling down goes back in time.
- Each trace shows its title (or its lifted line) and its date.
- A trace grows thicker with each layer the writer adds. That's a record of returns, never of mood, length or intensity.
- Versions of the same fold sit together.
- No counts, totals or streaks appear anywhere.

**Deleting a page that has kept traces.** The confirmation says how many traces came from the page and offers two choices: delete just the page (the traces stay, since they're already copies) or delete the page and those traces.

### 4.8 The margin: the companion by invitation (Phase 5)

**Asking.** Select text in either layer and choose **Ask**. The companion writes a short note in graphite in the right margin beside the selection; on narrow screens it appears directly under the selection. It's labelled plainly: "Companion".

**What it sees.** Only the selected text and which layer it came from. Not the rest of the page, other pages, Session memory or the profile.

**How it speaks.** At most three sentences, in a plain voice; the Sessions rules against poster-quote language, stacked metaphors and "delve" apply.
- **It may:** reflect, offer a word, ask one question, or say there's nothing it would add.
- **It must not:**
  - say which layer is truer
  - say what something "really" means or reveals
  - diagnose, or name a mood or a part
  - give advice unless asked
  - turn a playful pair solemn

**What the writer can do with a note.**
- **Dismiss it:** it's gone and never stored.
- **Keep it:** it's pinned to the page as companion-written material, visibly different from the writer's own words.
- **Copy it.**

Notes never enter the writer's text on their own.

**Bring into a session.** Also from the selection menu. It opens a new Session with the selection in the Talk composer, unsent (§4.2).

### 4.9 Return and Overnight (Phase 6)

**Return.** At most once a day, when Folio opens, one kept trace may appear as a slip at the top edge, showing "From before · 14 March" and its lifted line (or the writer's title). The choices are **Open** and **Not now**. The slip never contains model-written text.

A trace can return only if all of these hold:
- the writer hasn't turned Return off
- the trace allows it
- it was kept at least 14 days ago
- it hasn't been shown in the last 60 days
- grounding hasn't activated in the last 72 hours on any surface (read from `companionPreferences/current.lastGroundingAt`)
- grounding wasn't active on its source page in the 72 hours before it was kept

The choice is deterministic, in this order:
1. An anniversary: a trace kept within ±3 days of today's date in an earlier year.
2. Otherwise, the trace never shown before that was kept earliest.
3. Otherwise, the trace shown least recently.

**Overnight.** On a selection, or on a newly typed question, choose **Answer in the morning**.
- **The slip.** The question becomes a sealed slip on the page.
- **Timing.** The answer becomes available at 07:00 writer-local time the next day, and at least 6 hours after holding. It's written the first time Folio opens after that.
- **Honest copy.** It says "The answer will be here in the morning", never "I'll think about it overnight".
- **Safety.** The question passes the crisis checks before it can be held. A question with any crisis signal is never held, and shore starts immediately. **A crisis never waits until morning.**
- **Grounded answers.** If grounding activated in the 72 hours before the answer is written, it's written in the grounded register.
- **SMS.** Sending the answer by text (through the field channel) is a possible later extension, not part of Phase 6.

### 4.10 The edition (Phase 7)

1. Choose traces from the floor.
2. They're laid out as a simple book, one trace per page: lifted line, underside, date, title, and any layers with their dates. Kept margin notes appear only if the writer includes them, labelled "Companion".
3. The browser's print dialog saves it as a PDF or prints it. A5 is the default size.

There's no generated text and no "your year in review". The MapForge large-format print pipeline is a possible later crossover.

### 4.11 Experiments

Each experiment needs its own one-page spec and a test before anything is built.

| ID | Experiment | Source | Question it tests | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | The long room: inside a fold is larger than outside; undersides can have undersides | Below the page | Can impossible space feel legible? | Disorientation; accessibility |
| E2 | The blank between: two traces side by side, with the gap as a new page that refers to both | Below the page | Does arrangement create meaning without interpretation? | Turns into a canvas tool |
| E3 | A prism you turn: the writer writes two or three readings of one line, and turning shows one at a time | Below the page | Can changing perspective be a physical experience? | Gimmick |
| E4 | A life not lived: an explicitly fictional annex, labelled "imagined", never returned later as biography | Below the page | Can imagination work without prediction? | Escapism; confusion with memory |
| E5 | Undertext: deleted sentences kept on the device as faint silt, visible on request | The Deep | Is it useful to see what you didn't say? | Privacy; distress |
| E6 | Breath tide: with a heart-rate strap, the page's edge rises and falls with your breath | The Deep | Can the body be an instrument rather than an authority? | Biometrics treated as truth |

### 4.12 Art direction

**Materials.**
- **Paper:** warm by day; brown-black with pearl text at night.
- **The underside:** a slightly cooler, translucent paper, as if seen through the sheet.
- **The crease:** a thin amber edge, with a faint thin-film interference color that shows only while a fold is opening.
- **Graphite:** the companion's material.
- **Settling:** the motion of keeping looks like ink diffusing in water. That's a motion, never a water theme.

**Tokens.** Folio defines its own tokens, scoped under `.folio-root`. It can't reuse `--bg-primary` and friends, because `useTimeAwarePalette` and `useSeasonalPalette` rewrite those tokens at runtime.

**Typography.** Spectral for writing (both layers, same size) and Inter for controls. Both are already loaded (`index.html:43`). No handwriting fonts and no morphing type.

**Motion.** A small set of physical behaviors:

| Behavior | Where | Duration | With reduced motion |
|---|---|---|---|

**Sound.** The palette is paper, air, wood, a distant room and a low string. v1 uses Room's `forest` and `drone`. **Voice (copy).** Short, specific and plain: "Kept.", not "Saved to your collection ✨"; "Beneath", not "Hidden truth". No exclamation marks.

**Play.** The material has to allow silliness. A light pair can make a lopsided fold, and a title in the writer's own register ("the soup incident") gets the same dignity as any other.

## 5. Safety, privacy and honesty

### 5.1 The new risk

In Sessions, a person in distress gets an answer: the companion responds, in its grounded mode. In Folio, nothing answers by default. A writing tool can hold someone's worst night without anything noticing. Folio MUST notice at least as reliably as the Journal does in Sessions, and MUST offer a clear door to a response.

At the same time, a writing tool collects more literary, historical and fictional dark material than a chat, so false positives are more likely. The design answer is to make shore **cheap to be wrong about**:
- it never takes the page away
- it never blocks writing
- it never pops up a modal

It only removes spectacle and adds a door.

### 5.2 Detection coverage

Folio uses the existing shared contract for both detection layers:

- **L1:** `detectCrisisKeywords` (`functions/src/shared/crisisFloor.ts`), synchronous and local.
- **L2:** `classifyCrisisRisk` (`src/ai/openrouter.ts:466`, `task: 'crisis_check'` on gpt-5-nano). It has a 5 s timeout, fails open to risk 0, and doesn't count against the user's quota.

| Trigger | L1 | L2 | Text checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| A paragraph settles (2 s without typing in it, or the caret leaves it) | yes | yes, if it's at least 12 characters and wasn't already checked | The paragraph. Inside an underside, it's prefixed with its lifted line, because meaning lives in the pair. |
| Paste | yes, on all pasted text | yes | The last 1,000 characters of the pasted block (the classifier's own slice) |
| Ask (margin) | yes, before sending | yes, raced against the reply | The selection |
| Answer in the morning | yes | yes, awaited before holding | The question |
| Keep, or adding a layer | yes | only if not already checked | The new text |

**Scheduling:**
- at most one L2 call in flight per tab
- at most one call every 15 seconds; later requests collapse into the newest text
- an identical sample is skipped for 10 minutes (by hash)

That's at most 4 calls a minute, against the chat function's per-user limit of 30 a minute (`functions/src/chat.ts:182`), which leaves room for the margin.

**Offline:** L2 fails open, but L1 still runs. Checks that failed while offline are retried once when the connection returns, newest first, within the same limits.

**Not checked:** pages that are opened but not edited (they were checked when written), and text shown in Plain view or exported.

**Precedent:** the Journal's parts pipeline already runs L1 and L2 on pause (`src/engine/partOrchestrator.ts:553–581`). Folio is stricter than the Journal with parts turned off, which checks text only when the draft is added to the conversation.

### 5.3 Shore

Any activation, from either layer on any surface, uses the existing global grounding state in `src/store/groundingState.ts`:
- `activateGrounding` starts it.
- It exits automatically after `grounding.autoExitMinutes` (default 5), and the timer restarts on each new trigger.
- It already sets `data-grounding="true"` on `<html>` and saves `lastGroundingAt`.

```
 If things feel like too much right now, you can talk with the
 companion or reach someone.                  Talk now · Find help
 ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    (the page: flat, still, every word in place)
```

| Element | Normal | Shore |
|---|---|---|
| The page and every word | as written | unchanged, never hidden or moved |
| Writing, lifting, opening folds | available | available, without animation |
| Materials and motion | translucent underside, crease color, settle animations | flat warm paper, level, no motion |
| Step inside | available | closes at once, sound stops, button unavailable |
| Return | may show a slip | suppressed for 72 hours after the last activation |
| Ask (margin) | as in §4.8 | a grounded reply (safety rules first), with the shore door beneath it |
| Answer in the morning | available | unavailable |
| Crisis resources card | hidden | shown (the existing card, also rendered in Folio) |
| Shore line | — | fixed, translated copy at the top: "If things feel like too much right now, you can talk with the companion or reach someone." **Talk now** · **Find help** |

**Talk now** navigates within the app, without a reload, to a **new** Session. It's a new one because an existing session with messages wouldn't produce a fresh opener. Grounding is held in memory, so the new Session's opener is built grounded; a full page reload would lose that state. The Session opens with an offer, **Bring what you just wrote**. Tapping it places the paragraph that triggered shore into the Talk composer, unsent. The writer is never made to retell it, and nothing crosses the memory boundary without their tap.

Phase 1 MUST include a test that this navigation keeps grounding active and that the opener prompt is built with `isGrounding: true`.

**Find help** shows the crisis resources card again, even if it was dismissed.

**After shore.** When grounding exits automatically, features come back, but the materials stay flat and motion stays off for the rest of the visit. The strange world returns on the next visit. Snapping back into spectacle five minutes after distress would be wrong.

### 5.4 Resurfacing and holding

Covered in §4.9:
- Return never resurfaces anything within 72 hours of any grounding activation.
- It never resurfaces a trace that was kept within 72 hours of grounding on its page.
- Overnight never holds a question with a crisis signal.

### 5.5 Margin safety

- **Grounding is read at call time.** The note reads `isGroundingActive()` when the prompt is built and again before it's shown, never a value captured at render. This is the lesson from `src/engine/turnResponder.ts`.
- **L2 races the reply.**
  - If `classifyCrisisRisk` returns 2 before the reply finishes, the reply is aborted, grounding activates and shore appears.
  - If it returns 2 afterwards, grounding activates and shore appears. The note stays, because it was generated under `SAFETY_RULES`.
- **The prompt** includes `SAFETY_RULES` verbatim and the untrusted-content preamble. The selection is wrapped as untrusted user content (`src/ai/promptSafety.ts`).
- **A live eval gates release** (§6.10).

### 5.6 Imagery and language guardrails

- **The person never sinks.** Words settle; people don't. Folio's copy and visuals never use drowning, falling, "let go", "disappear" or "rest" anywhere near distress, and Folio has no ocean track.
- **Paper and air, not water.** Ink diffusing in water describes how keeping moves. It's never a scene.
- **Distance opens space; it never closes in.** Nothing in Folio is dark, heavy or claustrophobic by design.

### 5.7 Privacy: data flows

| Data | Stored | Sent to | When | Kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Page text (both layers) | Firestore `users/{uid}/folioPages` | Google Firebase | autosave | until the writer deletes it or the account |
| Edits the server hasn't confirmed | a localStorage copy on this device | nowhere | until the server confirms | cleared on confirmation, sign-out and deletion |
| View state (open folds, scroll, caret, distance) | localStorage | nowhere | continuously | cleared on sign-out and deletion |
| Safety excerpts (up to 1,000 characters) | not stored by us | OpenAI via `/api/chat` (`crisis_check`) | §5.2 | under OpenAI's API terms (the policy's existing wording) |
| Margin selection | not stored unless the note is kept | OpenAI via `/api/chat` | on Ask | as above |
| Kept notes, traces, layers, held questions and answers | Firestore | Google Firebase | when the writer acts | until deleted |
| Grounding timestamp | `companionPreferences/current.lastGroundingAt` (existing) | Firebase | on activation | existing behavior |
| Errors | Sentry, without content | Sentry | on error | Sentry's policy |
| Analytics | none | — | — | — |

**Rules:**
- **No content in telemetry.** Folio code MUST NOT put writer text into:
  - error messages
  - `console` output (Sentry records console calls as breadcrumbs by default)
  - first-party diagnostics
  - analytics
- **No Google Analytics events.** Folio sends none at all. GA shares a property with Ads; see the comment in `groundingState.ts:63–66`.
- **Admin.** There's no admin view of Folio content; the admin overview may show per-user counts of pages and traces. The policy's statement that administrators can view user data stays true at the database level and stays in the policy.
- **MCP.** The read-only MCP tools (`list-entries`, `get-entry`, `list-conversations`, `get-conversation`) don't expose Folio. Adding them would be a separate decision.
- **Export and deletion.** Export includes everything (§6.12). Account deletion already deletes recursively (`recursiveDelete` in `functions/src/account.ts`), so new collections are covered. The registry is still updated, for the tests.
- **Policy.** Before Folio opens beyond admins, `policy.whatWeStoreBody` and `policy.howAiWorksBody` gain sentences about Folio in all 17 languages (§6.13). The current wording ("When you write or speak in a session…", `en.ts:210`) doesn't cover Folio.
- **First visit.** One line appears under the blank page on the first visit (`folio.firstVisit`). It can be dismissed and links to the policy.

### 5.8 Honesty rules

- **No word implies secrecy or forgetting:** not "private", "secret", "hidden", "forget", "vanish" or "only you". Folio is not a vault.
- **No copy implies the companion is thinking when it isn't.** Overnight answers are written when you open the app in the morning, so the copy says when the answer will be there, not that anyone thought about it overnight.
- **Model text always looks different** (graphite, labelled). It never mixes into the writer's text except through a deliberate copy and paste.
- **A kept trace shows exact words with exact dates.** Nothing in Folio paraphrases the writer.

### 6.5 Editor architecture

**Schema** (TipTap 3.23; `@tiptap/react`, `@tiptap/pm`):

```ts
// src/folio/page/schema/fold.ts
export const Fold = Mark.create({
  name: 'fold',
  inclusive: false,          // typing at the edges doesn't extend it
  excludes: 'fold',          // folds can't overlap
  addAttributes() { return { foldId: { default: null } } },
  parseHTML() { return [{ tag: 'span[data-fold-id]' }] },
  renderHTML({ mark }) {
    return ['span', { 'data-fold-id': mark.attrs.foldId, class: 'folio-fold' }, 0]
  },
})

// src/folio/page/schema/underside.ts
export const Underside = Node.create({
  name: 'underside',
  group: 'block',
  content: 'paragraph+',
  defining: true,
  isolating: true,           // Backspace/Delete don't merge across its edges
  addAttributes() {
    return {
      foldId: { default: null },
      surfaceSnapshot: { default: '' },  // the lifted line at lift time, shown if it goes loose
      loose: { default: false },
      createdAt: { default: 0 },
    }
  },
  // React NodeView: crease edge, accessible group label, Close / Step inside / Keep / Ask
})
```

StarterKit 3.x includes more than the Journal uses. Folio disables `heading`, `codeBlock`, `blockquote`, `bulletList`, `orderedList`, `listItem` and `horizontalRule` (as `SessionJournalSurface.tsx:63–71` does), plus `link` (StarterKit 3 includes Link; Folio doesn't turn URLs into links). In effect the document is `(paragraph | underside)+`.

**Invariants.** The `foldIntegrity` plugin enforces these in `appendTransaction`, and each is unit-tested against every kind of edit:

| # | Invariant |
|---|---|
| I1 | Every `fold` mark's `foldId` has exactly one `underside` with that id. |
| I2 | Each underside sits directly after the paragraph containing its mark, ordered among that paragraph's undersides by mark position. |
| I3 | An underside with no matching mark becomes `loose: true`. It's never deleted. |
| I4 | No `fold` marks exist inside undersides in v1; any that appear are stripped. |
| I5 | `foldId`s are unique. If a duplicate ever appears, the later mark is stripped (its text stays). |
| I6 | A closed underside whose paragraphs are all empty is removed, together with its mark. |

**Open and closed.** This state lives in the `foldView` plugin's own state, not in the document: a `Set<foldId>` of open folds, rendered as node decorations (`folio-underside--closed`, which applies `display: none` and `aria-hidden`).
- A selection guard moves any selection that lands inside a closed underside to the end of its lifted line.
- Toggling dispatches a meta-only transaction: no document change, no save, no undo step.

**Paste.** A `transformPasted` handler strips `fold` marks and unwraps `underside` nodes into paragraphs.

**Spike S1 (Phase 0) chooses between two architectures:**
- **A (recommended): undersides as nodes in the page document.**
  - *Benefits:* one editor, one undo history, native copy, paste and selection, correct reading order for screen readers, and one save path.
  - *Risks:* hidden content in ProseMirror (selection, find-in-page, IME), and keeping the invariants correct.
- **B: a small separate editor per underside,** mounted under its paragraph through a widget decoration and a React portal, with content stored in a `folds` map.
  - *Benefits:* a simpler closed state.
  - *Costs:* two undo stacks, focus hand-offs, fragmented accessibility, and two save paths.

S1 builds A, with its invariants and tests, and runs these cases:
1. arrow navigation across closed and open undersides
2. IME composition (Japanese, Korean)
3. Backspace at both edges of an underside
4. splitting and merging the lifted paragraph
5. Android Chrome autocorrect and composition
6. iOS Safari selection

If A fails two of these, the plan switches to B.

**Spike S2: lifting on touch.** iOS and Android show their own selection menus, which can collide with TipTap's `BubbleMenu` (`@tiptap/react/menus`). Note that `@tiptap/extension-bubble-menu` is currently only an optional dependency of `@tiptap/react`; it becomes a direct dependency. The plan is a BubbleMenu on pointer devices and, on touch, a bottom bar that appears while a selection exists. This is validated on iOS Safari 17 and 18, and on Android Chrome.

**Ink weight and paragraph settle.** Folio reuses both extensions with their admin switches, as `SessionJournalSurface.tsx:135–146` does. Paragraph settle only iterates top-level paragraphs (`doc.forEach`), so paragraphs inside undersides never "settle". Phase 1 extends it to descend into undersides.

**Paths** (all under `users/{uid}/`):
- `folioPages/{pageId}`, with a `margin/{noteId}` subcollection
- `folioTraces/{traceId}`, with a `layers/{layerId}` subcollection
- `folioHeld/{id}`
- `folioPreferences/current`

**Queries.**
- Pages: `orderBy('updatedAt', 'desc').limit(50)`.
- Traces: `orderBy('keptAt', 'desc')`.
- Both are single-field and indexed automatically, so `firestore.indexes.json` doesn't change in Phases 1–3.
- Return reads all traces (expected to be in the hundreds) and chooses on the client.

**Save controller** (`usePageAutosave.ts`, modelled on `JournalDraftController` in `src/hooks/useJournalDraft.ts`):
- **Timing.** Debounce of 1.2 s, maximum wait of 10 s. It also flushes on `pagehide`, on `visibilitychange` to hidden, on unmount and on route change.
- **The write.** Each save writes `{doc, updatedAt, rev: rev + 1, editorId, preview, foldCount}` with `setDoc(..., { merge: true })`, fire-and-forget. With the persistent cache, the promise only settles when the server acknowledges, so the controller doesn't await it before the next save.
- **Status.** The save status comes from the page's own `onSnapshot` with `includeMetadataChanges`. Pending writes show "Saved on this device"; settled writes show "Saved".
- **Local copy.** Until the server confirms a revision, the latest document is also kept in localStorage (`us.folioDraft.<uid>.<pageId>`), the way Journal drafts are (`src/store/journalDrafts.ts`). This covers browsers where IndexedDB is unavailable and Firestore falls back to an in-memory cache (the case fixed in `2985b02`). On load, a local copy newer than the server's wins and is saved.
- **Size.** The app warns at 400 KB of serialized document ("This page is getting long. A new page might be easier."). At 900 KB it refuses Lift and paste with a message, and the local copy keeps everything. Firestore's limit is 1 MiB per document.

**The same page in two tabs.** `pageLock.ts` takes a per-page lock over `BroadcastChannel('us-folio')`. The second tab opens read-only with "This page is open in another tab · Edit here", and choosing Edit here takes the lock.

**The same page on two devices.** When a remote snapshot arrives with a higher `rev` from a different `editorId`:
- If this tab has no unsaved changes, the editor reloads with the remote document and keeps its view state.
- If it does, this tab's version is saved as a new page, titled from its first line with "(this device)", and the remote version opens, with a notice.

The app never merges silently and never drops words.

### 6.7 Security rules

Today `firestore.rules` (lines 24–28) gives the owner write access to everything under `users/{uid}/{collectionId}/**` except `smsSettings` and `smsMessages`. Firestore combines every matching `allow` rule, so a more specific rule can't take that access away. To make traces unchangeable, they have to be carved out of the generic rule itself:

```
match /users/{userId}/{collectionId}/{document=**} {
  allow read: if isOwner(userId);
  allow write: if isOwner(userId)
    && !(collectionId in ['smsSettings', 'smsMessages', 'folioTraces']);
}

match /users/{userId}/folioTraces/{traceId} {
  allow read: if isOwner(userId);
  allow create: if isOwner(userId)
    && request.resource.data.id == traceId
    && request.resource.data.author == 'writer'
    && request.resource.data.surface is string
    && request.resource.data.surface.size() <= 2000
    && request.resource.data.underside is string
    && request.resource.data.underside.size() <= 200000;
  allow update: if isOwner(userId)
    && request.resource.data.diff(resource.data).affectedKeys()
         .hasOnly(['title', 'returnAllowed', 'lastReturnedAt']);
  allow delete: if isOwner(userId);

  match /layers/{layerId} {
    allow read: if isOwner(userId);
    allow create: if isOwner(userId)
      && request.resource.data.id == layerId
      && request.resource.data.author == 'writer'
      && request.resource.data.text is string
      && request.resource.data.text.size() > 0
      && request.resource.data.text.size() <= 20000;
    allow delete: if isOwner(userId);
  }
}
```

Firestore doesn't cascade deletes, so the client deletes a trace's layers before the trace, in one batch. Account deletion's `recursiveDelete` already covers everything.

**Emulator tests** (`tests/firestore-rules.test.ts`):
- The owner can create, read and delete traces and layers.
- The owner can't change `surface`, `underside`, `keptAt` or `source`, or a layer's `text`.
- Another user can do nothing.
- The generic rule doesn't re-open writes through the nested `layers` path.

### 6.8 Safety integration

`FolioSafetyWatch` is a pure class with a small hook, fed by the editor:

```ts
class FolioSafetyWatch {
  constructor(deps: {
    l1: (text: string) => boolean                                   // detectCrisisKeywords
    l2: (text: string, lang: string) => Promise<{ risk: 0 | 1 | 2 }> // classifyCrisisRisk
    activate: () => void                                            // activateGrounding('auto')
    now: () => number
    language: () => string
  })
  settle(unit: { key: string; text: string; context?: string }): void  // L1 now; L2 queued
  paste(text: string): void
  gate(text: string): Promise<'clear' | 'crisis'>                       // Ask and hold: L1 + awaited L2 (5 s cap, fails open)
}
```

- **L1** runs synchronously when a unit settles. A hit calls `activateGrounding('auto')` immediately.
- **The L2 queue:**
  - one call in flight at a time, at least 15 s apart
  - the newest text wins
  - identical samples are skipped for 10 minutes (by hash)
  - text under 12 characters is skipped
  - `risk === 2` activates grounding
  - failures while offline are retried once on the `online` event
- **Units.** The editor computes them: a top-level paragraph, or an underside paragraph with its lifted line as `context` (`"<line>\n\n<paragraph>"`, then cut to the classifier's `slice(-1000)`).
- **`useShore()`** subscribes through `useGroundingMode()`. When grounding activates, it also records `groundedAt` on the open page (for Return) and sets a visit-scoped `quiet` flag that stays on after the automatic exit.
- **No changes** to `groundingState.ts`, `crisisFloor.ts` or the classifier.

**Tests** use fake timers and a fake classifier:
- settle → L1 → activation
- L2 spacing and coalescing
- de-duplication
- offline retry
- pasting a long block
- gate timeouts failing open
- never more than 4 L2 calls a minute in a typing simulation

### 6.9 Step inside and audio

**The dialog.**
- `StepInside` is portaled to `document.body` and made `inert`-aware: it sets `#root` inert while open and restores any pre-existing `inert` on close.
- It uses `useModalA11y`, `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, and an accessible name ("Inside: \<line\>").
- It closes when grounding activates (subscribed through `useGroundingMode`).
- This is the same pattern as `RoomField.tsx` (roughly lines 90–143).

**Distance mapping.** `distance.ts` maps stops 0–4 to CSS custom properties (`--folio-gap`, `--folio-margin`, `--folio-light-radius`, `--folio-light-opacity`) and to audio parameters (low-pass from 18 kHz down to 700 Hz; gain from 0.5 down to 0.12). It's pure and unit-tested.

**`useDistanceAudio`.**
- On the Sound tap, it creates or resumes an `AudioContext`.
- It creates `new Audio(url)` with `crossOrigin = 'anonymous'`, `loop` and `playsinline`.
- The graph is `MediaElementAudioSourceNode → BiquadFilterNode (lowpass) → GainNode → destination`.
- Distance changes are smoothed with `setTargetAtTime` (0.4 s).
- It tears down on leaving, on grounding, and on `visibilitychange` to hidden.

**CORS (checked 2026-10-15).** `seed-audio/v1/ocean.mp3` returns `access-control-allow-origin: https://undersurface.me` for that origin, so the Web Audio path works on the web. The Capacitor origins (`capacitor://localhost`, `https://localhost`) are unverified, and without CORS a media-element source outputs silence. The fallback: if CORS fails, or on native, play through the plain `HTMLAudioElement` and vary only its `volume` with distance.

**Spike S3** covers iOS Safari's ringer switch with Web Audio, audio-session interruptions, and resuming after a call.

### 6.14 Observability

- **Diagnostics.** First-party diagnostics (`engineLog`) record errors and safety-pipeline failures by code only, such as `folio.save.failed` or `folio.l2.failopen`. Never text.

- **Analytics and metrics.** No Google Analytics events and no engagement metrics (time on page, words, visits). Session Replay isn't installed; `replaysOnErrorSampleRate` in `main.tsx` has no effect without the integration, and Folio must keep it that way.

### 6.15 PWA, bundle, native

## 8. Evaluation

**What we're learning.** Whether lifting a line and writing beneath it creates "a useful or moving way to encounter the words" (*Below the page*). Not whether people stay longer.

**Owner dogfooding** (two weeks, after Phase 3). Arash uses Folio for real writing and keeps a short private log of moments when it felt like making something versus being managed.

**Moderated study** (5–8 adults, after Phase 3).
- **Material:** invented or low-stakes. Nobody is asked to disclose anything difficult.
- **Tasks:**
  1. Write a few lines about an ordinary event.
  2. Lift one and write beneath it.
  3. Step inside, then return.
  4. Keep it if you want, then find it again.
- **Comparison:** the same words on a plain page (Sessions' Journal) versus in Folio.
- **Questions:**
  - What did you think the fold meant?
  - Did the distance feel like your choice?
  - Did anything make you feel interpreted or managed?
  - Was there a moment you wanted to leave and couldn't find how?
- **Listening for:** "I could leave it unresolved", "I wanted to put these together", "it felt like something I made". These are what we hope to hear, not results.

**Kill or redesign if:**
- the fold consistently reads as the app trying to expose a hidden truth
- atmosphere overwhelms the writing
- people can't find the way out
- keeping becomes more attractive than making (people keep everything without writing beneath)
- shore false positives during ordinary dark writing happen often enough to feel like being watched

**Not measured:** time in the app, visits, streaks, words written, number of folds. Telemetry is limited to error codes.

## Appendix C: Background for reviewers new to the codebase

- **Sessions.** Opening the app drops the writer into a session with an AI companion across four surfaces:
  - **Talk:** streamed text.
  - **Journal:** a quiet editor, where optional "inner voices" (the companion's seven seeded parts) can respond on pause.
  - **Speak:** live voice.
  - **Room:** ambient sound and voice, or quiet company.

  An optional SMS channel carries the same companion between sessions. Many companion programs (memory, parts mapping, intentions, values and more) run behind feature flags.
- **The crisis stack.**
  - **L1** is a narrow, deterministic regex floor for sincere, present, first-person methods and plans.
  - **L2** is a precision-tuned gpt-5-nano classifier that runs on every user-authored path.
  - Either layer activates **grounding**: a global, in-memory state that changes every prompt to a grounded register, suppresses companion programs, shows a crisis-resources card, and saves a timestamp the SMS channel reads for 72 hours.
  - The classifier is deliberately tuned against over-calling. A previous version flagged grief, metaphor and gallows humor.
- **Fresh eyes.** A per-session switch that keeps durable memory out of the companion's context. It's honest that the transcript is still saved. Folio's margin is "fresh" by construction.
- **Parts ontology** (decided 2026-07-04). The seven seeded parts (Watcher, Tender, Still, Spark, Weaver, Open, Quiet One) belong to the companion. The writer's own parts are a separate, consent-based map. Folio uses neither.
- **Feature flags.** Live in `appConfig/global.features`. Core flags are on unless explicitly `false`; experimental flags are on only when explicitly `true`. The server rejects unknown keys, so functions deploy before hosting.
- **Deploy order:** indexes, then rules, then functions, then hosting.
