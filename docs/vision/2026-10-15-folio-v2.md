## 0. For reviewers

### 0.1 What this is

A proposal for a second, separate way of using UnderSurface.

Today the app is a companion: you open it and a conversation starts. **Folio** is a writing tool: you open it and there is a quiet page. The current system stays exactly as it is and stays one tap away. This document calls it **Sessions** (the owner calls it "legacy").

If you don't know the codebase, read Appendix C first.

### 0.2 Decisions the owner has already made

1. Writing tool first, not companion first.
2. Folio is a complete, separate mode that lives alongside Sessions. Both stay usable.
3. The crisis-safety stack covers Folio just as it covers Sessions.

These are fixed. Tell us if one of them causes a problem.

### 0.3 What changed from draft 1

The Codex review judged draft 1's implementation to be more fully imagined than its art, and warned that the early product could become an elegant annotation editor. This draft makes four structural changes:

- **It has one authored operation.** That operation is **Apart**: set how far a lifted line and its beneath stand from each other, and write in the space between them.
- **It tests the art before building the editor.** A disposable-prototype study comes first.
- **It brings time into the core.** The writer chooses when to come back to something.
- **It turns the risky mechanics into contracts.** Hidden writing, storage, kept things, safety and states each get a normative contract.

## 1. The proposition

### 1.1 In one sentence

> **Folio lets you compose relationships between passages, keep those relationships, and meet them again over time.**

This sentence is also the test that every feature has to pass (§2.1).

### 1.2 The act

> Lift a line. Write what's beneath it. Then decide how far apart the two should stand, and write what lives between them.

The writer authors three things that an ordinary page doesn't invite:

- **The beneath.** A second passage attached to a line. It has equal standing; it isn't a correction or a hidden truth.
- **The distance.** How far apart the two stand, chosen from five stops. It's saved as part of the writing.
- **The between.** A third passage that exists only in the space the distance opened.

Then there are two choices about time:

- **Keep.** A dated snapshot of the whole composition: line, distance, between and beneath.
- **Come back to this.** The writer picks a day to meet it again. On that day it's waiting at the top of Folio, and they can write beside it.

### 1.3 "What can I make here that I wouldn't have thought to make on an ordinary page?"

A small composition of two or three passages whose arrangement is part of what it says:
- a line
- what's beneath it
- how far apart they stand
- what lies between them

It's kept with its date, met again on a day you chose, and new words go beside it.

**A caveat.** You could type something similar on any page using blank lines. The claim isn't that this is impossible elsewhere. The claim is that this page makes it the natural thing to do, and that people will make relationships here they wouldn't otherwise make. That's a hypothesis, and §4 tests it before anything is built for production.

### 1.4 Five encounters (illustrative; all text invented)

**Ordinary.** It's late. You write a few lines about a dinner, select "It was nice to see everyone." and choose **Lift**. A crease opens under the line, and the caret is waiting on a strip of slightly different paper. You write "I felt absent the whole time." Then you close it. The line now has a small crease mark at its end. Nothing else changes.

**Made.** A few days later you open the fold and press **Farther** twice. The beneath moves down the page, leaving a band of open paper. In the middle of that space the word *between* waits, faintly. You write "Someone passed me the bread and I said thank you like a person." The three passages now stand at the distance you chose. You keep it. In the kept slip they stand exactly as you set them, and if you print it, the gap is real white space.

**Playful.** You write "We laughed until the soup went cold." Beneath it: "Nobody remembers what was funny." You push them as far apart as they go, a whole screen of empty paper, and in the middle you write only "(soup)". You keep it and name it *the soup incident*.

Another night you write "The meeting went fine." Beneath it you write four paragraphs. The fold's inside is much bigger than its outside, and the page lets it be. The app never noticed that anything was funny; you made it funny.

**Return.** When you keep the dinner fold, you choose **Come back to this · In a month**. A month later you open Folio and it's at the top: *You asked to come back to this · 14 October*. You open it. The composition is exactly as you left it. Beside it, dated today, you write "I think I was waiting for someone to notice." The original doesn't change, and nobody congratulates you.

**A hard night.** You write something heavier. A few seconds later the paper goes flat and still, and every word stays where you left it. A plain line appears at the top: you can talk with the companion now, or reach someone.

For the next three days, nothing you kept comes up by itself. Anything you asked to come back to waits in Kept until then. If you choose **Talk now**, a Session opens in its grounded mode, and you're offered (not forced) the chance to bring what you just wrote. (§5.5 is the authoritative description.)

### 1.5 What the first version leaves out

These are each a separate hypothesis in §9 and have to earn their place:
- Step inside
- sound
- the margin companion
- automatic resurfacing
- delayed AI answers
- the printed edition
- the further spatial experiments

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 The originality criterion

Draft 1 had three tests. This draft keeps two of them and replaces the third with a positive criterion. Every feature answers three questions:

1. **Does it help the writer compose, preserve or re-encounter a relationship between passages?** If not, it's either support (and stays small and plain) or it's out.
2. **If every generated reply disappeared, would this still be worth doing?** (from *Below the page*)
3. **Does it become recognizably the writer's through what they do in it, without the system declaring who they are?** (from *Below the page*)

**Removed:** draft 1's "Could a chat window do this?" and its section on what chat boxes can't do. Those claims were broad and partly untrue: chat products already have memory, scheduled tasks and generated documents. The argument doesn't need them.

**Omissions are scope, not originality.** Leaving out search, folders and formatting keeps the first version small. It doesn't make Folio original, and it can make saved writing harder to use. Support features are judged by one question: does this help the writer get back to their own material without becoming the center of the experience? Search is a later candidate on those terms (§9, H7).

### 2.2 Laws

1. **Only the writer's decisions make anything last.**
   - The writer writes the passages.
   - Nothing is automatically kept, summarized, collected or "noticed".
   - If a later phase lets a companion note be kept, it lasts because the writer chose to keep it, and it stays labelled as not the writer's.
2. **Neither passage is truer.** The app never ranks the line against its beneath or between. It never labels a meaning, a mood or a part, and never infers who the writer is.
3. **Plain words, strange material.**
   - Metaphor lives in materials, space and motion.
   - Labels, buttons and any companion voice stay plain.
   - The form may be lopsided, vast, tiny or absurd, but the app never detects tone in order to apply an effect.
4. **Time is a material.** Kept things keep their shape. New words go beside old ones and never replace them. Things come back when the writer asked them to.
5. **The exit survives every state.** Every view has a visible, immediate way back. Nothing ends with a completion ritual.
6. **It doesn't need you.**
   - no streaks or reminders
   - no notifications
   - no withered garden after an absence
   - no measures of time spent

   A "come back" waits silently. It never sends a message.
7. **What you made survives what you do by accident.** This law is new in this draft.
   - Hidden writing is never changed while hidden.
   - One device's save never silently overwrites another's.
   - A keep can be undone.
   - A kept thing can be corrected without erasing what it was.

**The safety law overrides all seven: grounding brings you to shore.** When distress is detected:
- the strange material switches off: the paper goes flat and warm, and motion stops
- nothing resurfaces
- a plain door appears to the companion and to help

The writer's words always stay on the page.

### 2.3 What Folio is not

- **Not a notes app.** The first version has no folders, tags, formatting toolbar or tasks. (These are left out for scope, not as proof of originality; see §2.1.)
- **Not a prompt machine.** No prompts and no daily question.
- **Not an analysis tool.** No summaries, themes, mood charts or "patterns".
- **Not therapy, and not a replacement for Sessions.** Folio offers the door to Sessions.
- **Not private in the sense of unseen.** Pages are stored in the account, and short excerpts go to an AI service for safety checks (§5.6).

### 3.2 Living alongside Sessions

This is unchanged from draft 1.

**What stays the same in Sessions:** everything. That includes Talk, Journal, Speak, Room, the opener, every companion program, the SMS channel and admin. The only visible change is a "Folio" door in the sidebar, shown only to writers who have access.

**Home.** Opening the app (`/`) goes to wherever the writer was last, Folio or Sessions. This is remembered per device (`settings.lastHome`). Writers who never open Folio see no change.

**Doors.**
- **Sessions → Folio:** a "Folio" item in the sidebar (`EntriesList`), above the session list.
- **Folio → Sessions:** a "Sessions" link in Folio's top bar. It goes to today's active session, or to a new one, exactly as opening the app does now.

**Memory boundary.**
- Folio pages, folds and traces never enter the companion's prompts in Sessions: no reflection, memories, profile, echoes or letters drawn from them.
- Sessions' memory never enters Folio.
- In the first version, the only crossing is shore's **Bring what you just wrote** (§5.5). It places text in the Talk composer, unsent, and only when the writer taps it.

**Shared:**
- account, consent, language, theme and timezone
- the crisis stack and grounding state, and the crisis resources card
- data export and account deletion

**Not shared:**
- the sidebar
- the atmosphere effects (breathing background, cursor glow, bilateral pulse, inner weather)
- part thoughts and cards
- analytics events

**Access.** Folio sits behind a new experimental flag, `folioEnabled`, which is off by default. Admins can open Folio while the flag is off. Phase 3 needs per-person invitations (§7, §11).

```
    It was nice to see everyone.⌟ Everyone asked about the job.

```

- **Blank state.** One placeholder line: "Write anything. Select a line to lift it." There's no prompt, date header or companion.
- **Writing.**
  - The typeface is Spectral, with the existing ink-weight and paragraph-settle extensions and their admin switches.
  - Text is paragraphs and soft line breaks (Shift+Enter), so poems keep their lines.
  - There are no headings, lists, block quotes, code or links. Bold and italic are an open question (§11).
- **Saving.** Automatic, with four honest statuses (§5.2):
  - "Saved"
  - "Saved on this device"
  - "Not saved yet. Keep this tab open." (with **Copy page**)
  - "Couldn't save to your account. Your words are on this device."
- **Top bar.**
  - Left: "Pages".
  - Right: "Kept" (from Phase 2), "Sessions", and a "•••" menu with Reading view, New page, Delete page, Find help and Folio settings.
  - **It doesn't fade.** Quiet comes from having few controls, small type and low chroma, not from lowered contrast (§3.10).
- **Pages list.** Each page's first line and date, newest first. You can open or delete a page. Deleting asks about any traces kept from it (§3.7).
- **Reading view.** A linear rendering with every fold expanded, the distances shown as real space, and no motion. It's for reading, printing and export. It is **not** the accessibility alternative: the editor itself is (§3.10).

### 3.5 Lift and the beneath

**Lifting.**
- Select text inside one paragraph of the page, not inside an underside. A **Lift** control appears near the selection on pointer devices, or in a bottom bar on touch screens (spike S2).
- With no selection, the keyboard command lifts the sentence around the caret. Sentence boundaries come from `Intl.Segmenter`, falling back to the whole paragraph.
- A lift covers 1 to 400 characters. A range that overlaps an existing fold can't be lifted; the app says "This line already has something beneath it" and offers **Open**.
- Lifting is one undo step. It marks the line, inserts an open underside directly after its paragraph at distance "touching", and puts the caret in the beneath.

**The beneath.**
- It's ordinary writing in paragraphs, in the same typeface, size and color as the page. The paper is different (§3.11); the words never are.
- Several lines in one paragraph can each be lifted. Their undersides stack after the paragraph in line order.
- There's one level only: undersides don't have undersides.
- Its length has no limit beyond the page size guard. A fold's inside may be far bigger than its outside.

**Opening and closing.**
- A lifted line ends in a small crease mark (a folded-corner glyph) and has a faint amber underline. Clicking or tapping either one toggles the underside.
- **Closing never changes the document.** Open or closed is view state, remembered per device and per page together with scroll and caret position. Toggling saves nothing and adds no undo step.
- **Empty undersides.** While both the beneath and the between are empty, the control reads **Unlift** instead of **Close**, and Esc does the same thing. Unlifting removes the mark and the empty underside as one undo step, so Mod+Z brings them back. Close is never offered on an empty fold, so closing never deletes anything.

**Editing the lifted line, copying, pasting and deleting** all follow the hidden-writing contract (§5.1).

### 3.6 Apart and the between

This is the operation the first version bets on. The prototype study (§4) decides whether it stays.

**The writer's action.** On an open fold, the writer uses **Closer** and **Farther** on the underside's edge. There are five stops:

| Stop | Name |
|---|---|
| 0 | touching (the default) |
| 1 | close |
| 2 | apart |
| 3 | far |
| 4 | very far |

The same thing can be done from the keyboard (provisional bindings, spike S5), or by dragging the beneath's edge with a pointer or a finger. Dragging snaps to the stops and is never the only way.

**What changes.**
- The vertical space between the lifted line's paragraph and the beneath changes. On screen it's real empty paper. The prototype starts with 0, 1, 3, 7 and 15 line-heights for the five stops.
- From stop 1 upward, the space holds the **between**: a writable passage in the middle of the gap. When it's empty, the faint word *between* shows only while the fold has focus.
- Any text in the between adds its own lines on top of the gap.

**What remains.** The distance and the between are writing, not a view preference:
- saved in the page document, and undoable
- kept by Keep
- shown in Reading view
- exported, and printed

**Rules.**
- **Moving closer never hides writing.** If the between has text, Closer stops at "close". The control says why: "There's writing between them." To return to touching, the writer empties the between first.
- Distance never changes the size, contrast or readability of any passage.
- **The stops differ with animations off.** With reduced motion, a change of distance is instant. With animation, the beneath glides for 320 ms per stop. Either way the difference is layout, not motion.
- A closed fold shows only its crease mark, never its gap.

**The linear form** is used for Reading view, export, print and screen readers:

```
It was nice to see everyone.

        (empty space: none, 1, 2, 4 or 8 blank lines, by stop)

    Between: Someone passed me the bread and I said thank you like a person.

        (the same empty space)

    Beneath: I felt absent the whole time.
```

- **Print:** the gap is printed white space. "Very far" is half a page.
- **Screen readers:** the underside is announced as "Beneath 'It was nice to see everyone.', set far apart, 4 of 5", with a "Between" group inside it when there's a between.
- **Markdown export:** the distance is written as a line, "(set far apart)", followed by the blank lines.

**Viewing versus authored**

| Thing | Kind | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Open or closed, scroll, caret | Viewing | This device only, never kept |
| Distance and between | Authored | The page, its traces, export and print |
| Step inside's standing distance (later, §9 H1) | Viewing | Nowhere: it isn't stored at all |

**Why Apart, and not another operation.** Codex suggested two kinds of operation: separating two passages to make room for a third, or turning one object to show two readings. Apart is the first kind, and it's in v2 for five reasons:
- It extends the existing fold instead of adding a second kind of object.
- It survives without animation, because it's plain layout.
- It prints.
- It gives play a lever, through absurd distances.
- The study can observe it: do people write between, and do they choose a distance on purpose?

The turning prism stays in §9 as E3. Phase P can swap it in if Apart fails.

### 3.7 Keep and the floor (Phase 2)

**Keeping.**
- From an open fold, the writer chooses **Keep**.
- The app saves a snapshot of the lifted line, the distance, the between and the beneath, with the date.
- In the animation, the composition folds into a slip and settles to the bottom edge. With reduced motion it's a 120 ms fade.
- Then one line appears: "Kept. · Undo · Come back to this…"
  - **Undo** lasts 10 seconds, or until the next action. It deletes the trace outright.
- Keeping the same fold again later makes a new trace, linked to the earlier one (`previousTraceId`).

**A trace**

```
 the dinner                                                  14 Oct
 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   It was nice to see everyone.            │  14 Nov
                                           │  I think I was waiting
                                           │  for someone to notice.
        Someone passed me the bread and    │
        I said thank you like a person.    │
                                           │
   I felt absent the whole time.           │
 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Add · Make a corrected copy · Come back to this · •••
```

A trace shows and offers:
- **The composition** exactly as it was kept, at its distance, with its date. It's read-only.
- **Add.** The writer writes something beside it, dated today. On wide screens it's a column beside the original; on narrow screens it goes below.
  - An added entry has a 10-second undo and can be deleted at any time.
  - It's never edited in place: to change one, delete it and add again.
- **Make a corrected copy.** This opens the kept composition in an editor. Saving creates a new trace marked "Corrected from 14 Oct" (`correctedFromId`).
  - The original stays unless the writer deletes it.
  - The trace view shows the corrected copy, with "Earlier wording" one tap away.
- **Name it.** An optional title in the writer's own words, such as "the soup incident". It can be changed.
- **Come back to this** (§3.8).
- **Go to the page.** Opens the source page at the fold, or says the page was deleted.
- **Delete.** The confirmation reads: "This deletes the kept words and everything added to them. It can't be undone."

**The floor** (the Kept view):
- Traces are stacked like layers of sediment: newest at the top, so scrolling down goes back in time.
- Each trace shows its title (or its lifted line), its date, and its "Coming back 14 Nov" date if it has one.
- A trace grows thicker with each later entry. That records returns, never mood, length or intensity.
- Versions and corrected copies sit together.
- A quiet "Waiting" group at the top holds come-backs that are due but held (§5.5).
- No counts, totals or streaks appear anywhere.

**Deleting a page that has kept traces.** The confirmation says how many traces came from the page and offers two choices:
- delete just the page (the traces are copies, so they stay)
- delete the page and those traces

**The promise, in the app's own words:** "Kept words are a dated snapshot. Folio never changes them." No copy says "permanent", "forever", "unchangeable" or "sealed" (§5.3).

### 3.8 Come back to this (Phase 2)

This is the core's time mechanism. The writer chooses it, it's deterministic, and it needs no model.

**Where.** On a trace: offered right after Keep, and from the trace view.

**Choices.**
- **Tomorrow · In a week · In a month · Pick a day**
- A trace has at most one come-back date; choosing again replaces it.
- **Don't bring this back** clears the date.

**When.** The trace is due at 07:00 in the writer's timezone (`settings.timezone`) on the chosen date. It appears the next time the writer opens Folio on or after that time.

**How it appears.**
- One slip at the top of the page: *You asked to come back to this · 14 October*, followed by the trace's title or lifted line. The date is the day the writer asked, so the slip quotes their own request back to them.
- The slip offers **Open**, **Not now** and **Don't bring this back**. **Not now** hides it until the next day the writer opens Folio.
- If several traces are due, the earliest comes first, one at a time. The next appears after the writer opens or dismisses the current one. Nothing is counted.

**Opening.** The trace opens with its composition and an Add area ready, dated today.

**No notifications.** Folio sends no push, email or SMS. If the writer doesn't open Folio for months, the trace waits.

**Safety.** Within 72 hours of any grounding activation, due traces don't appear at the top. They wait in Kept under "Waiting" and come up after the window. Shore never cancels or changes what the writer chose; it only delays when it arrives (§5.5).

**What happened to Overnight.** Draft 1's Overnight (a question answered by the model the next morning) is dropped as specified. Codex pointed out that delaying an ordinary model call doesn't make the answer better. The useful part of Overnight was meeting yesterday's words with fresh eyes, and that's **Come back to this · Tomorrow**. Any delayed AI answer is a separate later hypothesis that has to justify the delay (§9, H4).

### 3.9 Shore, from the writer's side

When grounding activates, from either layer on any surface:
- The paper goes flat and warm, and motion stops.
- Every word stays where it is, and writing keeps working.
- A fixed, translated line appears at the top: "If things feel like too much right now, you can talk with the companion or reach someone." It offers **Talk now** and **Find help**.
- The existing crisis resources card appears.

**Talk now** opens a new Session in its grounded mode, inside the app and without a reload, so grounding is still active when the opener is built. The Session offers **Bring what you just wrote**. Tapping it puts the paragraph that triggered shore into the Talk composer, unsent.

**Find help** shows the crisis resources card again, even if it was dismissed. It's also always in the ••• menu.

§5.5 is the full, authoritative state table.

### 3.10 Accessibility

**The complete authoring path is the editor itself**, used with a keyboard and with screen readers. Reading view is for reading and export; it isn't the way someone who can't use the visual editor authors.

**Commands.** Every action has a visible control and a keyboard path. The keyboard bindings are provisional until spike S5.

| Action | Control | Keyboard (provisional) |
|---|---|---|
| Lift | button by the selection, or the bottom bar on touch | `Mod+Alt+↓` on an unlifted line |
| Open | crease mark | `Mod+Alt+↓` on a lifted line |
| Close, or Unlift when empty | button | `Esc` inside the underside |
| Closer / Farther | buttons | `Mod+Alt+[` / `Mod+Alt+]` |
| Move between the line, the between and the beneath | — | ordinary arrow keys (the reading order is the document order) |
| Next or previous fold | — | `Mod+Alt+J` / `Mod+Alt+K` |
| Keep, Add, Come back | buttons | focusable, operated with Enter |

**Screen readers.**
- A lifted line is described as "has writing beneath it, closed" (or "open").
- An open underside is a group named "Beneath '‹line›', set ‹distance›, ‹n› of 5", with a "Between" group inside it when there's a between.
- Closed undersides are hidden from the accessibility tree, and they're reached through the line's open control.
- A polite live region announces lifting, unlifting, distance changes ("Farther: far, 4 of 5"), protection events ("Writing beneath a line was kept and opened"), conflicts, and changes of save status (only when the status changes, not on every save).

**Phase 1 acceptance.** These tasks are scripted and run without sighted help in VoiceOver (macOS and iOS) and NVDA (Windows), and again keyboard-only:
1. write a paragraph
2. lift a line and write beneath it
3. set the distance and write between
4. close the fold, find it again and reopen it
5. unlift an empty fold
6. recover from a delete across a closed fold

**Quiet without hiding.**
- Nothing fades.
- Control text meets 4.5:1 contrast. The crease glyph and focus indicators meet 3:1.
- Contrast is measured on rendered pixels, using axe plus manual checks, on both papers and in shore.
- Focus is always visible.

**Sizes.** Touch targets are 44 px and control text is at least 12 px ([`src/components/Session/cardControls.ts`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/components/Session/cardControls.ts)).

**Motion.** `prefers-reduced-motion` is honored everywhere, and a Folio setting can force it.

**Nothing depends on one sense or skill.**
- The crease is a glyph plus an underline, not only a color.
- There's no sound in the first version.
- Dragging is never the only way to do anything.

**Find in page.** The browser's find doesn't see closed undersides. In browsers that support `hidden="until-found"`, spike S1 tries it with the `beforematch` event, so a match opens its fold. Everywhere else this is a documented limit, and Reading view can be searched.

## 4. The prototype study (Phase P)

### 4.1 Why it comes first

Draft 1 tested technical feasibility first and the art last, after weeks of production engineering. This draft reverses the order. The artistic premise is tested with throwaway prototypes before the production editor exists. Phase 0's spikes run in parallel during the study's two-week gap.

### 4.2 The prototypes

**Built outside the app.**
- They live in `prototypes/folio-lab/` *(new)*: one static Vite page that never ships with the app.
- They're excluded from `tsconfig.app.json`, ESLint and the build.
- Storage is local only. There are no accounts, AI calls, analytics or network requests.
- They run on a moderator's laptop and a phone. Serving them on a Firebase preview channel would be an infrastructure decision for the owner (§11).

**Three variants** share one editor shell, typeface and paper. They differ only in the operation:

| Variant | What the writer can do |
|---|---|
| **N: Note** | Select a line and choose **Add note**. A note field opens under the line and toggles with a small icon. This is an ordinary annotation, designed properly, not a strawman. |
| **F: Fold** | Lift a line and write beneath it, with the crease mark and open and close. |
| **A: Fold + Apart** | Everything in F, plus Closer and Farther and the between. |

All three also offer a **Come back** slip. The moderator triggers it in session 2.

**Not built:** the safety pipeline, persistence beyond the device, i18n and conflict handling.

**Safety in the study.**
- Material is invented or low-stakes, and nobody is asked to disclose anything difficult.
- The moderator follows a script and can stop at any time.
- The prototype shows a fixed line: "This is a study prototype. You can stop at any time." It sits next to a printed sheet of crisis resources for the participant's region.
- No production data is involved.

### 4.3 Participants and protocol

**Participants.** Nine adults (three for each order of a Latin square), plus the owner, who uses the prototype for two weeks of real writing.

**Session 1 (about 60 minutes).**

1. **Directed (about 10 minutes per variant, in counterbalanced order).**
   - Every variant gets the same seed paragraph about an ordinary dinner.
   - First prompt: "Try the controls on one line."
   - Then: "Make something with this paragraph that you'd want to keep."
   - The moderator never says what any operation means.
2. **Unprompted (15 minutes).** A blank page in whichever variant the participant prefers, with: "Write about anything from this week. Use any of the controls, or none."
   - We observe whether they lift anything, how, and for what.
   - Afterwards we ask: "What were you trying to do there?"
3. **Interview.**
   - "For each thing you made: what's the relationship between the passages?" The answers are coded as contradiction, elaboration, correction, joke, question, unresolved or other.
   - "Did any version let you make something you wouldn't have made on a normal page?"
   - "Which felt like taking notes, and which felt like making something?"
   - "Did anything make you feel interpreted or managed?"
4. **Choosing.** Each participant picks one thing to come back to in two weeks.

**Session 2 (two weeks later, 20 to 30 minutes).**
- The slip appears, the participant opens it, and we observe.
- Then: "Write beside it if you want."
- Interview:
  - "What was it like to meet this again?"
  - "Does the arrangement (the distance, what's between) mean anything now?"

### 4.4 What would change the direction

The evidence we want is the one Codex named: *people use the operation to make a relationship between passages that they wouldn't otherwise have made, and can describe that relationship without repeating the product's explanation.*

| If we see | It means | Then |
|---|---|---|
| F and N produce the same relationships, described in the same words ("a note", "a comment") | The fold isn't a different medium | Rethink the core before Phase 1: try the prism (E3), or stop |
| In A, people write between or choose a distance on purpose, and describe the relationship in their own words | Apart is an authored operation | Apart is in Phase 1 |
| In A, distance is used only for looking, never with a between, and described as decoration | Apart is a view preference | Drop it from the core; Phase 1 ships F |
| Nobody lifts anything during unprompted writing, in any variant | A problem of discovery or of desire | Redesign the entry before Phase 1 |
| At two weeks, the encounter is only recall ("oh, right") | Come back is weak as designed | Keep it minimal; build no further time features |
| At two weeks, people write beside the original, or say the arrangement means something now | Time and composition work together | Proceed |
| Anyone feels interpreted or managed, or feels the second passage must be the "real" one | A framing problem | Fix the copy and visuals before Phase 1 |

**Not counted as success:** time spent, number of lifts, or enjoyment of the visuals.

### 4.5 Size

- **Build:** 4 to 6 days. The three variants share one shell.
- **Calendar:** about three weeks, including the two-week gap.

## 5. Contracts

These are normative. The implementation in §6 must satisfy them, and §7 lists the tests that prove it.

### 5.1 C1: Hidden writing

> **Writing the writer can't see is never changed while hidden. Any operation that would change it either leaves it untouched or first makes it visible.**

**Why this needs a contract.** Codex reported, and this draft reproduced against the installed packages with a minimal version of the draft 1 schema, that:
- A text selection whose ends lie outside a closed underside still contains it. Copying that selection copies the hidden text.
- Delete removes the underside entirely, even with `isolating: true`.
- Splitting a lifted line in the middle puts the same `foldId` in two paragraphs.

The same reproduction confirmed the mechanism this contract relies on: a restore performed in `appendTransaction` joins the triggering edit's undo event, so one undo returns the original document.

**Behavior with closed folds**

| Operation | Behavior |
|---|---|
| A selection that spans a closed fold | Allowed. The crease mark of each closed fold inside it is shown as selected. |
| Copy | Copies what's visible. Closed undersides are left out. Open undersides are copied as plain paragraphs in reading order (between, then beneath). Fold marks are stripped. |
| Cut | Copy as above, then Delete as below. |
| Delete, Backspace, typing over, pasting over or dropping onto a spanning selection | The visible text in the range is removed. Every closed underside in the range is kept: it's moved to where the deletion happened, marked loose if its line was deleted, and **opened**. A notice offers "Writing beneath a line was kept · Remove it too · Undo". |
| Select All, then Delete | The same: all visible text goes, and closed undersides stay, open and loose. Deleting a whole page on purpose is **Delete page** in the menu, with a confirmation. |
| Undo or redo that changes a closed underside | Opens it first, so the change is visible. |
| Enter inside a lifted line (splitting it) | The fold stays with the fragment containing the start of the lifted text, and the underside follows that fragment. The other fragment becomes ordinary text, and its underline disappears at once, so the change is visible. One undo restores it. A soft break (Shift+Enter) doesn't split the paragraph or affect the fold. |
| Merging two paragraphs | Each fold keeps its line. The undersides re-order after the merged paragraph (I2). |
| A visible edit deletes a whole lifted line | Its underside stays, becomes loose and opens, with **Keep it here**, **Turn into page text** and **Remove**. |
| Paste | Never creates folds. Pasted undersides become ordinary paragraphs. |
| Drag and drop of a range containing a closed fold | Treated as cut, then paste. The moved text loses its fold marks. The closed underside stays where it was, opened and loose. Moving a fold isn't supported in the first version. |
| IME composition, autocorrect, spellcheck or OS text replacement | These replace visible text only. If the safety net below ever restores hidden writing after one of them, it's logged as a code-only diagnostic (`folio.hidden.restored`). |
| Find in page | Closed undersides aren't found. Where `hidden="until-found"` works, a match opens its fold (§3.10). |

**Enforcement, in two layers.**
1. **Commands.** Folio's own command handles keyboard deletion, cut, paste, drop and typing over a selection. It computes the visible ranges and deletes only those, and tags the transaction `folio:visibleEdit`.
2. **A safety net.** The `hiddenWriting` plugin's `appendTransaction` compares the closed undersides before and after every transaction that changes the document. If a transaction without the `folio:visibleEdit` tag removed or changed one, the plugin restores it, loose and open, in the same undo event. This catches whatever the commands miss: IME, extensions and platform input.

**Hard requirement.** On every device in the matrix (§6.12), spike S1 must show **zero** cases where hidden writing is lost or changed by any operation in the table. One reproducible case rejects the design until it's fixed. If architecture A can't meet the requirement, architecture B is evaluated against the same table. This replaces draft 1's rule of switching only if two cases failed.

**Invariants**, enforced in `appendTransaction` and each unit-tested:

| # | Invariant |
|---|---|
| I1 | Every `fold` mark's `foldId` has exactly one underside with that id. |
| I2 | Each underside sits directly after the paragraph containing its mark, ordered among that paragraph's undersides by mark position. |
| I3 | An underside with no matching mark is `loose`. It's removed only by an explicit **Remove**. |
| I4 | No `fold` marks exist inside undersides; any that appear are stripped (their text stays). |
| I5 | `foldId`s are unique. If one is duplicated (for example by a split), the fragment containing the start of the original lifted text keeps it. |
| I6 | A closed underside's content changes only through a transaction tagged `folio:visibleEdit`. Otherwise it's restored. |
| I7 | A between exists only at distance 1 or more, and the distance can't drop to 0 while the between has text. |

Draft 1's I6 ("a closed empty underside is removed") is gone: closing never edits, and empty folds are handled by Unlift (§3.5).

### 5.2 C2: Storage without silent loss

**Guarantees**

1. **Concurrent edits.** Two devices or tabs editing the same page never silently overwrite each other. If both changed it, both versions survive and the writer is told.
2. **Offline edits.** Editing offline survives a reload if the device could store it. The status says honestly whether it could.
3. **Deletion.** A page deleted on one device is never recreated by another device reconnecting. That device's unsaved words are offered as a new page.
4. **Statuses.** "Saved" means the server has acknowledged exactly the words on screen. "Saved on this device" means Folio's own store on the device holds them.

**The model**

- **A chain of revisions.**
  - Every save has a new random `revId`.
  - The page document carries its `revId` and its `parentRevId`, the revision it was built on.
- **Compare-and-swap in the security rules.**
  - An update is accepted only if `request.resource.data.parentRevId == resource.data.revId`.
  - A create is accepted only with `parentRevId == null`.
  - So a write built on a stale revision is rejected by the server, even if it was queued offline. A rejected write never touches the server's copy.
  - Because unique ids are chained (rather than a counter being incremented), a second offline write that was built on the first rejected one is rejected too. A counter would let it through.
- **A store of pending saves on the device.**
  - Before each server write, the controller saves `{ baseRevId, pendingRevIds, doc }` to localStorage (`us.folioPending.<uid>.<pageId>`). It records whether that local write succeeded, as `writeJournalDraft` already does ([`src/store/journalDrafts.ts:62–81`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/store/journalDrafts.ts#L62-L81)).
  - The store is cleared only when the server acknowledges the revision that matches the editor's current content.
- **Acknowledgements arrive in order.** The Firestore SDK sends one client's writes in order. If revision 12 is acknowledged while revision 13 is pending, the status stays "Saved on this device", and the store keeps 13.
- **A rejection is a conflict.**
  - When a write is rejected because its parent is stale, the controller stops writing and keeps the editor content.
  - The server's version stays as the page.
  - This device's version is saved from the pending store as a new page, "‹first line› (this device, 15 Oct 14:02)", and a notice explains what happened.
  - Nothing is ever merged.
- **A remote delete.**
  - If the page no longer exists, an update arrives as a create that carries a parent, and it's rejected. The snapshot also shows the document gone.
  - The writer sees: "This page was deleted on another device. Your recent words are still here." with **Keep as a new page** and **Discard**.
  - The page is never recreated automatically.
- **On load**, if there's a pending store for the page:

  | Situation | Result |
  |---|---|
  | Its parent matches the server's `revId` | It's committed normally (compare-and-swap) |
  | Its parent doesn't match | It becomes a conflict copy |
  | The server's page is gone | The remote-delete prompt |

- **Two tabs.** The `BroadcastChannel` lock stays, so only one tab edits a page and the other shows "Edit here". It's a courtesy. Correctness comes from compare-and-swap.
- **Statuses**

  | Status | Shown when |
  |---|---|
  | Saved | The server has acknowledged the current content |
  | Saved on this device | The pending store holds the current content, and the server hasn't acknowledged it yet |
  | Not saved yet. Keep this tab open. · Copy page | The pending store couldn't be written, and the server hasn't acknowledged the content |
  | Couldn't save to your account. Your words are on this device. | The server rejected the write for a reason other than a conflict, and the pending store holds the content |

  Firestore's own persistent cache may hold a queued write too. Folio claims local safety only from its own store.
- **Size guard.**
  - At 400 KB of serialized document, the app warns: "This page is getting long. A new page might be easier."
  - At 900 KB it refuses Lift and paste.
  - Firestore's limit is 1 MiB per document.

**Spike S4: mandatory outcomes.** Phase 0 can't exit until all eight pass as automated emulator tests, using two SDK instances and `disableNetwork`:

1. **Concurrent writes, online.** Both clients write from the same parent: one is accepted, the other is rejected and becomes a conflict copy. No words are lost.
2. **Concurrent writes, offline.** The same, with both clients offline and then reconnecting, in either order.
3. **Delayed acknowledgement.** The acknowledgement of revision N arrives after revision N+1 was typed, and the status and store stay correct.
4. **Rejected chain.** After the first rejection, the rest of a chain of queued offline writes is rejected and none is applied.
5. **Edit versus delete.** An offline edit, a remote delete, then reconnect: the page isn't recreated and the words are offered as a new page.
6. **localStorage unavailable** (or over quota). The status never says "Saved on this device".
7. **IndexedDB unavailable.** Firestore falls back to its memory cache; after an offline reload, the behavior matches what the status claimed.
8. **Reload in the middle of a chain.** The pending store is committed, or becomes a conflict copy, correctly.

S4 also confirms two assumptions about the Firestore SDK that this design relies on:
- a rejected queued write doesn't stop later queued writes from being sent
- a rejected write's latency-compensated value is rolled back locally

### 5.3 C3: Kept things

Draft 1 claimed traces were unchangeable. Codex showed that its rules still allowed deleting a trace and creating a new one at the same id with different content. This draft deliberately chooses **snapshots the app never rewrites** over **identities that can never be reused**:

- **No editing.** No code path in the app edits a kept composition or an added entry. The rules reject updates to kept fields, which guards against bugs in the app.
- **Deleting.** The writer can always delete a trace and everything added to it.
- **Undo.** For 10 seconds after Keep or Add, Undo deletes what was just created.
- **Correction.** A corrected copy is a new trace. The original stays unless the writer deletes it.
- **Identity.** Trace ids are random, and the app never reuses one. Folio doesn't stop a client outside the app from recreating a deleted id, and nothing in Folio depends on that.
- **Copy.** It never says "permanent", "forever", "unchangeable" or "sealed" (enforced by the copy test in §3.12).

### 5.4 C4: Safety coverage

**Goals.**
- Folio notices distress at least as reliably as the Journal in Sessions.
- It never treats an unavailable check as clear.
- It doesn't change the shared classifier's calibration.

**Units and samples.**
- **Units.** Every paragraph in the page, in a between and in a beneath has a stable paragraph id (`pid`), maintained by the editor. That paragraph is the unit.
- **Samples.** A unit's sample is its paragraph. For a paragraph in a between or a beneath, the sample starts with a context prefix: the lifted line, at most 240 characters, with the middle trimmed if longer.
- **Budget.** A sample is at most 1,000 characters, the classifier's existing window (`text.slice(-1000)`, [`src/ai/openrouter.ts:476`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/ai/openrouter.ts#L476)). Folio doesn't change the window the classifier was tuned on. A paragraph longer than the remaining budget is split into overlapping windows (150 characters of overlap), each a separate sample with the prefix. So the context is never cut off, and a long paste is covered end to end.

**When checks run**

| Trigger | L1 (`detectCrisisKeywords`) | L2 (classifier) |
|---|---|---|
| A unit settles (2 s without typing in it, or the caret leaves it) | On the unit's full text, immediately | Its samples are scheduled |
| Paste | On all the pasted text, immediately | Every affected unit is scheduled |
| Keep, Add, or a corrected copy | On the new text | Scheduled, unless the identical sample was already classified |

**The L2 scheduler.**
- **Slots.** Pending samples are held in a map keyed by `pid` and window index. A new revision of the same unit replaces that unit's pending samples. Different units never replace each other, which fixes draft 1's "newest text wins".
- **Order.** Oldest first, by when each unit first settled, each with its latest text.
- **Rate.** One call in flight, with at least 6 seconds between call starts. That's at most 10 a minute, against the chat function's per-user limit of 30 a minute ([`functions/src/chat.ts:182`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/functions/src/chat.ts#L182)).
- **Deduplication.** A sample whose hash was *classified* in the last 10 minutes is skipped. Hashes are kept in memory only.
- **Short text.** Samples under 12 characters skip L2. L1 still runs.
- **Backlog.** Nothing is ever dropped. The queue drains at the rate limit; L1 has already covered all the text. While more than 30 samples are pending, a code-only diagnostic (`folio.l2.backlog`) is logged.
- **Results.**
  - Classified, risk 2: `activateGrounding('auto')`.
  - Classified, risk 0 or 1: recorded as checked.
  - **Unavailable** (timeout, HTTP error, malformed response or no network): the sample stays pending and is retried after 30 seconds, 2 minutes and 10 minutes, then on the `online` event or the next settle. It's never recorded as checked.
- **Reload.** Each page's pending and unavailable sample keys (`pid` and window index, never text) are stored on the device and re-queued from the document on the next open. An unchecked paragraph survives a reload.
- **Not checked:**
  - text that's only read (opened, in Reading view, or exported)
  - text that was already classified

**An additive classifier result.** The current `classifyCrisisRisk` returns `{ risk: 0 }` both for a real negative and for every failure: HTTP errors, non-string content, invalid risk values and exceptions ([`src/ai/openrouter.ts:498–512`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/ai/openrouter.ts#L498-L512)). Folio needs to tell those apart, without changing any existing caller:

**The Ask contract.** The margin is a later hypothesis (§9, H2), but its contract is fixed now so it can't drift. It's **race the generation, gate the display**:
1. L1 runs on the selection. A hit activates grounding, and no request is sent.
2. The L2 check and the reply start together. The reply streams into a buffer that isn't shown.
3. The reply is shown only when L2 is *classified* with risk below 2, or *unavailable* (the shared fail-open).
4. If L2 returns risk 2:
   - **Before the reply is shown:** the reply is aborted and discarded, grounding activates, and a new reply is requested, built grounded.
   - **After it's shown:** grounding activates, and the note stays, because it was generated under `SAFETY_RULES`.
5. Grounding is read at call time and again before display, never from render-time state.

**Deferred display.** This applies only to later hypotheses that hold generated text to show later. Holding requires L1 to be clear and L2 to be *classified* with risk below 2. An unavailable check stays pending and is never treated as clear. This is a Folio scheduling rule, not a new threshold: grounding still activates exactly on an L1 hit or an L2 risk of 2.

**A pipeline eval before Phase 1.**
- **What it runs.** `test:folio-safety` *(new, live, `RUN_FOLIO_SAFETY_EVAL=1`)* runs the real Folio sampler (units, context prefixes and windows) against the real classifier.
- **Fixtures.**
  - ordinary narrative
  - quoted speech ("she said 'I want to die' and laughed")
  - fiction and literary dark material
  - historical mentions of a crisis
  - grief, and dark humor
  - multilingual text (at least French, Spanish, Japanese and Persian)
  - **pairs whose risk appears only together**, such as "It was a normal Tuesday." with "I've decided how I'll do it." beneath it
  - long pastes with one risk sentence at varying offsets
  - the established active-risk fixtures from `crisisClassifier.eval.ts`
- **Reports.**
  - Recall on the active-risk fixtures. It must meet the shared eval's bar: every must-flag case flags.
  - The false-activation rate on the ordinary, literary and dark fixtures. This is reported to the owner and informs §5.5 and §8.
  - Latency.
- **Release.** It joins [`scripts/run-release-evals.mjs`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/scripts/run-release-evals.mjs) before Folio opens beyond admins.

### 5.5 C5: States

This table is the authoritative timeline. Any other description in this document defers to it.

**The states**

- **Normal.**
- **Shore.** Grounding is active. It's the shared global state, set by either layer on any surface in this app. It ends only through the shared automatic exit, 5 minutes after the last trigger. Whether to add a manual exit is an open question (§11).
- **Quiet.** Shore has ended, and the visit hasn't. A visit ends when Folio reloads, or when the tab has been hidden for 30 minutes. Going to Sessions and back within a visit stays quiet.
- **Recent.** An overlay that applies while less than 72 hours have passed since `lastGroundingAt`. That value is persisted, so it covers every surface and device. Recent can apply together with any of the states above.

| | Normal | Shore | Quiet | Recent (overlay) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The words | As written | Unchanged; never hidden or moved | Unchanged | — |
| Writing, lifting, opening, Closer / Farther | Available | Available, without animation | Available, without animation | — |
| Materials and motion | Full | Flat warm paper, no motion | Flat, no motion | — |
| Keep, Add, corrected copy | Available | Available (L1 and L2 run on the new text) | Available | Available; the trace is stamped `groundedNearKeep` |
| Setting a come-back date | Available | Available | Available | Available (when it arrives is delayed, below) |
| The come-back slip at the top | Shown when due | Hidden | Hidden | Held: due traces wait in Kept ("Waiting") until the overlay ends |
| Shore line, Talk now, Find help | — | Shown | Hidden (Find help stays in •••) | — |
| Crisis resources card | Hidden | Shown | Can be reopened from ••• | — |
| *(later, H1)* Step inside | Available | Closes at once; unavailable | Unavailable | — |
| *(later, H3)* Automatic Return | Eligible | None | None | None |
| *(later, H2)* Ask | Normal register | Grounded reply, with the shore door | Normal register | Grounded register (the SMS channel's 72-hour rule) |

**What a false activation costs.** Draft 1 called shore "cheap to be wrong about". That assumed its emotional cost. This is what observably changes when shore activates:
- the paper flattens and motion stops
- a line with crisis options appears at the top
- the crisis resources card appears
- in later phases, Step inside closes

For 72 hours afterwards, come-backs wait. No words are removed.

We don't know how this feels when it's wrong, during ordinary dark, historical or fictional writing. It may feel like being watched or judged. The pipeline eval reports a false-activation rate before Phase 1 (§5.4), and the integrated study asks about it directly (§8). This draft proposes **no change** to the shared calibration and no bypass.

**Rules.**
- **No content in telemetry.** Folio code MUST NOT put writer text in error messages, `console` output (Sentry records console calls as breadcrumbs by default), first-party diagnostics or analytics. Diagnostics carry codes only.
- **No Google Analytics events at all.** GA shares a property with Ads (see [`src/store/groundingState.ts:63–66`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/store/groundingState.ts#L63-L66)).
- **Admin.** There's no admin view of Folio content. The admin overview may show per-user counts of pages and traces. The policy's statement that administrators can view user data stays true at the database level, and stays in the policy.
- **MCP.** The read-only MCP tools don't expose Folio.
- **Export and deletion.** Export includes everything (§6.8). Account deletion's `recursiveDelete` already covers new collections.
- **Policy.** Before Folio opens beyond admins, `policy.whatWeStoreBody` and `policy.howAiWorksBody` gain sentences about Folio in all 17 languages.
- **First visit.** One line appears under the blank page (`folio.firstVisit`). It can be dismissed and links to the policy.

**Honesty.**
- No word implies secrecy, forgetting or permanence (the copy test in §3.12).
- **Come back** says "You asked to come back to this". It's the writer's own request, presented as theirs, never as the app thinking of them.
- A kept trace shows exact words with exact dates. Nothing in Folio paraphrases the writer.
- Any model text (later phases) always looks different and is labelled.

**Imagery.**
- **The person never sinks.** Words settle; people don't. Near distress, no copy or visual uses drowning, falling, "let go", "disappear" or "rest".
- Distance opens space and never closes in. Copy for Apart never says "sink", "deeper" or "drop".
- The material is paper and air, not water.

### 6.3 Routing and App.tsx

This is unchanged from draft 1, and was verified again at `35f8297`. Appendix F.6 has the current code.

**How it works today.**
- `App.tsx` resolves a root visit (`/` or `/new`) once, during init, to today's active session or to `/session/new`.
- A redirect effect sends every path other than `/`, `/admin*` and `/session*` back to `/`.

```ts
    language: () => string
    unchecked: UncheckedStore                                       // pid + window keys per page, no text
  })
  settle(unit: { pid: string; text: string; context?: string }): void
  paste(units: Array<{ pid: string; text: string; context?: string }>): void
  forget(pid: string): void                                         // the unit was deleted
  restore(pageId: string, units: Map<string, Unit>): void           // re-queue unchecked keys on open
}
```

**`samples.ts`** is pure: `buildSamples(unit) → string[]`. It applies the context budget and the windows.

**`useShore()`** subscribes through `useGroundingMode()`. `visit.ts` tracks when a visit ends: on reload, or after the tab has been hidden for 30 minutes. Folio doesn't change `groundingState.ts`, `crisisFloor.ts` or the classifier's behavior.

**Tests** use fake timers and a fake `check`:
- **Two units are both checked.** Paragraph A settles while a call is in flight, then paragraph B settles, and both are classified. This is the case Codex described.
- **Coalescing.** Revisions of the same unit coalesce.
- **Unavailable checks.** They stay pending and retry with backoff, and are never recorded as checked or deduplicated.
- **Reload.** Unchecked keys are re-queued after a reload.
- **Coverage of long text.** The windows cover a 20,000-character paste with the risk sentence at any offset, and every window carries the context prefix.
- **Limits.** At most 10 calls a minute. L1 runs synchronously on settle and on paste.

## 8. Evaluation

**What we're learning.** Phase P answers whether the operation creates relationships people wouldn't otherwise make (§4). Phase 3 answers whether the integrated version is trustworthy and worth returning to. Neither asks whether people stay longer.

**Owner dogfooding.** Two weeks in Phase P, and the whole of Phase 3. The owner keeps a short private log of moments that felt like making something, and moments that felt like being managed.

**Integrated study (Phase 3).**
- **Participants and setting:** 6 to 8 invited adults, over 3 to 4 weeks, on their own devices.
- **Material:** their own writing. Low stakes are encouraged, and no difficult disclosure is asked for.
- **What we look at:**
  - **Preservation.** Any lost or changed words. The tolerance is zero, and every incident gets a root cause.
  - **Conflicts and remote deletes.** How often they happened, and whether the notices made sense.
  - **Accessibility.** Whether participants who use assistive technology completed what they wanted to do.
  - **False shore activations.** Participants report them. The code-only count of shore activations in Folio is compared with what participants say happened.
  - **Return.** Did come-backs lead to writing beside the original? Did people choose dates?
  - **Making versus keeping.** Do people keep without writing beneath, between or beside?
- **Questions:**
  - "What did the fold mean to you?"
  - "Did the distance feel like your choice?"
  - "Did anything make you feel interpreted, watched or managed?"
  - "Was there a moment you wanted to leave and couldn't find how?"

**Kill or redesign if:**
- the fold consistently reads as the app trying to expose a hidden truth
- Apart reads as decoration
- people can't find the way out
- keeping becomes more attractive than making
- shore false positives during ordinary dark writing happen often enough to feel like being watched (participants say so, or the rate is above the threshold the owner sets, §11)
- there's any preservation incident that a contract change can't fix

**Not measured:** time in the app, visits, streaks, words written or the number of folds. Telemetry is limited to codes.

## 9. Later hypotheses

Each of these has to earn inclusion on its own. Each needs a one-page spec, a claim tied to §2.1, a test, and a decision. None of them is a scheduled phase.

### H1: Step inside

- **Claim.** Standing inside a composition, as a full-screen space with light, changes how it's encountered.
- **Design notes.**
  - The standing distance is labelled **Stand closer** / **Stand back**, so it isn't confused with Apart. It's a viewing distance and isn't stored anywhere.
  - **Back to the page** restores the exact previous selection and scroll. **Back and write** puts the caret at the end of the beneath.
  - It's unavailable in Shore and Quiet.
  - It's a modal dialog, with the focus trap and `inert` root that Room uses.
- **Sound.** Sound is a further sub-hypothesis.
  - It reuses Room's `forest` and `drone` tracks. `ocean` is excluded by the imagery guardrail.
  - Web Audio provides the distance; the fallback is volume only.
  - The seed-audio bucket allows `https://undersurface.me` (checked 2026-10-15); the native origins are unverified.
  - Spike S3 would cover iOS and native.
- **Risk.** Spectacle over writing.

### H2: The margin (Ask)

- **What it is.** The companion, by invitation. The writer selects text and chooses **Ask**, and a short note in graphite appears in the margin, labelled "Companion".
- **What it sees.** Only the selection and which passage it came from.
- **Voice.** The kernel gets a new `'page'` channel, and the plain-voice rules apply.
- **Contract.** §5.4's Ask contract.
- **The writer's options.** A note can be dismissed (never stored), kept (labelled as the companion's) or copied.
- **Crossing.** **Bring into a session** reuses Phase 1's handoff.
- **Eval.** `test:folio`, with about 30 cases: contradiction pairs, playful pairs, grief, dark humor, injection attempts and non-English text.
- **Risk.** It turns Folio back into a companion.

### H3: Automatic Return (the tide)

- **What it is.** Kept things come back unasked.
- **Kept separate from Keep.**
  - **The global default is off.** The writer turns it on in Folio settings.
  - Each trace has its own "May come back on its own" switch, which is also off until the writer turns it on.
- **Eligibility** (draft 1's rules):
  - at least 14 days after keeping
  - not shown in the last 60 days
  - anniversaries first
  - never within 72 hours of grounding
- **Claim.** An unasked return is meaningful, not intrusive.
- **Risk.** It feels like the app deciding what you should remember.

### H4: Delayed answers

- **What it was.** Draft 1's Overnight.
- **Condition.** It's included only if it can say what the delay contributes beyond withholding an immediate reply. One candidate gives the delay a job: after a "Tomorrow" come-back, once the writer has written beside the trace, they may Ask (H2). That involves no delayed generation at all.
- **Safety.** If any held generation remains, it follows §5.4's deferred-display rule.
- **Copy.** It never says "I'll think about it overnight".

### H5: The edition

- **What it is.** A printable book of chosen traces: one per page, each with its composition, distance, dates, title and entries.
- **Format.** Printed through the browser's print dialog. A5 is the default size.
- **Contents.** No generated text, except labelled companion notes that the writer chose to include.

### H6: Spatial and material experiments

| ID | Experiment | Status |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | The long room: undersides inside undersides | Partly served by unlimited beneaths; nesting stays an experiment |
| E2 | The blank between | **Promoted to the core as Apart** |
| E3 | A prism you turn: two or three readings of one line, shown one at a time | **The alternative authored operation** if Phase P rejects Apart |
| E4 | A life not lived: an explicitly fictional annex | Experiment |
| E5 | Undertext: deleted sentences kept on the device as faint silt | Experiment; privacy question |
| E6 | Breath tide: the page's edge moves with the breath, using a heart-rate strap | Experiment; risk of biometrics being treated as truth |
| E7 | A specimen grown from a kept fold's own shape | Experiment; risk of drifting into collectibles |

### H7: Search

- **What it is.** A support feature.
- **Test.** Does it help the writer get back to their own material without becoming the center of the experience (§2.1)?
- **Status.** A candidate for after Phase 3.

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apart reads as decoration | Medium | High | Phase P tests it against a note and a plain fold; E3 is the alternative; Phase 1 can ship without it |
| Hidden writing is lost through an editing path nobody tested | Medium | High | C1's two layers, a safety net that restores, zero tolerance in S1, a production diagnostic |
| Compare-and-swap in the rules behaves differently from the assumptions about the SDK | Low | High | S4 verifies the SDK assumptions and all eight outcomes before Phase 1 |
| Conflict copies multiply and confuse | Low | Medium | Conflicts need two devices editing one page; the notices say what happened; the study measures it |
| A paragraph goes unchecked | Low | High | Per-unit slots, unavailable checks kept pending, unchecked keys surviving reload, L1 on all text |
| False shore activations feel like being watched | Medium | Medium | Measured in S6 and in the study; the cost described honestly; the calibration unchanged |
| Lift is hard to discover on phones | Medium | High | S2, the placeholder line, Phase P's unprompted writing |
| Keep feels like testimony | Medium | Medium | Undo, corrected copies, snapshot wording, deletion always available |
| Folio becomes a notes app | Medium | High | §2.1's criterion; later features gated individually |
| Two products double the maintenance | High | Medium | Isolation in `src/folio/`; one safety stack; Sessions unchanged |
| Translation cost in every phase | High | Low | Translate in batches per phase: machine first, then native review |
| The policy change needs re-consent | Low | Medium | Owner decision (§11) |
| The "Bring what you just wrote" handoff crosses the memory boundary without consent | Low | Medium | A one-time `sessionStorage` key, read only by the composer, never sent without a tap; tested |

## Appendix C: Background for reviewers new to the codebase

- **Sessions.** Opening the app drops the writer into a session with an AI companion, across four surfaces:
  - **Talk:** streamed text.
  - **Journal:** a quiet editor where optional "inner voices" can respond on pause.
  - **Speak:** live voice.
  - **Room:** ambient sound and voice, or quiet company.

  An optional SMS channel carries the same companion between sessions.
- **The crisis stack.**
  - **L1** is a narrow, deterministic regex floor for sincere, present, first-person methods and plans.
  - **L2** is a gpt-5-nano classifier tuned for precision, which runs on every path the user writes. It fails open to risk 0.
  - Either layer activates **grounding**: a global, in-memory state.
    - It changes every prompt to a grounded register and suppresses companion programs.
    - It shows a crisis resources card.
    - It saves a timestamp that the SMS channel reads for 72 hours.
  - The classifier is deliberately tuned against over-calling. A previous version flagged grief, metaphor and gallows humor.
- **Fresh eyes.** A per-session switch that keeps durable memory out of the companion's context.
- **The parts ontology** (decided 2026-07-04). The seven seeded parts belong to the companion. The writer's own parts are a separate, consent-based map. Folio uses neither.
- **Feature flags** live in `appConfig/global.features`. The server rejects unknown keys, so functions deploy before hosting.
- **Deploy order:** indexes, then rules, then functions, then hosting.

### F.1 The L2 classifier fails open to the same value as a real negative ([`src/ai/openrouter.ts:452–516`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/ai/openrouter.ts#L452-L516))

This motivates the additive `checkCrisisRisk` in C4 (§5.4). Every failure path returns `{ risk: 0 }`, which is indistinguishable from a successful "no risk".

```ts
 * Synchronous L2 crisis classifier — multilingual, narrow-by-construction.
 *
 * Calls the chat function with `task: 'crisis_check'`, which routes to a
 * smaller/faster model with structured-output enforcement. Risk tier semantics
 * are documented in crisisClassifierPrompt.ts.
 *
 * Fails open on timeout / network error / parse failure: returns {risk: 0}.
 * The L1 regex layer in sessionOrchestrator already ran by the time we get
 * here, so falling open leaves the existing safety net intact. The
 * alternative — bricking message send on transient API failure — is worse.
 *
 * Used by partOrchestrator for diary-mode crisis classification. In session
 * mode, L2 is folded into the therapist stream as a `<<RISK:N>>` preamble.
 */
export async function classifyCrisisRisk(
  text: string,
  language?: string,
): Promise<CrisisRiskResult> {
  const token = await getAuthToken()
  const controller = new AbortController()
  const timeout = setTimeout(() => controller.abort(), 5000)

  // Recent slice is enough — explicit ideation tends to appear in the most
  // recent paragraph, and a tighter input keeps latency + cost down.
  const sample = text.slice(-1000)
  const userContent = language && language !== 'en'
    ? `Language: ${language}\n\nText:\n${sample}`
    : sample

  try {
    const response = await fetch(apiUrl('/api/chat'), {
      method: 'POST',
      headers: {
        'Authorization': `Bearer ${token}`,
        'Content-Type': 'application/json',
      },
      body: JSON.stringify({
        task: 'crisis_check',
        messages: [
          { role: 'system', content: CRISIS_CLASSIFIER_PROMPT },
          { role: 'user', content: userContent },
        ],
      }),
      signal: controller.signal,
    })

    if (!response.ok) return { risk: 0 }

    const data = await response.json()
    const content = data?.choices?.[0]?.message?.content
    if (typeof content !== 'string') return { risk: 0 }

    const parsed = JSON.parse(content) as { risk?: unknown; reason?: unknown }
    const risk = parsed.risk
    if (risk !== 0 && risk !== 1 && risk !== 2) return { risk: 0 }
    return {
      risk: risk as CrisisRisk,
      reason: typeof parsed.reason === 'string' ? parsed.reason : undefined,
    }
  } catch {
    return { risk: 0 }
  } finally {
    clearTimeout(timeout)
  }
}
```

### F.2 Grounding is a global in-memory state with an auto-exit ([`src/store/groundingState.ts:1–86`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/store/groundingState.ts#L1-L86))

C5's "Shore" state is exactly this state. Folio subscribes to it and adds nothing to it. The activation sinks are how `lastGroundingAt` (the 72-hour "Recent" overlay) gets persisted.

```ts
import { getGlobalConfig } from './globalConfig'
import { engineLog } from '../services/diagnostics'

// Module-level state
let groundingActive = false
let autoExitTimer: ReturnType<typeof setTimeout> | null = null

const listeners = new Set<() => void>()
// Fired on EVERY activation request (first activation and re-triggers alike),
// regardless of which layer detected distress: L1 floor, raced L2 classifier,
// in-stream <<RISK:N>> preamble, voice/Room transcripts. Used to persist a
// server-readable crisis timestamp. Sinks run best-effort and can never throw
// into the crisis path.
type ActivationSink = (at: number) => void | Promise<unknown>
const activationSinks = new Set<ActivationSink>()

export function onGroundingActivated(sink: ActivationSink): () => void {
  activationSinks.add(sink)
  return () => { activationSinks.delete(sink) }
}

function notifyActivation() {
  const at = Date.now()
  for (const sink of activationSinks) {
    try {
      const result = sink(at)
      if (result && typeof (result as Promise<unknown>).catch === 'function') {
        void (result as Promise<unknown>).catch(() => { /* best-effort */ })
      }
    } catch {
      // A sink failure must never reach the caller that detected distress.
    }
  }
}

function notify() {
  for (const fn of listeners) fn()
}

export function subscribeGrounding(callback: () => void) {
  listeners.add(callback)
  return () => { listeners.delete(callback) }
}

function resetAutoExit() {
  if (autoExitTimer) clearTimeout(autoExitTimer)
  const config = getGlobalConfig()
  const minutes = config?.grounding?.autoExitMinutes ?? 5
  autoExitTimer = setTimeout(() => {
    deactivateGrounding()
  }, minutes * 60 * 1000)
}

export function activateGrounding(trigger: 'auto' | 'manual' = 'auto'): void {
  if (groundingActive) {
    // Re-trigger resets the auto-exit timer
    resetAutoExit()
    notifyActivation()
    return
  }
  groundingActive = true
  document.documentElement.setAttribute('data-grounding', 'true')
  // First-party diagnostics only. Crisis-state signals must never reach
  // Google Analytics: GA is keyed to the user id and shares a property with
  // Ads conversions, so a grounding event there attaches sensitive-category
  // health data to an advertising identifier.
  engineLog('info', 'grounding.activated', 'grounding mode activated', { extra: { trigger } })
  notify()
  resetAutoExit()
  notifyActivation()
}

export function deactivateGrounding(): void {
  if (!groundingActive) return
  groundingActive = false
  document.documentElement.removeAttribute('data-grounding')
  if (autoExitTimer) {
    clearTimeout(autoExitTimer)
    autoExitTimer = null
  }
  notify()
}

export function isGroundingActive(): boolean {
  return groundingActive
}
```

### F.3 The Journal's crisis check in Sessions, the baseline Folio must match ([`src/engine/partOrchestrator.ts:553–581`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/engine/partOrchestrator.ts#L553-L581))

The Journal runs L1 and then a fire-and-forget L2 on the last 1,000 characters, with a 10-second limit on identical samples. A failed L2 is simply lost. Folio's scheduler (§5.4) is stricter: each paragraph has its own slot, and an unavailable check stays pending.

```ts

  // mode. L1 fires immediately if hit; L2 fires non-blocking with a 10s
  // rate limit on identical text. Either layer activating grounding biases
  // part selection toward self-role parts via the existing scorePart logic.
  private checkCrisisInJournalMode(text: string): void {
    if (detectCrisisKeywords(text)) {
      activateGrounding('auto')
      return
    }

    const sample = text.slice(-1000)
    const now = Date.now()
    if (
      sample === this.lastCrisisCheckSample
      && now - this.lastCrisisCheckTime < this.CRISIS_CHECK_RATE_LIMIT_MS
    ) {
      return
    }
    this.lastCrisisCheckSample = sample
    this.lastCrisisCheckTime = now

    classifyCrisisRisk(sample, getLanguageCode())
      .then(result => {
        if (result.risk === 2) {
          activateGrounding('auto')
        }
      })
      .catch(() => { /* fail-open inside classifyCrisisRisk */ })
  }
```

```ts
    return true
```

```js
function applyTransaction(history, state, tr, options) {
    let historyTr = tr.getMeta(historyKey), rebased;
    if (historyTr)
        return historyTr.historyState;
    if (tr.getMeta(closeHistoryKey))
        history = new HistoryState(history.done, history.undone, null, 0, -1);
    let appended = tr.getMeta("appendedTransaction");
    if (tr.steps.length == 0) {
        return history;
    }
    else if (appended && appended.getMeta(historyKey)) {
        if (appended.getMeta(historyKey).redo)
            return new HistoryState(history.done.addTransform(tr, undefined, options, mustPreserveItems(state)), history.undone, rangesFor(tr.mapping.maps), history.prevTime, history.prevComposition);
        else
            return new HistoryState(history.done, history.undone.addTransform(tr, undefined, options, mustPreserveItems(state)), null, history.prevTime, history.prevComposition);
    }
    else if (tr.getMeta("addToHistory") !== false && !(appended && appended.getMeta("addToHistory") === false)) {
        // Group transforms that occur in quick succession into one event.
        let composition = tr.getMeta("composition");
        let newGroup = history.prevTime == 0 ||
            (!appended && history.prevComposition != composition &&
                (history.prevTime < (tr.time || 0) - options.newGroupDelay || !isAdjacentTo(tr, history.prevRanges)));
```

The original run used prosemirror-state 1.4.4 and prosemirror-history 1.5.0, with prosemirror-model and prosemirror-commands at the versions installed in the repository at `35f8297`.

```js
const NM = ''   // run from a directory where the packages are installed
const { Schema } = require(NM + 'prosemirror-model')
const { EditorState, TextSelection, Plugin } = require(NM + 'prosemirror-state')
const { history, undo } = require(NM + 'prosemirror-history')
const { splitBlock, deleteSelection } = require(NM + 'prosemirror-commands')

const schema = new Schema({
  nodes: {
    doc: { content: '(paragraph | underside)+' },
    paragraph: { content: 'text*', group: 'block' },
    underside: { content: 'paragraph+', group: 'block', defining: true, isolating: true, attrs: { foldId: { default: null } } },
    text: {},
  },
  marks: { fold: { attrs: { foldId: { default: null } }, inclusive: false, excludes: 'fold' } },
})
const p = (t, marks) => schema.node('paragraph', null, t ? [schema.text(t, marks)] : [])
const fold = schema.marks.fold.create({ foldId: 'f1' })
const doc = schema.node('doc', null, [
  p('Before.'),
  schema.node('paragraph', null, [schema.text('It was nice to see everyone.', [fold])]),
  schema.node('underside', { foldId: 'f1' }, [p('I felt absent the whole time.')]),
  p('After.'),
])
const text = (d) => { const out = []; d.descendants(n => { if (n.isText) out.push(n.text) }); return out.join(' | ') }
const run = (state, cmd) => { let s = state; cmd(s, tr => { s = s.apply(tr) }); return s }

// 1. Spanning selection from "Before." into "After." deletes the underside
let s = EditorState.create({ doc, plugins: [history()] })
const from = 3, to = s.doc.content.size - 3
s = s.apply(s.tr.setSelection(TextSelection.create(s.doc, from, to)))
const copied = s.selection.content().content.toString()
console.log('1a copy of spanning selection includes hidden text:', copied.includes('absent'))
const afterDel = run(s, deleteSelection)
console.log('1b after Delete:', text(afterDel.doc), '| underside survives:', text(afterDel.doc).includes('absent'))

// 2. Splitting inside the lifted line duplicates the foldId across paragraphs
let s2 = EditorState.create({ doc })
const linePos = 9 + 2 + 11 // inside "It was nice|"
s2 = s2.apply(s2.tr.setSelection(TextSelection.create(s2.doc, linePos)))
s2 = run(s2, splitBlock)
const ids = []
s2.doc.forEach(n => { if (n.type.name === 'paragraph') n.forEach(c => { const m = c.marks.find(mk => mk.type.name === 'fold'); if (m) ids.push(m.attrs.foldId + ':' + c.text) }) })
console.log('2 fold fragments after split:', ids)

// 3. appendTransaction that restores a deleted underside joins the same undo event
const restore = new Plugin({
  appendTransaction(trs, oldState, newState) {
    if (!trs.some(t => t.docChanged)) return null
    let had = null; oldState.doc.forEach(n => { if (n.type.name === 'underside') had = n })
    let has = false; newState.doc.forEach(n => { if (n.type.name === 'underside') has = true })
    if (!had || has) return null
    return newState.tr.insert(newState.selection.from > 1 ? newState.doc.content.size : 0, had)
  },
})
let s3 = EditorState.create({ doc, plugins: [history(), restore] })
s3 = s3.apply(s3.tr.setSelection(TextSelection.create(s3.doc, from, to)))
s3 = run(s3, deleteSelection)
console.log('3a after Delete with restore plugin:', text(s3.doc))
s3 = run(s3, undo)
console.log('3b one undo returns the original doc:', s3.doc.eq(doc))
```

**Limits.**
- This isn't a Folio editor: no view, no DOM, no browser, no IME.
- It shows that the schema alone doesn't protect hidden content, and that a restore can share an undo event with the edit that triggered it.
- The restore plugin here is deliberately crude: it re-inserts at the end of the document. The real one places the underside where the deletion happened and opens it (§5.1).
