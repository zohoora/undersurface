## 0. For reviewers

### 0.1 What this is

A proposal for a second, separate way of using UnderSurface.

Today the app is a companion: you open it and a conversation starts. **Folio** is a writing tool: you open it and there is a quiet page. The current system stays exactly as it is and stays one tap away. This document calls it **Sessions** (the owner calls it "legacy").

If you don't know the codebase, read Appendix C first.

### 0.2 Decisions the owner has already made

1. Writing tool first, not companion first.
2. Folio is a complete, separate mode that lives alongside Sessions. Both stay usable.
3. The crisis-safety stack covers Folio just as it covers Sessions.

These are fixed. Tell us if one of them causes a problem.

## 1. The proposition

### 1.1 In one sentence

> **Folio lets you compose relationships between passages, keep those relationships, and meet them again over time.**

This sentence is also the test that every feature has to pass (§2.1).

### 1.2 The act

> Lift a line. Write what's beneath it. Then decide how far apart the two should stand, and write what lives between them.

The writer authors three things that an ordinary page doesn't invite:

- **The beneath.** A second passage attached to a line. It has equal standing; it isn't a correction or a hidden truth.
- **The distance.** How far apart the two stand, chosen from five stops. It's saved as part of the writing.
- **The between.** A third passage that exists only in the space the distance opened.

Then there are two choices about time:

- **Keep.** A dated snapshot of the whole composition: line, distance, between and beneath.
- **Come back to this.** The writer picks a day to meet it again. On that day it's waiting at the top of Folio, and they can write beside it. If the study supports it, they can also lift its line into today's page and write beneath it again (§3.8).

### 1.3 "What can I make here that I wouldn't have thought to make on an ordinary page?"

A small composition of two or three passages whose arrangement is part of what it says:
- a line
- what's beneath it
- how far apart they stand
- what lies between them

It's kept with its date, met again on a day you chose, and new words go beside it.

**A caveat.** You could type something similar on any page using blank lines. The claim isn't that this is impossible elsewhere. The claim is that this page makes it the natural thing to do, and that people will make relationships here they wouldn't otherwise make. That's a hypothesis, and §4 tests it before anything is built for production.

**What Apart adds beyond pressing Enter.** Blank lines are spacing. Apart's distance is a named, saved stance:
- "far" or "very far" is announced to a screen reader, written into export as "(set far apart)" and kept with the trace
- when the fold closes, the distance folds away behind the crease with everything else, so a closed fold holds *how far* as well as *what*
- the between can exist only once the two passages have been set apart: it's a passage made possible by an act of separation

If that's all it is, it's thin, and the study is built to find out: D2a and D2b (§4.4) score the between and the distance separately. The fold is the core. Apart is the hypothesis most likely to fail.

### 1.4 Five encounters (illustrative; all text invented)

**Ordinary.** It's late. You write a few lines about a dinner, select "It was nice to see everyone." and choose **Lift**. A crease opens under the line, and the caret is waiting on a strip of slightly different paper. You write "I felt absent the whole time." Then you close it. The line now has a small crease mark at its end. Nothing else changes.

**Made.** A few days later you open the fold and press **Farther** twice. The beneath moves down the page, leaving a band of open paper. In the middle of that space the word *between* waits, faintly. You write "Someone passed me the bread and I said thank you like a person." The three passages now stand at the distance you chose. You keep it. In the kept slip they stand exactly as you set them, and if you print it, the gap is real white space.

**Playful.** You write "We laughed until the soup went cold." Beneath it: "Nobody remembers what was funny." You push them as far apart as they go, a whole screen of empty paper, and in the middle you write only "(soup)". You keep it and name it *the soup incident*.

Another night you write "The meeting went fine." Beneath it you write four paragraphs. The fold's inside is much bigger than its outside, and the page lets it be. The app never noticed that anything was funny; you made it funny.

**Return.** When you keep the dinner fold, you choose **Come back to this · In a month**. A month later you open Folio and it's at the top: *You asked to come back to this · 14 October*. You open it. The composition is exactly as you left it. Beside it, dated today, you write "I think I was waiting for someone to notice." The original doesn't change, and nobody congratulates you.

**A hard night.** You write something heavier. A little later the paper goes flat and still, and every word stays where you left it. A plain line appears at the top: you can talk with the companion now, or reach someone.

If you choose **Talk now**, a Session opens in its grounded mode. If you choose **Talk now, bringing this**, Folio first shows you the words it would bring, and you decide. If you say **I'm okay**, the line goes away and the paper stays calm.

For the next three days, nothing you kept comes up by itself. Anything you asked to come back to waits in Kept, where you can open it whenever you like. (§5.5 is the authoritative description.)

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 The originality criterion

Draft 1 had three tests. This draft keeps two of them and replaces the third with a positive criterion. Every feature answers three questions:

1. **Does it help the writer compose, preserve or re-encounter a relationship between passages?** If not, it's either support (and stays small and plain) or it's out.
2. **If every generated reply disappeared, would this still be worth doing?** (from *Below the page*)
3. **Does it become recognizably the writer's through what they do in it, without the system declaring who they are?** (from *Below the page*)

**Removed:** draft 1's "Could a chat window do this?" and its section on what chat boxes can't do. Those claims were broad and partly untrue: chat products already have memory, scheduled tasks and generated documents. The argument doesn't need them.

**Omissions are scope, not originality.** Leaving out search, folders and formatting keeps the first version small. It doesn't make Folio original, and it can make saved writing harder to use. Support features are judged by one question: does this help the writer get back to their own material without becoming the center of the experience? Search is a later candidate on those terms (§9, H7).

### 2.2 Laws

1. **Only the writer's decisions make anything last.**
   - The writer writes the passages.
   - Nothing is automatically kept, summarized, collected or "noticed".
   - If a later phase lets a companion note be kept, it lasts because the writer chose to keep it, and it stays labelled as not the writer's.
2. **Neither passage is truer.** The app never ranks the line against its beneath or between. It never labels a meaning, a mood or a part, and never infers who the writer is.
3. **Plain words, strange material.**
   - Metaphor lives in materials, space and motion.
   - Labels, buttons and any companion voice stay plain.
   - The form may be lopsided, vast, tiny or absurd, but the app never detects tone in order to apply an effect.
4. **Time is a material.** Kept things keep their shape. New words go beside old ones and never replace them. Things come back when the writer asked them to.
5. **The exit survives every state.** Every view has a visible, immediate way back. Nothing ends with a completion ritual.
6. **It doesn't need you.**
   - no streaks or reminders
   - no notifications
   - no withered garden after an absence
   - no measures of time spent

   A "come back" waits silently. It never sends a message.
7. **What you made survives what you do by accident.** This law is new in this draft.
   - Hidden writing is never changed while hidden.
   - One device's save never silently overwrites another's.
   - A keep can be undone.
   - A kept thing can be corrected without erasing what it was.

**The safety law overrides all seven: grounding brings you to shore.** When distress is detected:
- the strange material switches off: the paper goes flat and warm, and motion stops
- nothing resurfaces
- a plain door appears to the companion and to help

The writer's words always stay on the page.

### 2.3 What Folio is not

- **Not a notes app.** The first version has no folders, tags, formatting toolbar or tasks. (These are left out for scope, not as proof of originality; see §2.1.)
- **Not a prompt machine.** No prompts and no daily question.
- **Not an analysis tool.** No summaries, themes, mood charts or "patterns".
- **Not therapy, and not a replacement for Sessions.** Folio offers the door to Sessions.
- **Not private in the sense of unseen.** Pages are stored in the account. What's written goes to an AI service for safety checks, in pieces of at most 1,000 characters. The only exception is a fragment under 20 characters on a nearly empty page, which the app's own rules check alone (§5.4, §5.6).

### 3.5 Lift and the beneath

**Lifting.**
- Select text inside one paragraph of the page, not inside an underside. A **Lift** control appears near the selection on pointer devices, or in a bottom bar on touch screens (spike S2).
- With no selection, the keyboard command lifts the sentence around the caret. Sentence boundaries come from `Intl.Segmenter`, falling back to the whole paragraph.
- A lift covers 1 to 400 characters. A range that overlaps an existing fold can't be lifted; the app says "This line already has something beneath it" and offers **Open**.
- Lifting is one undo step. It marks the line, inserts an open underside directly after its paragraph at distance "touching", and puts the caret in the beneath.

**The beneath.**
- It's ordinary writing in paragraphs, in the same typeface, size and color as the page. The paper is different (§3.11); the words never are.
- Several lines in one paragraph can each be lifted. Their undersides stack after the paragraph in line order.
- There's one level only: undersides don't have undersides.
- Its length has no limit beyond the page size guard. A fold's inside may be far bigger than its outside.

**Opening and closing.**
- A lifted line ends in a small crease mark (a folded-corner glyph) and has a faint amber underline. Clicking or tapping either one toggles the underside.
- **Closing never changes the document.** Open or closed is view state, remembered per device and per page together with scroll and caret position. Toggling saves nothing and adds no undo step.
- **Empty undersides.** While both the beneath and the between are empty, the control reads **Unlift** instead of **Close**, and Esc does the same thing. Unlifting removes the mark and the empty underside as one undo step, so Mod+Z brings them back. Close is never offered on an empty fold, so closing never deletes anything.

**Editing the lifted line, copying, pasting and deleting** all follow the hidden-writing contract (§5.1).

### 3.6 Apart and the between

This is the operation the first version bets on. The prototype study (§4) decides whether it stays.

**The writer's action.** On an open fold, the writer uses **Closer** and **Farther** on the underside's edge. There are five stops:

| Stop | Name |
|---|---|
| 0 | touching (the default) |
| 1 | close |
| 2 | apart |
| 3 | far |
| 4 | very far |

The same thing can be done from the keyboard (provisional bindings, spike S5), or by dragging the beneath's edge with a pointer or a finger. Dragging snaps to the stops and is never the only way.

**What changes.**
- The vertical space between the lifted line's paragraph and the beneath changes. On screen it's real empty paper. The prototype starts with 0, 1, 3, 7 and 15 line-heights for the five stops.
- From stop 1 upward, the space holds the **between**: a writable passage in the middle of the gap. When it's empty, the faint word *between* shows only while the fold has focus.
- Any text in the between adds its own lines on top of the gap.

**The geometry, exactly.** The distance is empty paper, and the between never uses it up.
- The stop's space is split equally above and below the between.
- The between's own lines are added to that space; they're never subtracted from it.
- So a "far" fold with a long between is taller than a "far" fold with an empty one. The air around the between stays what the writer chose.

Gemini asked whether this makes the distance "dead whitespace" once there's a between. It is whitespace; that's the material. What the writer authors is how much air surrounds the middle passage.

**What the distance means.** Whatever the writer means by it. The app never interprets it (Law 2). It has no effect beyond layout, print and export:
- It doesn't make a fold harder to open.
- It doesn't change when anything comes back.
- It doesn't change how anything is checked.

If writers treat it as decoration, that's the result Phase P is designed to detect (§4.4).

**What remains.** The distance and the between are writing, not a view preference:
- saved in the page document, and undoable
- kept by Keep
- shown in Reading view
- exported, and printed

**Rules.**
- **Moving closer never hides writing.** If the between has text, Closer stops at "close". The control says why: "There's writing between them." To return to touching, the writer empties the between first.
- Distance never changes the size, contrast or readability of any passage.
- **The stops differ with animations off.** With reduced motion, a change of distance is instant. With animation, the beneath glides for 320 ms per stop. Either way the difference is layout, not motion.
- A closed fold shows only its crease mark, never its gap.

**The linear form** is used for Reading view, export, print and screen readers:

```
It was nice to see everyone.

        (empty space: the blank lines above the between, in the table below)

    Between: Someone passed me the bread and I said thank you like a person.

        (the same number below it)

    Beneath: I felt absent the whole time.
```

- **Blank lines** *(one derivation, draft 4)*. On screen, a stop's line-heights are split equally above and below the between. The linear form uses the same numbers as blank lines, and with a between it rounds each half up:

  | Stop | On screen (line-heights) | Linear, empty between (blank lines) | Linear, with a between (blank lines above, and again below) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0 touching | 0 | 0 | — (no between at touching) |
  | 1 close | 1 | 1 | 1 |
  | 2 apart | 3 | 3 | 2 |
  | 3 far | 7 | 7 | 4 |
  | 4 very far | 15 | 15 | 8 |

- **Print:** the gap is printed white space. "Very far" is about a third of an A4 or Letter page.
- **Screen readers:** the underside is announced as "Beneath 'It was nice to see everyone.', set far apart, 4 of 5", with a "Between" group inside it when there's a between.
- **Markdown export:** the distance is written as a line, "(set far apart)", followed by the blank lines.

**Viewing versus authored**

| Thing | Kind | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Open or closed, scroll, caret | Viewing | This device only, never kept |
| Distance and between | Authored | The page, its traces, export and print |
| Step inside's standing distance (later, §9 H1) | Viewing | Nowhere: it isn't stored at all |

**Why Apart, and not another operation.** Codex suggested two kinds of operation: separating two passages to make room for a third, or turning one object to show two readings. Apart is the first kind, and it's in this plan for five reasons:
- It extends the existing fold instead of adding a second kind of object.
- It survives without animation, because it's plain layout.
- It prints.
- It gives play a lever, through absurd distances.
- The study can observe it: do people write between, and do they choose a distance on purpose?

The turning prism stays in §9 as E3. Phase P can swap it in if Apart fails.

### 3.7 Keep and the floor (Phase 2)

**Keeping.**
- From an open fold, the writer chooses **Keep**.
- The app saves a snapshot of the lifted line, the distance, the between and the beneath, with the date.
- In the animation, the composition folds into a slip and settles to the bottom edge. With reduced motion it's a 120 ms fade.
- Then one line appears: "Kept. · Undo · Come back to this…"
  - **Undo** lasts 10 seconds, or until the next action. It deletes the trace outright.
- Keeping the same fold again later makes a new trace, linked to the earlier one (`previousTraceId`).

**A trace**

```
 the dinner                                                  14 Oct
 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   It was nice to see everyone.            │  14 Nov
                                           │  I think I was waiting
                                           │  for someone to notice.
        Someone passed me the bread and    │
        I said thank you like a person.    │
                                           │
   I felt absent the whole time.           │
 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Add · Come back to this · •••
```

A trace shows and offers:
- **The composition** exactly as it was kept, at its distance, with its date. It's read-only.
- **Add.** The writer writes something beside it, dated today. On wide screens it's a column beside the original; on narrow screens it goes below.
  - An added entry has a 10-second undo and can be deleted at any time.
  - It's never edited in place: to change one, delete it and add again.
- **Make a corrected copy** *(after Phase 3, and only if Phase 3 shows writers want it; Fable A9)*. This opens the kept composition in an editor. Saving creates a new trace marked "Corrected from 14 Oct" (`correctedFromId`).
  - The original stays unless the writer deletes it.
  - The trace view shows the corrected copy, with "Earlier wording" one tap away.
  - Until it exists, a correction is Undo within 10 seconds, deleting the trace, or keeping the fold again from the page. That still meets C3: nothing kept is rewritten, and nothing is irrevocable.
- **Name it.** An optional title in the writer's own words, such as "the soup incident". It can be changed.
- **Come back to this** (§3.8).
- **Go to the page.** Opens the source page at the fold, or says the page was deleted.
- **Delete.** The confirmation reads: "This deletes the kept words and everything added to them. It can't be undone."

**The floor** (the Kept view):
- Traces are stacked like layers of sediment: newest at the top, so scrolling down goes back in time.
- Each trace shows its title (or its lifted line), its date, and its "Coming back 14 Nov" date if it has one.
- A trace grows thicker with each later entry. That records returns, never mood, length or intensity.
- Versions and corrected copies sit together.
- A quiet "Waiting" group at the top holds come-backs that are due but held (§5.5).
- No counts, totals or streaks appear anywhere.

**Deleting a page that has kept traces.** The confirmation says how many traces came from the page and offers two choices:
- delete just the page (the traces are copies, so they stay)
- delete the page and those traces

**The promise, in the app's own words** (`folio.keptPromise`, so the copy test sees it): "Kept words are a dated snapshot. Folio never changes them." No copy says "permanent", "forever", "unchangeable" or "sealed" (§5.3).

### 3.8 Come back to this (Phase 2)

This is the core's time mechanism. The writer chooses it, it's deterministic, and it needs no model.

**Where.** On a trace: offered right after Keep, and from the trace view.

**Choices.**
- **Tomorrow · In a week · In a month · Pick a day**
- A trace has at most one come-back date; choosing again replaces it.
- **Don't bring this back** clears the date.

**When.** The trace is due at 07:00 in the writer's timezone (`settings.timezone`) on the chosen date. It appears the next time the writer opens Folio on or after that time.

**How it appears.**
- One slip at the top of the page: *You asked to come back to this · 14 October*, followed by the trace's title or lifted line. The date is the day the writer asked, so the slip quotes their own request back to them.
- The slip offers **Open**, **Not now** and **Don't bring this back**. **Not now** hides it until the next day the writer opens Folio.
- If several traces are due, the earliest comes first, one at a time. The next appears after the writer opens or dismisses the current one. Nothing is counted.

**Opening.** The trace opens with its composition and an Add area ready, dated today.

**No notifications.** Folio sends no push, email or SMS. If the writer doesn't open Folio for months, the trace waits.

**Safety.** Within 72 hours of any grounding activation, due traces don't appear at the top. They wait in Kept under "Waiting" and come up after the window. Each one there offers **Open**, because the writer asked for it: only the slip that would appear unasked is held *(changed in draft 4; Fable A2)*. Shore never cancels or changes what the writer chose (§5.5).

**Lift into today's page** *(Phase 2, only if the study supports it: §4.4, D5b; Fable A6)*.
- **The act.** From a trace, or from its come-back slip, the writer chooses **Lift into today's page**. The trace's lifted line, quoted exactly, becomes the lifted line of a new fold on the current page, marked "from 14 Oct". The caret waits in the beneath.
- **Why.** The source visions made time a material of composition: "A phrase from last winter can show through a phrase from today, if the writer chooses" (*Below the page*). Add, a column beside a read-only snapshot, is the annotation shape Codex warned against. This makes meeting again the same act as Lift: the old words become a line you write beneath today.
- **What doesn't change.** The trace. The new fold is ordinary page writing, so C1, C2 and C4 apply unchanged; the quoted line is new page text and is checked like any other. The underside records where the line came from (`fromTraceId`, `fromKeptAt`; §6.5).

**What happened to Overnight.** Draft 1's Overnight (a question answered by the model the next morning) is dropped as specified. Codex pointed out that delaying an ordinary model call doesn't make the answer better. The useful part of Overnight was meeting yesterday's words with fresh eyes, and that's **Come back to this · Tomorrow**. Any delayed AI answer is a separate later hypothesis that has to justify the delay (§9, H4).

### 3.9 Shore, from the writer's side

When grounding activates in this tab, from either layer:
- The paper goes flat and warm, and motion stops.
- Every word stays where it is, and writing keeps working.
- A fixed, translated line appears at the top: "If things feel like too much right now, you can talk with the companion or reach someone." It offers **Talk now**, **Talk now, bringing this** (only when there's something to bring), **Find help** and **I'm okay**.
- The existing crisis resources card appears.

Grounding is held in the tab's memory ([`src/store/groundingState.ts:5`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/store/groundingState.ts#L5)), so it belongs to this tab, not to every tab of the app *(clarified in draft 4)*.

**Talk now** opens a new Session in its grounded mode, inside the app and without a reload, so grounding is still active when the opener is built.

**Talk now, bringing this** *(changed in draft 4; Fable A8)*. The choice is made in Folio, before leaving:
- Folio first shows the words it would bring, with "These words will be placed in the Talk box, unsent." and **Go** / **Not this**.
- Only **Go** writes the one-time handoff. The Talk composer then holds the words, unsent.
- **What "this" is:** the unit whose check started shore, with its lifted line if it's in an underside. For an L1 hit on a pair of adjacent units (§5.4), both. Never the whole sample window.
- **When nothing is offered:** after a late check (§5.4), and when shore started outside Folio.

Draft 3.1 described the offer in three places three different ways, and its "paragraph that triggered shore" had no meaning for a window, a composition or a late check.

**Find help** shows the crisis resources card again, even if it was dismissed. It's also always in the ••• menu. This needs a small change to `CrisisResources` (Appendix B): today its dismissal is local state that only resets when the card is hidden.

**I'm okay** *(new in draft 4; Fable A3, A4)*. Tapping it, or dismissing the crisis card while in Folio, moves the page to **Quiet**: the shore line goes, and the paper stays flat and still.
- **What it doesn't do.** It doesn't end the shared grounding state. That keeps the companion grounded in this tab and still ends on its own automatic exit. Folio changes only how the page looks. No threshold, timer or activation changes.
- **Why.** Folio has no conversation to absorb a shift in register: the flat page *is* the intervention. A writer who says they're okay is believed about the page. The card's existing "I'm okay" button only hides the card ([`src/components/CrisisResources.tsx:15, 41, 117–131`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/components/CrisisResources.tsx#L15): both its close control and its "I'm okay" button set local state); on a page held flat by shore, that would have promised an exit that didn't exist.
- **Text already seen.** After **I'm okay**, a later activation brings the shore line back only if it comes from text the writer hasn't already been shown shore for in this visit (§5.5). This matters because the floor fires on quoted and persona lines (checked by running it: "some nights I wanted to end my life" and `she said "I want to kill myself"` both match), and it runs again on every settle. Without this, a poem in a dead friend's voice would hold the page in shore for as long as it's being edited.

**Reload.** A reload ends grounding in this tab, and so does the app's own reload after a deploy. Folio mirrors each activation's time to this device, and a page loaded within 30 minutes of one starts in **Quiet**, so motion doesn't come back mid-crisis.

§5.5 is the full, authoritative state table.

### 3.10 Accessibility

**The complete authoring path is the editor itself**, used with a keyboard and with screen readers. Reading view is for reading and export; it isn't the way someone who can't use the visual editor authors.

**Commands.** Every action has a visible control and a keyboard path. The keyboard bindings are provisional until spike S5.

| Action | Control | Keyboard (provisional) |
|---|---|---|
| Lift | button by the selection, or the bottom bar on touch | `Mod+Alt+↓` on an unlifted line |
| Open | crease mark | `Mod+Alt+↓` on a lifted line |
| Close, or Unlift when empty | button | `Esc` inside the underside |
| Closer / Farther | buttons | `Mod+Alt+[` / `Mod+Alt+]` |
| Move between the line, the between and the beneath | — | ordinary arrow keys (the reading order is the document order) |
| Next or previous fold | — | `Mod+Alt+J` / `Mod+Alt+K` |
| Keep, Add, Come back | buttons | focusable, operated with Enter |

**Screen readers.**
- **The crease is a button** with an accessible name: "Open writing beneath" or "Close writing beneath". Voice Control users can say it.
- **Finding folds.** `Mod+Alt+J` and `Mod+Alt+K` move to the next and previous fold and announce "Writing beneath '‹line›', closed" (or "open"). This is the discovery path. Screen readers reading line by line inside an editable region don't reliably announce descriptions on inner spans, so the plan doesn't rely on them *(clarified in draft 4; Fable A11)*.
- An open underside is a group named "Beneath '‹line›', set ‹distance›, ‹n› of 5", with a "Between" group inside it when there's a between.
- Closed undersides are hidden from the accessibility tree, and they're reached through the crease button.
- A polite live region announces:
  - lifting and unlifting
  - distance changes ("Farther: far, 4 of 5")
  - protection events ("Writing beneath a line was kept and opened")
  - an undo that opened a fold instead of changing it ("Opened writing beneath a line. Undo again to change it.")
  - conflicts
  - save status only when it degrades ("Not saved yet…", "Couldn't save…") and once when it recovers ("Saved"). The ordinary flicker between "Saved" and "Saved on this device" while typing isn't announced, so a screen-reader user hears their own words, not the status *(changed in draft 4)*.

**Phase 1 acceptance.** These tasks are scripted and run with the screen curtain on (no visual help) in VoiceOver (macOS and iOS) and NVDA (Windows), and again keyboard-only. That's a developer's test. Phase 3 includes at least two people who use assistive technology every day (§8):
1. write a paragraph
2. lift a line and write beneath it
3. set the distance and write between
4. close the fold, find it again and reopen it
5. unlift an empty fold
6. recover from a delete across a closed fold

**Quiet without hiding.**
- Nothing fades.
- Control text meets 4.5:1 contrast. The crease glyph and focus indicators meet 3:1.
- Contrast is measured on rendered pixels, using axe plus manual checks, on both papers and in shore.
- Focus is always visible.

**Sizes.** Touch targets are 44 px and control text is at least 12 px ([`src/components/Session/cardControls.ts`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/components/Session/cardControls.ts)).

**Motion.** `prefers-reduced-motion` is honored everywhere, and a Folio setting can force it.

**Nothing depends on one sense or skill.**
- The crease is a glyph plus an underline, not only a color.
- There's no sound in the first version.
- Dragging is never the only way to do anything.
- Switch and dwell users rely on key repeat for scanning. K1 and the fence (§5.1) apply only at undersides, betweens and beneaths, so scanning elsewhere is unaffected.

**Find in page.** The browser's find doesn't see closed undersides, because their text isn't in the DOM (§5.1). Reading view shows everything, and the browser can search it.

Draft 2 planned to try `hidden="until-found"`. That needs the hidden text to be present in the DOM, which conflicts with keeping it out of reach of input and extensions. C1 wins. This is a documented limit.

### 3.11 Art direction

**Materials.**
- **Paper:** warm by day; brown-black with pearl text at night.
- **The underside:** a slightly cooler, translucent paper, as if seen through the sheet.
- **The gap:** the same paper as the page, so distance reads as space, not as a box. The faintest darkening sits at the crease end.
- **The crease:** a thin amber edge, with a faint thin-film color that shows only while a fold is opening.
- **Settling:** keeping moves like ink diffusing in water. That describes a motion, never a water theme.

**Tokens.** Folio defines its own tokens, scoped under `.folio-root`. It can't reuse `--bg-primary` and the related tokens, because `useTimeAwarePalette` and `useSeasonalPalette` rewrite them at runtime. The values below are starting points, and contrast is measured on rendered pixels in Phase 1.

Draft 3.1's light crease, `#B9853F`, computed at 2.93:1 on the paper, under its own 3:1 target. `#A8763A` computes at 3.57:1 on the paper and about 3.4:1 on the underside. The thin-film color that shows while a fold opens is never the only cue.

**Typography.** Spectral for writing (every passage the same size) and Inter for controls. Both are already loaded ([`index.html:43`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/index.html#L43)).

**Motion.** Transitions always finish, and nothing waits on an animation before you can act.

**Voice.** Short, specific and plain: "Kept.", not "Saved to your collection ✨"; "Beneath", not "Hidden truth". No exclamation marks.

**Play.** The material has to allow silliness. Absurd distances, a beneath far longer than its line, and a title in the writer's own register ("the soup incident") get the same dignity as anything else. The writer causes the oddness; the app never adds it.

## 4. The prototype study (Phase P)

### 4.1 Why it comes first

Draft 1 tested technical feasibility first and the art last, after weeks of production engineering. This draft reverses the order. The artistic premise is tested with throwaway prototypes before the production editor exists. Phase 0's spikes run in parallel during the study's two-week gap.

### 4.2 The prototypes

**Built outside the app.**
- They live in `prototypes/folio-lab/` *(new)*: one static Vite page that never ships with the app.
- They're excluded from `tsconfig.app.json`, ESLint and the build.
- Storage is local to the device. There are no accounts, AI calls, analytics or network requests.
- They run on a moderator's laptop and phone, and, for participants who agree, on the participant's own phone for the two weeks, so the return is a real one *(draft 4; Fable A10)*. Putting a static page on participants' phones needs hosting. A Firebase preview channel would be an infrastructure decision for the owner (§11).

**Three variants** share one editor shell, typeface and paper. They differ only in the operation:

| Variant | What the writer can do |
|---|---|
| **N: Note** | Select a line and choose **Add note**. A note field opens under the line and toggles with a small icon. This is an ordinary annotation, designed properly, not a strawman. |
| **F: Fold** | Lift a line and write beneath it, with the crease mark and open and close. |
| **A: Fold + Apart** | Everything in F, plus Closer and Farther and the between. |

All three offer a **Come back** slip. F and A also offer **Lift into today's page** from a kept thing (§3.8), so Session 2 can test making with time, not only re-reading.

**Not built:** the safety pipeline, persistence beyond the device, i18n and conflict handling.

**Safety, consent and the participants' writing** *(changed in draft 4; Fable A7)*.
- **Material.** The unprompted prompt asks for something ordinary or made up (§4.3). Nobody is asked to disclose anything difficult.
- **The moderator** follows a script and can stop at any time. The prototype shows a fixed line: "This is a study prototype. You can stop at any time." It sits next to a printed sheet of crisis resources for the participant's region.
- **Consent.** A one-page sheet, signed before Session 1, says:
  - what is kept: the pages made in the prototype, the session recordings and their transcripts
  - who reads them: the moderator and the two coders
  - that everything is deleted within 30 days of the study report, on a date named in the study plan
  - that participants may use invented material, may stop at any time, and may have everything deleted
  - for participants who take the prototype home: that it stores their writing only on their own phone, and that they decide what to show in Session 2
- **Reading order.** The moderator reads nothing a participant wrote until the participant has described it.
- **No production data** is involved, and nothing goes to the app's servers.

### 4.3 Participants and protocol

**Participants.**
- Nine adults who already write for themselves at least weekly: notes, a diary, poems. The premise is about writers, and a general sample would test something else (§11, question 17).
- **Seeds and order** *(draft 4; Fable A10)*. Three seed paragraphs of equal weight about ordinary scenes. Each participant meets each variant once, each with a different seed, in an order given by a Graeco-Latin square: across the nine, each variant meets each seed and each position equally. Draft 3 used one seed three times, so by the third variant participants were repeating themselves, and the "same as a note" test would have measured fatigue.
- **Limit.** Three orders balance position, not the direction of carryover. With nine people that's the best available, and the report says so.
- **The owner** also uses the prototype for two weeks of real writing. That informs taste, such as the distances, but **doesn't count toward any threshold** in §4.4.

**Before session 1.** The rules in §4.4 are pre-registered and committed.

**Session 1 (about 60 minutes).**

1. **Directed (about 10 minutes per variant).**
   - First: "Try the controls on one line." This is a warm-up and is **never scored** *(draft 4)*. In A it requires pressing Closer and Farther, so scoring it would count what everyone was told to do.
   - Then: "Make something with this paragraph that you'd want to keep." This is scored.
   - The moderator never says what any operation means.
2. **Unprompted (15 minutes).** A blank page in whichever variant the participant prefers, with: "Write about something ordinary from this week, or make something up. Use any of the controls, or none."
   - We observe whether they lift anything, how, and for what.
   - Afterwards: "What were you trying to do there?"
3. **Interview,** in this order, so the open questions come before the closed one:
   - "For each thing you kept: what's the relationship between the passages?"
   - "Which felt like taking notes, and which felt like making something?" Asked before any variant is named back to them.
   - "Did either passage feel more true than the other?"
   - "Did anything make you feel interpreted or managed?"
   - Last, as colour only: "Did any version let you make something you wouldn't have made on a normal page?"
4. **Choosing.** Each participant picks one thing they kept to come back to in two weeks.

**Between sessions.** Participants who took the prototype home see the slip on the chosen day if they open it. What they did is shown to the moderator only if they choose to show it.

**Session 2 (two weeks later, 20 to 30 minutes).**
- **Three silent minutes** *(draft 4; Fable A1)*. The prototype is open with the slip, or the kept thing if it was already opened at home. The moderator says nothing. We observe whether the participant opens it, writes beside it, or lifts it into today's page.
- **Then the offer,** only in F or A: "If you want, you can put the line you kept into today's page." We observe whether they write beneath their own two-week-old words.
- **Interview:**
  - "What was it like to meet this again?"
  - "What, if anything, does the arrangement mean to you now?"

### 4.4 What would change the direction

The evidence we want is the one Codex named: *people use the operation to make a relationship between passages that they wouldn't otherwise have made, and can describe that relationship without repeating the product's explanation.*

Draft 2's version of this section described outcomes qualitatively, and every row ended in some form of "proceed" or "redesign". Gemini pointed out that a study like that can't fail. Draft 3 added numbers, but Fable showed that only D1 could stop anything: invited writing in Session 2 would always pass D5, harm led only to a copy fix, D1 counted a yes to a leading question, and D2 counted a task everyone was told to do (A1). Draft 4 rewrites the rules so each one can fail.

**Pre-registration.**
- Before the first session, the owner writes the rules below, with any changes, into `docs/vision/folio-study-1-plan.md` *(new)* and commits it. The plan also names the data deletion date.
- The study report quotes them and is judged against them.
- No threshold changes, and no new measures, after anyone has seen data.
- With nine people these numbers are commitments, not statistics. They exist so the result can't be argued into "proceed".

**Coding.**
- **Two coders** code independently from the transcripts and the kept artifacts, then reconcile. Their agreement before reconciling is reported.
- **Not blind.** Transcripts name the controls (Closer, beneath, note), so coders can't be blind to the variant. Draft 3 claimed they could be "wherever the material allows"; that's dropped. Instead, they code the relationship from the participant's own words, not from the artifact's features.
- **Relationship codes.** *Making* codes: contradiction, joke, question, unresolved, and other relationships the coders agree are made rather than annotated. *Note-like* codes: elaboration and correction, unless the participant's own description shows a made relationship.
- **"The same as a note".** A kept thing from F or A is the same as a note if the participant describes it with note words ("a note", "a comment", "an annotation", "a footnote") or it's coded note-like.
- **What's scored.** Only things made for "Make something you'd want to keep", in the unprompted segment, and in Session 2. Nothing from "Try the controls".

**Decision rules.** These are proposed defaults; the owner sets the final numbers (§11, question 14).

| # | Measure | Result | Then |

| | | fewer | No between in Phase 1 |

| | | fewer | No distance control. `gap` is fixed: at 1 if D2a passed (a between needs room), else at 0 |
| D3 | **Unprompted use.** Count the participants who lift anything during the 15 unprompted minutes, in F or A | 3 or more of 9 | Discovery is adequate |

**One redesign, then stop.** A failed premise allows exactly one redesign and one second study, with no third. "Make the affordance more obvious" counts as the redesign.

**Not counted as success:** time spent, number of lifts, or enjoyment of the visuals.

### 4.5 Size

- **Build:** 5 to 7 days. The three variants share one shell; draft 4 adds three seeds, Lift into today's page and a phone install.
- **Calendar:** about three weeks, including the two-week gap, plus recruiting.

## 5. Contracts

These are normative. The implementation in §6 must satisfy them, and §7 lists the tests that prove it.

### 5.1 C1: Hidden writing

> **Writing the writer can't see is never changed while hidden. Any operation that would change it either leaves it untouched or first makes it visible, and gives the writer a moment to see it.**

The last clause is new in draft 3, from Gemini's R1. Opening a fold and changing it 35 ms later isn't "first making it visible" in any sense a person would recognize.

**Why this needs a contract.** Codex reported three failures, and draft 2 reproduced them against the installed packages with a minimal version of the draft 1 schema:
- A text selection whose ends lie outside a closed underside still contains it. Copying that selection copies the hidden text.
- Delete removes the underside entirely, even with `isolating: true`.
- Splitting a lifted line in the middle puts the same `foldId` in two paragraphs.

Draft 3 reproduced two more (Appendix G.2):
- **Backspace at the edge.** In plain ProseMirror, Backspace at the start of the paragraph after an underside selects the whole underside on the first press and deletes it on the second. Key repeat fires about every 30 to 40 ms, so holding Backspace deletes an entire beneath in under a tenth of a second.
- **The history guard.** A filter that refuses history transactions that would change a closed underside leaves the undo stack intact. The same undo succeeds once the fold is open.

Fable reproduced four more for draft 4: native Cut, Backspace in an empty paragraph beside a closed fold, Backspace at the start of a beneath, and Enter at the end of a lifted line. They're new rows in the table below, and the scripts are in [`folio-repros/`](folio-repros/).

The first reproduction also confirmed the mechanism the safety net relies on: a restore performed in `appendTransaction` joins the triggering edit's undo event, so one undo returns the original document.

**Behavior with closed folds**

| Operation | Behavior |
|---|---|
| A selection that spans a closed fold | Allowed. The crease mark of each closed fold inside it is shown as selected. The underside's text isn't in the DOM, so the browser's native selection can't contain it (layer 1, below). |
| Copy | Copies what's visible. Closed undersides are left out. Open undersides are copied as plain paragraphs in reading order (between, then beneath). Fold marks are stripped. |
| Cut | Copy as above, then Delete as below. |
| Delete, Backspace, typing over, pasting over or dropping onto a spanning selection | The visible text in the range is removed. Every closed underside in the range is kept: it's moved to where the deletion happened, marked loose if its line was deleted, and **opened**. The caret goes to the deletion point, outside the opened underside, and the underside is **fenced** (next rows). A notice offers "Writing beneath a line was kept · Remove it too · Undo". |
| Select All, then Delete | The same: all visible text goes, and closed undersides stay, open, loose and fenced. Deleting a whole page on purpose is **Delete page** in the menu, with a confirmation. |
| Backspace or Delete at the edge of an underside, open or closed *(new)* | The first press selects the underside; a closed one shows its crease as selected. Only a **fresh** press deletes it. A closed one is then handled as in the deletion row above: kept, opened and fenced. **Key repeat never selects, enters or deletes an underside.** |
| Key repeat or a fast burst after a protection event *(new)* | The opened underside is fenced. For 600 ms, and for as long as the key that caused the protection is held, no edit may select, enter, change or delete it. Edits elsewhere go ahead, and typing continues at the caret, which is outside the underside. |
| Undo or redo that would change a closed underside *(changed)* | **Refused while it's closed.** Instead, the press opens the fold, scrolls it into view, and announces "Opened writing beneath a line. Undo again to change it." Nothing in the document or the undo history changes. The next press applies.<br><br>This covers every way an undo arrives:<br>• the keyboard<br>• TipTap's commands<br>• the browser's Edit menu and iOS shake-to-undo, which arrive as `beforeinput` `historyUndo` and are handled inside prosemirror-history (1.5.0, `dist/index.js:388`; Appendix A) |
| Enter inside a lifted line (splitting it) | The fold stays with the fragment containing the start of the lifted text, and the underside follows that fragment. The other fragment becomes ordinary text, and its underline disappears at once, so the change is visible. One undo restores it. A soft break (Shift+Enter) doesn't split the paragraph or affect the fold. |
| Merging two paragraphs | Each fold keeps its line. The undersides re-order after the merged paragraph (I2). |
| A visible edit deletes a whole lifted line | Its underside stays, becomes loose, opens and is fenced, with **Keep it here**, **Turn into page text** and **Remove**. |
| Paste | Never creates folds. Pasted undersides become ordinary paragraphs. |
| Drag and drop of a range containing a closed fold | Treated as cut, then paste. The moved text loses its fold marks. The closed underside stays where it was, opened, loose and fenced. Moving a fold isn't supported in the first version. Gemini's R4 assumed protection would cancel the whole drop. It doesn't: the drop goes ahead. |
| IME composition, autocorrect, spellcheck, OS text replacement, browser extensions *(changed)* | These can act only on text in the DOM, and closed undersides have none there.<br><br>When input starts on a selection that holds a closed fold, the selection is first reduced to its visible text and collapsed (the pre-input collapse, in layer 2 below), so composition always starts on a caret.<br><br>If anything still removes a closed underside's placeholder, the safety net restores the underside and logs `folio.hidden.restored`.<br><br>S1 counts composition glitches separately from changes to hidden writing. Only the second is a C1 failure, but both are reported. |
| Find in page *(changed)* | Closed undersides aren't found. Reading view can be searched (§3.10). |
| Cut from the Edit menu, the context menu, or `Shift+Delete` on Windows *(new in draft 4)* | The same as Cut: the visible text goes to the clipboard, and the deletion follows the Delete row. Layer 2 handles the browser's `cut` event itself, because ProseMirror's own handler deletes the whole selection without consulting any key handler (prosemirror-view 1.41.6, `dist/index.js:3619–3638`). Fable reproduced it in jsdom: a native `cut` removed the closed underside, and only the safety net would have brought it back. |
| Backspace or Delete in an empty paragraph next to a closed underside *(new in draft 4)* | The empty paragraph is removed, and the underside is node-selected, its crease shown as selected. The caret never enters it. Reproduced by Fable: in plain ProseMirror, one Backspace puts the caret at the end of the closed beneath, and the next character typed goes into hidden writing, invisibly. It's a first press, so the key-repeat rule doesn't catch it. |
| Backspace at the start of a beneath, or Delete at the end of a between *(new in draft 4)* | Moves the caret across the boundary and deletes nothing. The between and the beneath can't be selected as whole nodes (`selectable: false`). Reproduced by Fable: in plain ProseMirror the first press node-selects the whole between, and a held key deletes it 30 to 40 ms later. |
| Enter at the end of a lifted line *(new in draft 4)* | The new paragraph goes after the line's undersides, and the caret goes with it. Plain `splitBlock` puts it between the line and its underside, and the integrity plugin would then move the underside, so the caret would appear to jump. Enter at the start of a lifted line puts the new empty paragraph before the line, and the fold stays with the line. |
| Programmatic loads: hydrating a pending entry, adopting a remote revision, resolving a conflict, `setContent` *(new in draft 4)* | The document is replaced in a transaction tagged `folio:load`, outside the undo history. The view's set of closed folds is re-derived from the new document in the same transaction. The safety net ignores it. Without this, loading a revision in which another device deliberately removed an underside would trip the net, bring the underside back, and save it again, undoing the other device's Remove. |

**Enforcement, in four layers.** Draft 2 had layers 2 and 4. Layers 1 and 3 are new.

1. **Structure.** A closed underside renders as an empty, zero-height `contenteditable="false"` placeholder.
   - It's a node view with no `contentDOM`, so ProseMirror leaves the node's children to the node view, which renders none.
   - Its text exists only in the editor state. Opening the fold recreates the node view with its content.
   - Nothing that works on the DOM can reach text that isn't there: native input, IME, spellcheck, autocorrect, OS text replacement, and Grammarly-style extensions.
   - This replaces draft 2's `display: none` decoration, which left the hidden text in the DOM.
   - **A selection never rests inside a closed underside** *(3.1; made precise in draft 4)*. A command can place one there in the editor state, for example `joinBackward` after Backspace in an empty paragraph. ProseMirror doesn't stop it: Fable's jsdom run drew the caret on the empty placeholder, and an inserted character went into the hidden beneath.
     - The remap is an `appendTransaction` in `foldView` that runs on **every** transaction whose selection ends inside a closed underside, so it's applied before the next keystroke can be read. A correction made later, in the view, could lose that race.
     - It maps the selection to a node selection of the whole underside when the transaction came from Backspace or Delete beside it, and otherwise to the nearest text position outside it.
     - Changing only the selection is safe: it changes no text and adds no undo step.
     - S1's first case tests caret movement across the placeholders.
2. **Input guards.** Folio's commands handle keyboard deletion, cut, copy, paste, drop and typing over a selection (`handleKeyDown`, `handleTextInput`, `handlePaste`, `handleDrop`, and `handleDOMEvents` for `cut` and `copy`). They:
   - compute the visible ranges, delete only those, and tag the transaction `folio:visibleEdit`
   - cover **every** deletion binding, not only Backspace and Delete (K4)
   - apply the key-repeat rule (`event.repeat`) and the fence
   - run the **pre-input collapse**. On `beforeinput`, `compositionstart` and printable `keydown`, a selection that holds a closed fold is reduced to its visible text and collapsed *before* the browser acts.
     - On Android, where `keydown` reports key code 229, `beforeinput` and `compositionstart` are the hooks.
     - Nobody knows yet whether every browser honors a selection change made during `compositionstart`. S1 tests it on each device, and layer 4 covers the browsers that don't.
3. **The history guard.** A `filterTransaction` refuses any history transaction (`isHistoryTransaction`) that would change a closed underside, and asks the view to open it.
   - Refusing is safe here because history transactions are made by the program; they're never read back from the DOM.
   - Gemini warned (R2) that filtering transactions derived from the DOM during composition desynchronizes the editor. That's why the guard is limited to history transactions.
   - **The follow-up open is deferred** with `queueMicrotask` *(draft 4; Fable B16)*. `filterTransaction` runs inside the dispatch it filters; a nested `view.dispatch` there would be overwritten when the outer one finishes, and the fold would stay closed.
4. **The safety net.** The `hiddenWriting` plugin's `appendTransaction` compares the closed undersides before and after every transaction that changes the document.
   - It compares each closed underside's **content** (`content.eq`) and its `foldId`, not the whole node *(draft 4; Fable B16)*. A whole-node comparison would see an attribute fix, such as the `pid` fixer re-assigning an id, as a change. The net would restore it, the fixer would fix it again, and `appendTransaction` would loop.
   - If a transaction tagged neither `folio:visibleEdit` nor `folio:load` removed or changed one, the plugin restores it, loose and open, in the same undo event, and logs `folio.hidden.restored`.
   - After layers 1 to 3 it should never fire. S1 and production watch for it.
   - It ignores history transactions: layer 3 has already refused any that touch a closed underside. Without that exemption, a restore appended to an undo would land on the redo stack (Appendix F.9, the `appended.getMeta(historyKey)` branch), and undo would appear to do nothing.

**Hard requirement.** On every device in the matrix (§6.12), spike S1 must show **zero** cases where hidden writing is lost or changed by any operation in the table.
- One reproducible case rejects the design until it's fixed.
- If architecture A can't meet the requirement, architecture B is evaluated against the same table.
- This replaces draft 1's rule of switching only if two cases failed.
- Composition glitches near folds are reported separately. They aren't C1 failures, but they block Phase 1 if they lose or duplicate visible text.

**Input rules**, enforced by layer 2 and each unit-tested with synthetic key events:

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| K1 | Key repeat (`event.repeat === true`) never selects, enters or deletes an underside, a between or a beneath *(draft 4 adds the last two; Fable B7)*. |
| K2 | A fenced underside can't be selected, entered, changed or deleted until its fence lifts: 600 ms after the protection event and after the triggering key is released. It also lifts at once on `blur` or when the tab is hidden, and it never lasts more than 3 seconds, because a `keyup` lost when focus leaves the window would otherwise hold it forever *(draft 4)*. |
| K3 | After a protection event the caret is outside the opened underside. |
| K4 | Every deletion binding goes through layer 2 *(new in draft 4; Fable B15)*. That's the full set in prosemirror-commands 1.7.1's `pcBaseKeymap` and `macBaseKeymap`: Backspace, Delete, `Mod-Backspace`, `Mod-Delete`, `Shift-Backspace`, `Alt-Backspace`, `Alt-Delete`, `Ctrl-Alt-Backspace`, `Ctrl-h`, `Ctrl-d` and `Alt-d`, plus `Shift+Delete` as cut on Windows. Each chains to the same delete, join and select-node commands, so each can reach an underside. |

**Invariants**, enforced in `appendTransaction` and each unit-tested:

| # | Invariant |
|---|---|
| I1 | Every `fold` mark's `foldId` has exactly one underside with that id. |
| I2 | Each underside sits directly after the paragraph containing its mark, ordered among that paragraph's undersides by mark position. |

| I7 | A between exists only at distance 1 or more, and the distance can't drop to 0 while the between has text. |

Draft 1's I6 ("a closed empty underside is removed") is gone: closing never edits, and empty folds are handled by Unlift (§3.5).

### 5.2 C2: Storage without silent loss

**Guarantees**

1. **Concurrent edits.** Two devices or tabs editing the same page never silently overwrite each other. If both changed it, both versions survive and the writer is told.
2. **Offline edits.** Editing offline survives a reload if the device could store it. The status says honestly whether it could.
3. **Deletion.** A page deleted on one device is never recreated by another device reconnecting. That device's unsaved words are offered as a new page.
4. **Statuses.** "Saved" means the server has acknowledged exactly the words on screen. "Saved on this device" means Folio's own store on the device holds them.

**The model**

- **A chain of revisions.**
  - Every save has a new random `revId`.
  - The page document carries its `revId` and its `parentRevId`, the revision it was built on.
- **Compare-and-swap in the security rules.**
  - An update is accepted only if `request.resource.data.parentRevId == resource.data.revId`.
  - A create is accepted only with `parentRevId == null`.
  - So a write built on a stale revision is rejected by the server, even if it was queued offline. A rejected write never touches the server's copy.
  - Because unique ids are chained (rather than a counter being incremented), a second offline write that was built on the first rejected one is rejected too. A counter would let it through.
- **A store of pending saves on the device.**
  - Before each server write, the controller saves `{ baseRevId, sentRevIds, doc, checked }` to localStorage and records whether that local write succeeded, as `writeJournalDraft` already does ([`src/store/journalDrafts.ts:62–81`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/store/journalDrafts.ts#L62-L81)).
  - **Each editor has its own entry** *(changed)*. The key is `us.folioPending.<uid>.<pageId>.<editorId>`, and `editorId` is random for each mounted editor, so for each tab. Draft 2 keyed it by page, which let a background tab's `pagehide` flush overwrite another tab's unsaved entry before compare-and-swap could see either (Gemini, R6).
  - localStorage rather than IndexedDB, because the flush on `pagehide` has to be synchronous. Its quota is small. A failed write shows the honest status, and S4 outcome 6 tests that.
  - **The chain** *(draft 4; Fable B1)*. `sentRevIds` is every revision this editor has sent since its base, acknowledged or not. Together with `baseRevId` it's the editor's chain, and classification depends on it.
  - **The checked set travels with the words** *(draft 4; Fable B10)*. `checked` is the page's checked set for exactly this content (§5.4). A resolution commit or a conflict copy carries it, so a fully checked page isn't checked again from scratch, and an old unit can't start shore just because its record was lost. The `pid`s don't change in a copy, so the keys stay valid.
  - An entry is cleared only when the server acknowledges the revision that matches its editor's content.
- **Acknowledgements arrive in order.** The Firestore SDK sends each queued write as its own batch, in order, and doesn't merge writes to the same document. This was checked in the SDK source and on the emulator (Appendix I). If revision 12 is acknowledged while revision 13 is pending, the status stays "Saved on this device", and the store keeps 13.
- **A rejection is classified before anything else happens, once per chain** *(changed in draft 4)*.
  - **Which rejections.** Every rejected write, whatever its code. The rules return `permission-denied` for a stale parent and for any other failed clause, and a document over Firestore's own 1 MiB limit comes back `invalid-argument` (Fable's probe, R6). The SDK retries transient failures itself; a write's promise rejects only on a permanent error. Draft 3.1 named only `permission-denied` (Fable B11).
  - **A write that landed can still be rejected** *(new in draft 4; Fable B1)*. After a dropped connection, the SDK re-sends every unacknowledged write, and it sends no resume token (`writeHandshake()`: "TODO(dimond): Support stream resumption", @firebase/firestore 4.14.1, `common-b12d6a5a.node.mjs:25015–25021`; the pipeline is re-sent at `:25924–25930`). If the first send had already been committed, the re-send fails the compare-and-swap, and the server holds that write's *own* revision. Fable's emulator probe reproduced it (R3, re-run for this draft). Draft 3.1's table would have called it a conflict and made a copy of the writer's own words. Whether the production backend deduplicates a re-sent write is unknown; the classification below doesn't depend on it.
  - **One conflict, many rejections** *(new in draft 4; Fable B2)*. Every queued write built on a stale parent is rejected, so a conflict after three offline saves produces three rejections (Fable's probe, R4). Draft 3.1 classified each one, and would have made three identical copies.
  - **The procedure.** On the first rejection, the controller stops sending, keeps the editor content, waits until the SDK has finished with this page's queue (no pending writes for it), then reads the page once with `getDocFromServer` and classifies the whole chain. Later rejections from the same chain are logged and ignored.

  | The server's copy, after the queue drains | Meaning | Result |
  |---|---|---|
  | Gone | A remote delete | The remote-delete prompt (below) |
  | `revId` is outside this editor's chain | Another device or tab wrote: a real conflict | One conflict copy (below) |
  | `revId` is in this editor's chain: its base, or any revision it sent | No other device has written. Everything up to that revision landed, even if an acknowledgement was lost | Every revision up to it counts as acknowledged. If the editor's content is newer, it's sent once as a new revision on top of the server's. If that write is rejected while the server still holds the same revision, the write itself breaks some other rule: "Couldn't save to your account. Your words are on this device.", retried with backoff and on the next edit, logged as `folio.save.rejectedNotConflict`. **Never** a conflict copy |
  | Can't be read (offline; `getDocFromServer` rejects with `unavailable`) | Unknown | The status stays as it was; classification runs again when the device is back online |

- **A conflict.**
  - The server's version stays as the page.
  - This device's version is saved from its pending entry, with its checked set, as a new page, "‹first line› (this device, 15 Oct 14:02)".
  - **The editor moves to the new page** *(draft 4; Fable B9)*, so the writer's own words stay under their caret. The notice says so: "This page changed on another device. Nothing was lost: you're now writing in this device's version, as a separate page."
  - Nothing is ever merged.
- **A remote delete.**
  - If the page no longer exists, an update arrives as a create that carries a parent, and it's rejected. The emulator confirmed this. The snapshot also shows the document gone.
  - The writer sees: "This page was deleted on another device. Your recent words are still here." with **Keep as a new page** and **Discard**.
  - The page is never recreated automatically.
- **Every move to editable** *(changed in draft 4)*, if this device has orphaned pending entries for the page. That's the first load, and the moment a waiting tab is granted the lock because the holder closed or crashed. Draft 3.1 specified only the first load (Fable B9):
  - **Orphaned entries only** *(3.1)*. An entry is orphaned when the editor that wrote it no longer exists, because its tab closed or reloaded. Each mounted editor holds a Web Lock named `folio-editor:<editorId>` for its whole life, and `navigator.locks.query()` shows which editors are alive. Only the tab that holds the page's edit lock resolves entries, and it never touches a live editor's entry. Without the Web Locks API, a `BroadcastChannel` ping stands in: an entry whose editor doesn't answer within 500 ms is orphaned.
  - **Server-confirmed state.** Folio waits for a snapshot that is server-confirmed: `fromCache === false` and `hasPendingWrites === false`.
  - **Why wait.** With a persistent cache, the SDK keeps its own queue of writes across reloads and replays it after a reload. The cached page then shows this device's own queued revision. Comparing a pending entry against that cached revision, or writing again before the queue drains, would make a false conflict copy of the writer's own words. Waiting until there are no pending writes means the SDK's queue for this page has been sent and answered first.
  - **Nothing is sent before confirmation** *(3.1)*. The editor adopts the newest orphaned entry: it moves that entry's content and chain into its own entry. The writer can keep writing, and every save goes only to that entry, with the status "Saved on this device". Online, confirmation normally takes under a second. Offline, it waits for the network. A write chained onto an unconfirmed revision could be rejected for a reason that isn't a conflict: with the memory cache (outcome 7), the SDK's queue is lost on reload and that revision never reaches the server.
  - **Once state is confirmed,** the adopted entry is resolved first, because its content is what the writer sees, and then any other orphaned entries, newest first. Each is resolved on its own:

  | The server-confirmed page | Result |
  |---|---|
  | `revId` is the entry's base or one of its pending revisions | No other device has written since. If the server's content equals the entry's, the entry is cleared. Otherwise the entry's content is committed as a new revision whose parent is the server's `revId` (compare-and-swap) |
  | `revId` is anything else | A conflict copy |
  | Gone | The remote-delete prompt |

  Draft 3 cleared an entry as "already saved" whenever the server held its last pending revision. An adopted entry can hold newer words than that, so equality of content now decides. When two entries share a base, the first commits, and the second then sees a revision outside its chain and becomes a conflict copy. Nothing is lost.

- **Two tabs** *(changed in draft 4)*.
  - **The lock.** Editing a page takes a real lock: `navigator.locks.request('folio:<uid>:<pageId>', …)`, held while the editor is editable.
  - **A second tab** queues its own request for the lock and is read-only while it waits: "This page is open in another tab. You can write here once it's closed there."
  - **When the first tab closes or crashes,** the browser grants the lock to the waiting tab, which runs the move-to-editable procedure above and then allows editing.
  - **No handover.** Draft 3's **Edit here** asked the holder to flush and release over `BroadcastChannel`. A queued lock gives the same result with no protocol, so it's dropped (Fable A9). If a writer needs it, it can come back after Phase 3.
  - **Without the Web Locks API,** Folio falls back to draft 2's `BroadcastChannel` courtesy lock.
  - **In every case,** correctness comes from compare-and-swap and from the separate pending entries.
- **Statuses**

  | Status | Shown when |
  |---|---|
  | Saved | The server has acknowledged the current content |
  | Saved on this device | The pending store holds the current content, and the server hasn't acknowledged it yet |
  | Not saved yet. Keep this tab open. · Copy page | The pending store couldn't be written, and the server hasn't acknowledged the content |
  | Couldn't save to your account. Your words are on this device. | The server rejected the write, classification found it wasn't a conflict, and the pending store holds the content |

  Firestore's own persistent cache may hold a queued write too. Folio claims local safety only from its own store.
- **Size guard.**
  - At 400 KB of serialized document, the app warns: "This page is getting long. A new page might be easier."
  - At 900 KB it refuses Lift and paste.
  - Firestore's limit is 1 MiB per document.
  - **Measured in UTF-8 bytes of the whole page document, not in characters** *(clarified)*. A page of Japanese or Persian text takes two or three bytes a character, so draft 2's character cap in the rules (950,000) could pass a document twice the size limit. The rule's cap stays only as a backstop.
  - **The whole document includes the checked set** (§5.4) *(3.1, corrected in draft 4)*. Empty and whitespace-only paragraphs aren't units and have no key (§5.4). So every unit has at least one character and costs at least about 105 bytes: roughly 85 of paragraph JSON with its `pid`, plus a key of about 19 bytes. A page reaches 900 KB before it can hold the rule's backstop of 12,000 keys, and the cap can't block an ordinary save. Draft 3.1 keyed empty paragraphs too, at about 71 bytes each, and a poet who spaces stanzas with blank paragraphs could have hit the cap first (Fable B12).
  - A write over the limit is rejected by the server, and the classification above reports it as "Couldn't save to your account", not as a conflict.

**Spike S4: mandatory outcomes.** Phase 0 can't exit until all thirteen pass as automated tests. Outcomes 1 to 9, 12 and 13 use the emulator with two SDK instances and `disableNetwork`. Outcomes 10 and 11, and the browser form of 12, need a real browser, because Node has no IndexedDB. The repository has no browser test harness yet, so building one against the emulator is part of S4's cost.

1. **Concurrent writes, online.** Both clients write from the same parent: one is accepted, the other is rejected and becomes a conflict copy. No words are lost.
2. **Concurrent writes, offline.** The same, with both clients offline and then reconnecting, in either order.
3. **Delayed acknowledgement.** The acknowledgement of revision N arrives after revision N+1 was typed, and the status and store stay correct.
4. **Rejected chain.** After the first rejection, the rest of a chain of queued offline writes is rejected and none is applied. *(Server side verified, Appendix I.)*
5. **Edit versus delete.** An offline edit, a remote delete, then reconnect: the page isn't recreated and the words are offered as a new page. *(Server side verified, Appendix I.)*
6. **localStorage unavailable** (or over quota). The status never says "Saved on this device".
7. **IndexedDB unavailable.** Firestore falls back to its memory cache; after an offline reload, the behavior matches what the status claimed.
8. **Reload in the middle of a chain.** Each pending entry is committed, or becomes a conflict copy, correctly.
9. **A rejection that isn't a conflict** *(new)*. An oversized page whose parent matches is rejected, classified as not a conflict, and makes no conflict copy. Both forms: over the rule's cap (`permission-denied`) and over Firestore's 1 MiB (`invalid-argument`). *(Both codes verified: Appendix I, and Fable's probe R6.)*
10. **Reload with the SDK's own queue** *(new; browser, persistent cache)*. Offline edits, a reload while still offline, more edits after the reload, then reconnect. No false conflict copy, and the page ends at this device's latest content. The same sequence runs with IndexedDB unavailable (memory cache), where the SDK's queue is lost on reload.
11. **Two tabs** *(new; browser)*. A background tab's `pagehide` flush never overwrites another tab's pending entry. A newly opened tab never resolves or commits the entry of a tab that is still open *(3.1)*. When the first tab closes or crashes, the waiting tab becomes editable and nothing is lost *(draft 4)*.
12. **A write that landed, sent again** *(new in draft 4)*. The emulator form sends the identical revision twice, as Fable's probe R3 does; the browser form cuts the network between the send and the acknowledgement. No conflict copy is made, and the status reaches "Saved". *(The server side is verified: Fable's probe R3.)*
13. **One conflict, several queued writes** *(new in draft 4)*. Three offline saves, a remote write, then reconnect: exactly one conflict copy, holding the latest content. *(The three rejections are verified: Fable's probe R4.)*

**The two SDK assumptions are verified** on the emulator with Firebase JS SDK 12.13.0 (Appendix I). Draft 2 named them as open:
- A rejected queued write doesn't stop later queued writes from being sent.
- A rejected write's latency-compensated value is rolled back locally.

Both must be checked again with the persistent, multi-tab cache (outcomes 10 and 11), and again on every major SDK upgrade.

### 5.3 C3: Kept things

Draft 1 claimed traces were unchangeable. Codex showed that its rules still allowed deleting a trace and creating a new one at the same id with different content. This draft deliberately chooses **snapshots the app never rewrites** over **identities that can never be reused**:

- **No editing.** No code path in the app edits a kept composition or an added entry. The rules reject updates to kept fields, which guards against bugs in the app.
- **Deleting.** The writer can always delete a trace and everything added to it.
- **Undo.** For 10 seconds after Keep or Add, Undo deletes what was just created.
- **Correction.** A corrected copy is a new trace. The original stays unless the writer deletes it.
- **Identity.** Trace ids are random, and the app never reuses one. Folio doesn't stop a client outside the app from recreating a deleted id, and nothing in Folio depends on that.
- **Copy.** It never says "permanent", "forever", "unchangeable" or "sealed" (enforced by the copy test in §3.12).

### 5.4 C4: Safety coverage

**Goals.**
- Folio notices distress at least as reliably as the Journal in Sessions.
- It never treats an unavailable check as clear.
- It doesn't change the shared classifier's calibration.

**Units and samples.**
- **Units.** Every paragraph in the page, in a between and in a beneath has a stable paragraph id (`pid`), maintained by the editor. A paragraph with at least one visible character is a unit. Empty and whitespace-only paragraphs aren't units: they're not sampled and have no key *(draft 4; Fable B12)*. Their blank space still appears in the windows of their neighbours.
- **Reading order.** The order Reading view uses. Page paragraphs come in order. After the paragraph holding a lifted line comes its underside: the between's paragraphs, then the beneath's. Closed undersides are included: safety covers all writing, seen or not.
- **Samples** *(changed)*. A unit's sample is a window of reading-order text, at most 1,000 characters, that contains the whole unit.
  - The window is filled first with the text before the unit, as the Journal's `slice(-1000)` does, then with the text after it if room remains.
  - A unit in a between or a beneath always starts with its lifted line (at most 240 characters, with the middle trimmed), even when the line falls outside the window. The trimmed form is what's sent and what's hashed into the key *(draft 4; Fable B21)*.
- **Why the change.**
  - The Journal sends the last 1,000 characters of the whole text, so its classifier sees the paragraphs before the newest one (Appendix F.3, re-read for this draft). Draft 2's samples were one paragraph plus the lifted line. They had lost that context, which contradicted draft 2's own goal of matching the Journal.
  - Gemini's R9 asked for the line, the between and the beneath to be read together. Reading-order windows do that, and also restore the Journal's context on the page.
  - Filling with the text after the unit covers a pair written out of order, such as a sentence inserted above one already checked.
- **Budget.** A sample is at most 1,000 characters, the classifier's existing window (`text.slice(-1000)`, [`src/ai/openrouter.ts:476`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/ai/openrouter.ts#L476)). Folio doesn't change the window the classifier was tuned on. A unit longer than the budget is split into overlapping windows (150 characters of overlap), each a separate sample that starts with the line, if there is one. A long paste is covered end to end.
- **What remains uncovered.** Two passages more than about 1,000 characters apart, whose risk appears only when they're read together, are never read together. The Journal has the same limit.

**Coverage is defined by content** *(new)*. Draft 2 tracked coverage through events: settles, and keys stored on one device. Gemini's R8 showed three ways to write text that's never checked under that design:
- the writer adds to a paragraph while its check is in flight, and the early result is recorded for the whole paragraph
- the tab closes before the paragraph settles
- an offline save is replayed later, outside the editor's events

Draft 3 defines coverage by the text itself:
- **The check key.** Each unit's check key is a hash of its `pid`, its lifted line (for units in an underside, in its trimmed form) and its own text: SHA-256, truncated to 16 hex characters. Context isn't part of the key.
  - **Why the `pid`** *(3.1)*. With text alone, the same words written a second time, in a darker place, would count as already checked. A refrain is exactly the kind of text a writer repeats. With the `pid`, each place is checked. A `pid` is new on a split or a paste, so moved text is checked where it lands.
- **The checked set.** The page carries a set of keys whose samples were all *classified* (risk 0, 1 or 2).
  - It's saved in the page document, as `safety.checked`, as part of ordinary saves, including the flush when the page closes. It never triggers a save of its own.
  - If a result arrives after the last save, the unit is checked again on the next open. That costs an extra call, never a miss.
- **Unchecked means not in the set.** A unit is unchecked when its current key isn't in the set. Every unchecked unit is compared and scheduled:
  - on open
  - after pending entries are hydrated
  - whenever a remote revision arrives

  This works on any device.
- **Only the tab that holds the page's lock runs checks** *(draft 4; Fable B14)*. A read-only tab can't save results, so it would repeat every call on every open and double the traffic. Its text is unchanged; it's checked by the holder, or when this tab becomes editable.
- **A result counts only for the text that was sent.** It's recorded only if the unit's key is still the key it was sent with. Otherwise the unit is scheduled again.
- **Context never triggers a re-check.** Editing paragraph 3 doesn't re-check paragraph 4, because paragraph 3's own window reads paragraph 4 as text after it. This keeps the number of calls bounded.
- **What it replaces.** The checked set replaces draft 2's per-device store of unchecked keys, and works across devices.
- **Traces too** *(3.1, Phase 2)*. A trace carries its own checked set, `safety.checked`. A trace stores plain text rather than paragraphs, so its units are its between, its beneath and each added entry, and the key uses a fixed name (`between`, `beneath`) or the entry's id in place of a `pid`.
  - On Keep, the between or the beneath is recorded as checked in the same write if every page paragraph it came from is in the page's checked set at that moment. Otherwise it's scheduled.
  - Unchecked trace units are swept when the trace or the Kept view opens.
  - Without this, a kept composition or an added entry was checked once, in the open tab, with no record of the result. A tab closed during the check, or a page edited before its own check finished, would have left kept words unchecked for good.

**When checks run**

| Trigger | L1 (`detectCrisisKeywords`) | L2 (classifier) |
|---|---|---|
| A unit settles (2 s without typing in it, or the caret leaves it) | On the unit, and on the unit joined with each neighbour in reading order, immediately | Scheduled if unchecked |
| The page is hidden or left (`pagehide`, `visibilitychange` to hidden, route change) *(new)* | The same, on every unit changed since it last settled, synchronously. A hit activates grounding at once; the write of `lastGroundingAt` that follows is best effort and may not leave a closing tab, as in Sessions today | Can't finish in time. The unit stays out of the saved set and is checked on the next open, on any device. |
| Paste | On all the pasted text, immediately | Every affected unit is scheduled |
| Opening a page, hydrating pending entries, or receiving a remote revision *(new)* | On every unchecked unit | Every unchecked unit is scheduled |
| Keep, Add, or a corrected copy | On the new text | Scheduled, unless its key is already in the trace's checked set (see Traces too) |
| Opening a trace or the Kept view *(3.1)* | On every unchecked trace unit | Every unchecked trace unit is scheduled |

**L1 on pairs** *(draft 4; Fable B13)*. The Journal runs L1 on its whole text ([`src/engine/partOrchestrator.ts:557–559`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/engine/partOrchestrator.ts#L557-L559)). Draft 3.1 ran it on each unit alone, so a sentence split by Enter in the middle would have matched neither half. Running it on each unit and on each adjacent pair costs nothing, because it's a regex, and is never weaker than the Journal for a sentence split across a break. An activation's *trigger* is the unit that matched alone or, if only a pair matched, the pair. §3.9 and §5.5 use the trigger to decide whether the shore line comes back.

**Late checks** *(changed in draft 4; the owner decides, §11 question 15)*.
- **When.** A page may hold text that was never checked by L2, because it was written offline and the tab closed before the text settled. L1 ran on it when the page was hidden. Opening that page later checks it with L2.
- **The problem with draft 3.1's answer** (Fable A2). Draft 3.1 started shore on any risk-2 result, as for fresh writing. But a late check can fire on a paragraph written weeks ago, while the writer is writing about something else. The page flattens for a reason they can't see. `lastGroundingAt` is stamped, so for 72 hours the companion in SMS and Sessions answers in its grounded register and proactive texts are cancelled. Come-backs are held. Sessions never faces this case, because it checks at write time, so "just as it covers Sessions" doesn't settle it.
- **Proposed rule** (recommended):
  - **Always check, and always record** the result in the checked set.
  - **The page was edited within the last 72 hours** (its `updatedAt` when it was opened): a risk-2 result starts shore as usual. Anything written recently gets the full response.
  - **Older:** the crisis resources card appears, the plain door, without flattening the page, without the shore line and without stamping `lastGroundingAt`. A code is logged, `folio.l2.lateRisk`, so §8 can count it. Nothing is offered to bring to Talk (§3.9).
- **The alternative** is draft 3.1's: start shore on every risk-2 result. Fable's engineering reviewer preferred it as the simpler rule. The art reviewer showed its cost on old pages.
- **Order.** The sweep starts only after the Recent overlay has been read, so the page's first presentation already accounts for a recent grounding, and a late result can't flash the page from Normal to Shore before Recent is known (Fable B21).

**The L2 scheduler.**
- **Slots.** Pending samples are held in a map keyed by `pid` and window index. A new revision of the same unit replaces that unit's pending samples. Different units never replace each other, which fixes draft 1's "newest text wins".
- **Order.** Oldest first, by when each unit first settled, each with its latest text.
- **Rate** *(changed in draft 4; Fable B6)*. One call in flight. Checks for settles, while the writer is typing, start at least 6 seconds apart: at most 10 a minute. Sweeps and pastes are at least 10 seconds apart: 6 a minute.
- **A separate limit on the server** *(new in draft 4; Fable B6)*. Today the chat function has one limiter for every task, 30 a minute per user ([`functions/src/chat.ts:182`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/functions/src/chat.ts#L182)), and a tripped limit returns 429 to all of them (`:287–290`). `classifyCrisisRisk` reads any non-OK response as risk 0 (Appendix F.1). So a Folio sweep in one tab, plus a Talk turn with its autocorrect and signal checks in another, could trip the limit and make Talk's independent safety check fail open for that turn. That would change Sessions, which §6.1 forbids.
  - The fix: the pinned classifier routes (`crisis_check`, `signal_check`) get their own limiter, for example 20 a minute per user, so classifier traffic never uses the therapist's budget, and the therapist's never starves the classifier.
  - A tripped limit returns a machine-readable `rate_limited`, which `checkCrisisRisk` reports as an unavailable cause and backs off from for longer.
  - This is a change outside `src/folio/` (Appendix B) and an owner decision (§11, question 16). It makes Sessions' own backstop safer, too.
- **Deduplication.** A unit whose key is in the checked set isn't scheduled.
- **Short text** *(3.1)*. A sample under 20 characters isn't sent to L2. The number is the Journal's floor, which it applies to its whole text ([`src/engine/partOrchestrator.ts:202`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/engine/partOrchestrator.ts#L202)). L1 still runs, and the key enters the checked set once it has. The Journal runs nothing under its floor, not even L1, so Folio is stricter here.
  - The floor applies to the sample, which carries context, not to the unit. So a short unit is skipped only when the text around it is short too, which in practice means a nearly empty page.
  - Draft 3 skipped every unit under 12 characters. A beneath of "soon." under a heavy line would never have been read with that line, though the Journal reads a short addition together with what came before it.
- **Backlog.** Nothing is ever dropped. The queue drains at the rate limit; L1 has already covered all the text. While more than 30 samples are pending, a code-only diagnostic (`folio.l2.backlog`) is logged. A 100 KB paste of short paragraphs would take about 20 minutes to drain.
  - **Considered, not adopted yet** (Fable B20): letting a classified window mark every unit wholly inside it as checked. It would cut a long paste's calls several times over. But the classifier was tuned on windows whose newest text is at the end, so S6 must first show that text in the middle of a window is recalled as well.
- **Results.**
  - **Classified, risk 2:** `activateGrounding('auto')`, except for a late check on an older page (above). The key is added to the checked set.
  - **Classified, risk 0 or 1:** the key is added to the checked set once every window of the unit has been classified with that same key.
  - **Unavailable** (timeout, HTTP error, malformed response or no network): the sample stays pending and is retried after 30 seconds, 2 minutes and 10 minutes, then on the `online` event or the next settle. It never enters the checked set.
  - **Stale:** the unit's key changed while the call was in flight. The result is discarded and the unit is scheduled again.
- **Not checked:**
  - text whose key is already in the checked set
  - Reading view and export, which add nothing new to check

**The Ask contract.** The margin is a later hypothesis (§9, H2), but its contract is fixed now so it can't drift. It's **race the generation, gate the display**:
1. L1 runs on the selection. A hit activates grounding, and no request is sent.
2. The L2 check and the reply start together. The reply streams into a buffer that isn't shown.
3. The reply is shown only when L2 is *classified* with risk below 2, or *unavailable* (the shared fail-open).
4. If L2 returns risk 2:
   - **Before the reply is shown:** the reply is aborted and discarded, grounding activates, and a new reply is requested, built grounded.
   - **After it's shown:** grounding activates, and the note stays, because it was generated under `SAFETY_RULES`.
5. Grounding is read at call time and again before display, never from render-time state.

**Deferred display.** This applies only to later hypotheses that hold generated text to show later. Holding requires L1 to be clear and L2 to be *classified* with risk below 2. An unavailable check stays pending and is never treated as clear. This is a Folio scheduling rule, not a new threshold: grounding still activates exactly on an L1 hit or an L2 risk of 2.

**A pipeline eval before Phase 1.**

  - **pairs whose risk appears only together**, such as "It was a normal Tuesday." with "I've decided how I'll do it." beneath it
  - *(new)* a line, a between and a beneath that carry risk only as a composition

- **Reports.**

  - The false-activation rate on the ordinary, literary and dark fixtures, with L1's and L2's reported separately *(draft 4)*. ### 5.5 C5: States

This table is the authoritative timeline. Any other description in this document defers to it.

**The states**

- **Normal.**
- **Shore.** Grounding is active in this tab and the writer hasn't said **I'm okay**. Grounding is the shared state, set by either layer on any surface, and it's held in the tab's memory ([`src/store/groundingState.ts:5`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/store/groundingState.ts#L5)). So it's per tab: Sessions grounded in one tab doesn't flatten Folio in another; only Recent crosses tabs.
  - **It ends** through the shared automatic exit, 5 minutes after the last trigger; when the writer says **I'm okay**, which moves the page to Quiet; or when the tab reloads or is replaced, including the app's own reload after a deploy *(draft 4; Fable A3)*. Draft 3.1 said the automatic exit was the only way, which was false.
- **Quiet.** Calm materials without the shore line. A page is in Quiet when:
  - shore has ended and the visit hasn't
  - the writer said **I'm okay** during shore (the shared grounding state may still be active, and the companion in this tab stays grounded until it ends)
  - the page was loaded within 30 minutes of an activation on this device. Folio mirrors each activation's time to localStorage, because after a reload the shared state is gone and Firestore's copy may not have arrived.

  A visit ends when Folio reloads (unless the 30-minute rule applies), or when the tab has been hidden for 30 minutes. Going to Sessions and back within a visit stays quiet.
- **Recent.** An overlay that applies while less than 72 hours have passed since `lastGroundingAt`. That value is persisted, so it covers every surface and device. Recent can apply together with any of the states above. Its reader is `safety/recent.ts`. The write behind it is throttled to one every 30 seconds and best effort ([`src/store/groundingPersistence.ts:17–36`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/store/groundingPersistence.ts#L17-L36)), so another device may see Recent late, or, if every write failed offline, not at all. The local mirror covers this device.

**The shore line comes back only for new text** *(new in draft 4; Fable A4)*. While the page is in Quiet within a visit, an activation brings back Shore, with its line, only if its trigger (§5.4: the unit, or the pair, that matched or was classified) hasn't already started shore in this visit. Otherwise the shared state is re-activated as usual, which keeps the companion grounded and restarts its timer, and the page stays in Quiet. No activation is suppressed: only the page's presentation is spared repeating itself.

Why it's needed: L1 runs again on every settle, and it fires on quoted and persona lines (checked by running `detectCrisisKeywords`: "some nights I wanted to end my life", `she said "I want to kill myself" and laughed`, and "In the poem the narrator says: I will hang myself tomorrow" all match). Without this rule, a poem in someone else's voice would hold the page in shore for as long as it's being edited, plus five minutes.

**Late checks.** On a page edited within 72 hours, a late check starts shore like any other. On an older page it shows the crisis resources card and changes no state (§5.4, pending the owner's decision).

The editor's own states belong to C1, not to this table. These are closed or open, fenced, and an undo refused while a fold is closed. They don't interact with Shore, Quiet or Recent.

| | Normal | Shore | Quiet | Recent (overlay) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The words | As written | Unchanged; never hidden or moved | Unchanged | — |
| Writing, lifting, opening, Closer / Farther | Available | Available, without animation | Available, without animation | — |
| Materials and motion | Full | Flat warm paper, no motion | Flat, no motion | — |
| Keep and Add | Available | Available; new text is checked, and kept text is checked unless its page paragraphs already were (§5.4) | Available | Available; the trace is stamped `groundedNearKeep` |
| Setting a come-back date | Available | Available | Available | Available (when it arrives is delayed, below) |
| The come-back slip at the top | Shown when due | Hidden | Hidden | Held: the slip doesn't appear. Due traces wait in Kept under "Waiting", and each one there offers **Open** |
| Shore line: Talk now, Talk now bringing this, Find help, I'm okay | — | Shown | Hidden (Find help stays in •••) | — |
| **I'm okay**, or dismissing the card | — | Moves the page to Quiet | — | — |
| Crisis resources card | Hidden | Shown | Can be reopened from ••• | — |
| *(later, H1)* Step inside | Available | Closes at once; unavailable | Unavailable | — |
| *(later, H3)* Automatic Return | Eligible | None | None | None |
| *(later, H2)* Ask | Normal register | Grounded reply, with the shore door | Normal register | Grounded register (the SMS channel's 72-hour rule) |

**What a false activation costs.** Draft 1 called shore "cheap to be wrong about". That assumed its emotional cost. This is what observably changes when shore activates:
- the paper flattens and motion stops
- a line with crisis options appears at the top
- the crisis resources card appears
- in later phases, Step inside closes
- **for 72 hours, in every channel** *(stated in draft 4; Fable A2)*: the companion in Sessions and SMS answers in its grounded register, proactive texts are cancelled, and come-back slips are held
- **for how long:** until the writer says **I'm okay**, or five minutes after the last trigger. Without **I'm okay**, every settle of a paragraph that L1 matches restarts those five minutes.

No words are removed.

We don't know how this feels when it's wrong, during ordinary dark, historical or fictional writing. It may feel like being watched or judged. The pipeline eval reports a false-activation rate before Phase 1, with L1's reported separately from L2's because their remedies differ (§5.4), and the integrated study asks about it directly (§8). This draft proposes **no change** to the shared calibration and no bypass. **I'm okay** and the new-text rule change only how the page looks.

### 5.6 C6: Privacy and honesty

**Data flows**

| Data | Stored | Sent to | When | Kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Page text (every passage, and the distances) | Firestore `users/{uid}/folioPages` | Google Firebase | On autosave | Until the writer deletes it, or deletes the account |

| Errors | Sentry, without content | Sentry | On error | Sentry's policy |
| Analytics | None | — | — | — |

**Rules.**
- **No content in telemetry.** Folio code MUST NOT put writer text in error messages, `console` output (Sentry records console calls as breadcrumbs by default), first-party diagnostics or analytics. Diagnostics carry codes only.
- **No Google Analytics events at all.** GA shares a property with Ads (see [`src/store/groundingState.ts:63–66`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/store/groundingState.ts#L63-L66)).
- **Admin.** There's no admin view of Folio content. The admin overview may show per-user counts of pages and traces. The policy's statement that administrators can view user data stays true at the database level, and stays in the policy.
- **MCP.** The read-only MCP tools don't expose Folio.
- **Export and deletion.** Export includes everything (§6.8). Account deletion's `recursiveDelete` already covers new collections.
- **Policy.** Before Folio opens beyond admins, the policy gains sentences about Folio in all 17 languages:
  - `policy.whatWeStoreBody`: Folio's pages and kept things
  - `policy.howAiWorksBody`: the safety checks, and the second crossing: a shore activation in Folio grounds the companion in Sessions and SMS for 72 hours (§3.2)
  - `policy.devicePreferencesBody` *(draft 4; Fable A12)*: today it says drafts are the only writing kept on the device and that none of it leaves the browser. Folio adds pending saves, which do go to the account, and view state.
- **First visit.** One line appears under the blank page (`folio.firstVisit`). It can be dismissed and links to the policy.

**Honesty.**
- No word implies secrecy, forgetting or permanence (the copy test in §3.12).
- No copy understates or overstates the safety checks. They cover what's written, with the narrow exception in §5.4, and the first-visit line says so (§3.12).
- **Come back** says "You asked to come back to this". It's the writer's own request, presented as theirs, never as the app thinking of them.
- A kept trace shows exact words with exact dates. Nothing in Folio paraphrases the writer.
- Any model text (later phases) always looks different and is labelled.

**Imagery.**
- **The person never sinks.** Words settle; people don't. Near distress, no copy or visual uses drowning, falling, "let go", "disappear" or "rest".
- Distance opens space and never closes in. Copy for Apart never says "sink", "deeper" or "drop".
- The material is paper and air, not water.

A new `useGlobalConfigStatus(): 'loading' | 'ready'` provides one, with `whenGlobalConfigReady(): Promise<void>` for code outside React. Admins never wait, because `isAdmin(email)` is synchronous. *(Draft 4 adds the next three; Fable B18.)*
  - **On a root visit**, when `lastHome` is `'folio'` and the writer isn't an admin, init awaits `whenGlobalConfigReady()` before deciding. Otherwise the config may not have arrived yet, and a writer with access would land in Sessions.
  - **On a `/folio` route**, `FolioSkeleton` shows while the status is loading, for at most 4 seconds. After that, loading counts as "no access" and the route bounces. Offline, on a device that never cached the config, it would otherwise wait forever.
  - **The invitation claim** (§6.4) is read with `getIdTokenResult(true)` once, on the first visit to `/folio`, so a writer invited a minute ago doesn't wait up to an hour for their token to refresh.
- **The redirect effect** allows `/folio*` once access is known, and bounces to `/` without it.
- **`resolveSessionsHome()`** is today's lookup for the active session, extracted into a function. Init and Folio's "Sessions" door both use it. Navigating to `/` after init renders nothing today, so the door can't simply go to `/`.
- **`goToSessions(handoff?)`** resolves the Sessions home. With a handoff (shore's **Talk now, bringing this**, after **Go**), it always opens `/session/new` and leaves the text in a one-time `sessionStorage` key, which the Talk composer reads once.
- **`settings.lastHome`** (`'session' | 'folio'`, default `'session'`) is written by an effect in `App.tsx` on route changes. `SessionView` doesn't change.
- **Global hooks keep running:** `useTheme`, `useTimeAwarePalette`, `useSeasonalPalette` and `useGroundingMode`. Folio ignores the Sessions tokens they rewrite and reads its own `--folio-*` tokens.

**StarterKit.** Folio disables `heading`, `codeBlock`, `blockquote`, `bulletList`, `orderedList`, `listItem`, `horizontalRule` and `link`, and keeps `hardBreak`. In effect the document is `(paragraph | underside)+`.

**Plugins.**
- **`foldIntegrity`** enforces I1–I5 and I7.
- **`hiddenWriting`** is C1's safety net (I6). It ignores history transactions.
- **`foldView`** holds the open/closed set and the fences in plugin state, not in the document.
  - **A closed underside** is a plain ProseMirror node view with no `contentDOM`: an empty, zero-height `contenteditable="false"` element with `aria-hidden`. Its text isn't in the DOM.
  - **An open underside** is the React node view with content: the crease edge, the accessible group name, and Close/Unlift · Closer · Farther · Keep.
  - **Toggling** passes the open state to the node view as a node decoration. The node view's `update` returns `false` when it changes, so ProseMirror recreates the view in the other form.
  - It shows closed folds inside a selection as selected.
  - Toggling is a transaction with metadata only: no document change, no save and no undo step.
- **`historyGuard`** is C1 layer 3: a `filterTransaction` on history transactions only. It refuses one that would change a closed underside, then opens that underside in a follow-up transaction carrying only metadata, dispatched in a microtask after the current dispatch has finished (§5.1).
- **`inputGuard`** is C1 layer 2's event side: every deletion binding (K4), the key-repeat rule, fences and the pre-input collapse, through `handleKeyDown` and `handleDOMEvents` for `beforeinput`, `compositionstart`, `cut` and `copy`. Its handlers run before ProseMirror's own. The `cut` handler writes the visible slice to `event.clipboardData`, calls `preventDefault`, then runs `visibleDelete`.
- **`foldView`'s selection remap** (I8) is an `appendTransaction`, so it applies before the next key is read.
- **The `pid` plugin** assigns ids and fixes duplicates on the paragraph outside closed undersides (I9).

**Clipboard.**
- `transformCopied` drops closed undersides and strips fold marks.
- `clipboardTextSerializer` does the same for plain text.
- `transformPasted` strips fold marks and unwraps undersides.

**Commands.** `visibleDelete` handles Backspace, Delete, Mod+X and replacing a spanning selection by typing, pasting or dropping (through `handleTextInput`, `handlePaste` and `handleDrop`). It deletes the visible ranges only and tags the transaction `folio:visibleEdit`.

**Spike S1: hidden writing (Phase 0).** S1 builds architecture A (undersides as nodes in the page document) and runs every row of C1's table, plus the cases below, on the device matrix:
1. arrow navigation across closed and open folds, including over the empty placeholder
2. IME composition (Japanese, Korean) next to and across folds, and specifically:
   - composing right after a lifted line. The `fold` mark is non-inclusive, which sends ProseMirror down its mark-cursor path at `compositionstart` (prosemirror-view 1.41.6, `dist/index.js:3475–3482`).
   - composing right before a closed placeholder in Chrome on Windows, the same path for ProseMirror issue #1500, a selection before an uneditable element
   - starting a composition on a selection that holds a closed fold (the pre-input collapse): does the browser honor the collapsed selection?
3. Android Chrome autocorrect replacing a word next to a fold
4. iOS text replacement
5. Backspace and Delete at both edges of an underside, at the edges of a between and a beneath, and in an empty paragraph beside a closed underside: a single press, a held key (K1), a fast burst after a protection event (K2), and every deletion binding (K4). After each, a typed character must not land in any closed underside.
6. Cut from the Edit menu and the context menu, and `Shift+Delete` on Windows, across a closed fold
7. Enter at the end and at the start of a lifted line, with the fold open and closed
8. undo and redo through closed folds, from the keyboard, the browser's Edit menu and iOS shake-to-undo

10. a remote revision arriving while folds are closed, including one that removes an underside (the `folio:load` path)

- no composition glitch that loses or duplicates visible text

**Spike S2: lifting on touch.** On pointer devices Lift uses BubbleMenu (`@tiptap/react/menus`, which makes `@tiptap/extension-bubble-menu` a direct dependency). On touch screens it uses a bottom bar that appears while a selection exists. This is validated on iOS Safari 17 and 18 and on Android Chrome.

**Paths** (all under `users/{uid}/`): `folioPages/{pageId}`, `folioTraces/{traceId}/entries/{entryId}` and `folioPreferences/current`.

**Queries.** All are single-field, so no composite index is needed. `firestore.indexes.json` gains only two exemptions *(3.1)*: `folioPages.doc` and `folioPages.safety.checked` are excluded from single-field indexing, so a near-1 MiB string and a long array don't add index entries to every write. Nothing queries them.

The queries:
- Pages: `orderBy('updatedAt', 'desc').limit(50)`.
- Traces: `orderBy('keptAt', 'desc')`.
- Due come-backs: `where('comeBackAt', '<=', now).orderBy('comeBackAt')`.

**Save controller** (implements C2):
- **Timing.** A debounce of 1.2 s while the page is under 50 KB, and 3 s above, with a maximum wait of 10 s. It also flushes on `pagehide`, on `visibilitychange` to hidden, on unmount and on route change.
  - **Why it scales** *(draft 4; Fable B19)*. Each save writes the whole page synchronously to localStorage, which stores it as UTF-16, so a 900 KB page costs about 1.8 MB per write on the main thread. On a long page on a mid-range phone, a 1.2 s debounce would make typing stutter. S4's outcome 6 measures it, and the 400 KB warning drops if localStorage's quota is reached first.
- **Each save:**
  1. Create a new `revId`.
  2. Write this editor's pending entry, with its chain and the current checked set, and record whether that succeeded.
  3. Call `setDoc(pageRef, page)` as a full overwrite, without `merge`. The page includes the current checked set.
  4. Handle the promise: it resolves on server acknowledgement and rejects on a rules denial.
  5. On a rejection, stop sending, wait for this page's queue to drain, and call `classifyRejection` once for the chain (§5.2). Later rejections from the same chain are logged and ignored. Never make a conflict copy before it answers.
- **Status** is derived from two things: whether the pending entry holds the current content, and whether the acknowledged `revId` equals the current one.
- **The snapshot listener** uses `includeMetadataChanges: true`. It never loads remote content over local edits. It treats only server-confirmed snapshots as the server's state, meaning `fromCache` is false and `hasPendingWrites` is false. Remote content is compared by `revId` and handled through conflict copies. When it does load content, the load is a `folio:load` transaction (§5.1).
- **The lock.** The editor is editable only while this tab holds the page's Web Lock (§5.2). A tab without it waits for it in the queue.

**Security rules.** `folioPages` and `folioTraces` are carved out of the generic owner rule. Firestore unions every matching `allow`, so a more specific rule can't take access away.

```
match /users/{userId}/{collectionId}/{document=**} {
  allow read: if isOwner(userId);
  allow write: if isOwner(userId)
    && !(collectionId in ['smsSettings', 'smsMessages', 'folioPages', 'folioTraces']);
}

// Phase 1: compare-and-swap on the revision chain (C2)
match /users/{userId}/folioPages/{pageId} {
  allow read: if isOwner(userId);
  allow create: if isOwner(userId) && validPage(pageId)
    && request.resource.data.parentRevId == null;
  allow update: if isOwner(userId) && validPage(pageId)
    && request.resource.data.parentRevId == resource.data.revId
    && request.resource.data.revId != resource.data.revId;
  allow delete: if isOwner(userId);
}

// Phase 2: kept fields can't be updated (C3)
match /users/{userId}/folioTraces/{traceId} {
  allow read: if isOwner(userId);
  allow create: if isOwner(userId) && validTrace(traceId);
  allow update: if isOwner(userId)
    && request.resource.data.diff(resource.data).affectedKeys()
         .hasOnly(['title', 'comeBackAt', 'comeBackTz', 'comeBackSetAt', 'lastComeBackShownAt', 'safety']);
  allow delete: if isOwner(userId);

  match /entries/{entryId} {
    allow read: if isOwner(userId);
    allow create: if isOwner(userId)
      && request.resource.data.id == entryId
      && request.resource.data.author == 'writer'
      && request.resource.data.text is string
      && request.resource.data.text.size() > 0
      && request.resource.data.text.size() <= 20000;
    allow delete: if isOwner(userId);
  }
}
```

**Validation functions.** `validPage` requires:
- `id == pageId`
- `doc` is a string of at most 950,000 characters
- `revId` is a string of at most 40 characters
- the `parentRevId` key is present (it's null on create), so the rule can read it
- `safety.checked` is a list of at most 12,000 entries. This is a backstop only: the size guard, which counts the set, always stops a page first (§5.2). Draft 3's 2,000 could have rejected every save of a long page of short paragraphs

The probe in Appendix I ran this rule, minus the last clause, on the emulator.

`validTrace` checks the id, `author == 'writer'`, string sizes, `gap` as an integer from 0 to 4, and `safety.checked` as a list of at most 1,000 entries.

### 6.7 Safety integration

```ts
class FolioSafetyWatch {
  constructor(deps: {
    l1: (text: string) => boolean                                   // detectCrisisKeywords, on units and adjacent pairs
    check: (sample: string, lang: string) => Promise<CrisisCheck>   // checkCrisisRisk
    activate: (trigger: string) => void                             // activateGrounding('auto'); the trigger feeds §5.5
    lateRisk: () => void                                            // a late risk-2 on a page not edited for 72 h: the card only (§5.4)
    isLockHolder: () => boolean                                     // only the lock holder checks
    pageUpdatedAt: () => number                                     // as of opening, for the late-check rule
    now: () => number
    language: () => string
    readingOrder: () => Unit[]                                      // the composition, in Reading view order
    checked: CheckedSet                                             // the page's set; saved with the page
  })
  settle(pid: string): void
  paste(pids: string[]): void
  hidden(): void                                                    // pagehide / hidden / route change: L1 on dirty units
  sweep(): void                                                     // open, hydrate, remote revision: schedule every unchecked unit
  forget(pid: string): void                                         // the unit was deleted
}

type Unit = { pid: string; text: string; line?: string }            // line: the lifted line, for underside units
```

**`samples.ts`** is pure: `buildSamples(units, index) → string[]`. It builds the window over reading order, adds the line anchor, and applies the windows for long units. **`checkedSet.ts`** is pure: `checkKey(unit)` over the `pid`, the line and the text, and `unchecked(units, set)`.

**`useShore()`** subscribes through `useGroundingMode()`. `visit.ts` tracks when a visit ends: on reload, or after the tab has been hidden for 30 minutes. Folio doesn't change `groundingState.ts`, `crisisFloor.ts` or the classifier's behavior.

**Tests** use fake timers and a fake `check`:
- **Two units are both checked.** Paragraph A settles while a call is in flight, then paragraph B settles, and both are classified. This is the case Codex described.
- **Coalescing.** Revisions of the same unit coalesce.
- **The same words in two places** *(3.1)*. Two units with identical text and different `pid`s are both checked.
- **Short units** *(3.1)*. A five-character beneath under a long line is sent to L2, because its sample, which includes the line, is over 20 characters. A five-character unit on an otherwise empty page isn't.
- **Unavailable checks.** They stay pending and retry with backoff, and never enter the checked set.
- **Edits while a check is in flight** *(new; Gemini, R8)*. The writer adds to paragraph A while its check is in flight. The returning result is discarded as stale, and A is checked again with its new text.
- **Closed before settling** *(new)*. A unit is typed and the page is hidden within 2 seconds. L1 runs synchronously, the key stays out of the saved set, and a new watch built from the saved page schedules it.
- **Another device** *(new)*. A page saved with unchecked units is opened on a second device, and every unchecked unit is scheduled there.
- **Reading order** *(new)*. A beneath's sample contains its line and its between. A paragraph inserted above one already checked has the later paragraph in its own window.
- **Coverage of long text.** The windows cover a 20,000-character paste with the risk sentence at any offset, and every window carries the line anchor where there is one.
- **Limits.** At most 10 calls a minute for settles and 6 for sweeps and pastes. L1 runs synchronously on settle, on paste and when the page is hidden.
- *(draft 4)* **A sentence split by Enter.** L1 matches the pair, and the trigger is the pair.
- *(draft 4)* **Late checks.** A late risk-2 on a page edited within 72 hours activates; on an older page it calls `lateRisk` and doesn't activate.
- *(draft 4)* **Only the lock holder.** A read-only tab schedules nothing.
- *(draft 4)* **Rate limited.** A `rate_limited` result is unavailable, backs off longer, and never enters the checked set.
- *(draft 4)* **Empty paragraphs** have no key and send nothing.
- *(draft 4)* **Shore presentation** (`shorePresentation.ts`): **I'm okay** moves to Quiet; an activation from a trigger already shown in this visit stays in Quiet; a new trigger returns to Shore; a reload within 30 minutes of an activation starts in Quiet.

## 8. Evaluation

**What we're learning.** Phase P answers whether the operation creates relationships people wouldn't otherwise make (§4). Phase 3 answers whether the integrated version is trustworthy and worth returning to. Neither asks whether people stay longer.

**Owner dogfooding.** Two weeks in Phase P, and the whole of Phase 3. The owner keeps a short private log of moments that felt like making something, and moments that felt like being managed.

**Integrated study (Phase 3).**
- **Participants and setting:** 6 to 8 invited adults, over 3 to 4 weeks, on their own devices. At least two use assistive technology every day *(draft 4)*.
- **Material:** their own writing. Low stakes are encouraged, and no difficult disclosure is asked for.
- **What we look at:**
  - **Preservation.** Any lost or changed words. The tolerance is zero, and every incident gets a root cause.
  - **Conflicts and remote deletes.** How often they happened, and whether the notices made sense.
  - **Accessibility.** Whether participants who use assistive technology completed what they wanted to do.
  - **False shore activations.** Participants report them. The code-only counts of shore activations, **I'm okay** presses and late risk results in Folio are compared with what participants say happened.
  - **Return.** Did come-backs lead to writing beside the original? Did people choose dates?
  - **Making versus keeping.** Do people keep without writing beneath, between or beside?
- **Questions:**
  - "What did the fold mean to you?"
  - "Did the distance feel like your choice?"
  - "Did anything make you feel interpreted, watched or managed?"
  - "Was there a moment you wanted to leave and couldn't find how?"

**Kill or redesign if:**
- the fold consistently reads as the app trying to expose a hidden truth
- Apart reads as decoration
- people can't find the way out
- keeping becomes more attractive than making
- shore false positives during ordinary dark writing happen often enough to feel like being watched (participants say so, or the rate is above the threshold the owner sets, §11)
- there's any preservation incident that a contract change can't fix

**Not measured:** time in the app, visits, streaks, words written or the number of folds. Telemetry is limited to codes.

## 9. Later hypotheses

Each of these has to earn inclusion on its own. Each needs a one-page spec, a claim tied to §2.1, a test, and a decision. None of them is a scheduled phase.

### H1: Step inside

- **Claim.** Standing inside a composition, as a full-screen space with light, changes how it's encountered.
- **Design notes.**
  - The standing distance is labelled **Stand closer** / **Stand back**, so it isn't confused with Apart. It's a viewing distance and isn't stored anywhere.
  - **Back to the page** restores the exact previous selection and scroll. **Back and write** puts the caret at the end of the beneath.
  - It's unavailable in Shore and Quiet.
  - It's a modal dialog, with the focus trap and `inert` root that Room uses.
- **Sound.** Sound is a further sub-hypothesis.
  - It reuses Room's `forest` and `drone` tracks. `ocean` is excluded by the imagery guardrail.
  - Web Audio provides the distance; the fallback is volume only.
  - The seed-audio bucket allows `https://undersurface.me` (checked 2026-10-15); the native origins are unverified.
  - Spike S3 would cover iOS and native.
- **Risk.** Spectacle over writing.

### H2: The margin (Ask)

- **What it is.** The companion, by invitation. The writer selects text and chooses **Ask**, and a short note in graphite appears in the margin, labelled "Companion".
- **What it sees.** Only the selection and which passage it came from.
- **Voice.** The kernel gets a new `'page'` channel, and the plain-voice rules apply.
- **Contract.** §5.4's Ask contract.
- **The writer's options.** A note can be dismissed (never stored), kept (labelled as the companion's) or copied.
- **Crossing.** **Bring into a session** reuses Phase 1's handoff.
- **Eval.** `test:folio`, with about 30 cases: contradiction pairs, playful pairs, grief, dark humor, injection attempts and non-English text.
- **Risk.** It turns Folio back into a companion.

### H3: Automatic Return (the tide)

- **What it is.** Kept things come back unasked.
- **Kept separate from Keep.**
  - **The global default is off.** The writer turns it on in Folio settings.
  - Each trace has its own "May come back on its own" switch, which is also off until the writer turns it on.
- **Eligibility** (draft 1's rules):
  - at least 14 days after keeping
  - not shown in the last 60 days
  - anniversaries first
  - never within 72 hours of grounding
- **Claim.** An unasked return is meaningful, not intrusive.
- **Risk.** It feels like the app deciding what you should remember.

### H4: Delayed answers

- **What it was.** Draft 1's Overnight.
- **Condition.** It's included only if it can say what the delay contributes beyond withholding an immediate reply. One candidate gives the delay a job: after a "Tomorrow" come-back, once the writer has written beside the trace, they may Ask (H2). That involves no delayed generation at all.
- **Safety.** If any held generation remains, it follows §5.4's deferred-display rule.
- **Copy.** It never says "I'll think about it overnight".

### H5: The edition

- **What it is.** A printable book of chosen traces: one per page, each with its composition, distance, dates, title and entries.
- **Format.** Printed through the browser's print dialog. A5 is the default size.
- **Contents.** No generated text, except labelled companion notes that the writer chose to include.

### H6: Spatial and material experiments

| ID | Experiment | Status |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | The long room: undersides inside undersides | Partly served by unlimited beneaths; nesting stays an experiment |
| E2 | The blank between | **Promoted to the core as Apart** |
| E3 | A prism you turn: two or three readings of one line, shown one at a time | **The alternative authored operation** if Phase P rejects Apart |
| E4 | A life not lived: an explicitly fictional annex | Experiment |
| E5 | Undertext: deleted sentences kept on the device as faint silt | Experiment; privacy question |
| E6 | Breath tide: the page's edge moves with the breath, using a heart-rate strap | Experiment; risk of biometrics being treated as truth |
| E7 | A specimen grown from a kept fold's own shape | Experiment; risk of drifting into collectibles |

### H7: Search

- **What it is.** A support feature.
- **Test.** Does it help the writer get back to their own material without becoming the center of the experience (§2.1)?
- **Status.** A candidate for after Phase 3.

## 11. Open questions for the owner

1. **Name.** "Folio" (a sheet folded once to make two leaves). Alternatives: "Underside", "Below", "Leaf".
2. **Memory.** Should a writer ever be able to opt a page into Sessions' memory? The default is no.
3. **Admin visibility.** Counts only. Confirm?
4. **Search.** A later hypothesis (H7). Agree?
5. **Bold and italic.** Keep them in pages, or keep pages plain text?
6. **Leaving shore** *(changed in draft 4)*. Folio's **I'm okay** moves the page to Quiet and leaves the shared grounding state to end on its own (§3.9). Agree? The alternatives are Fable's proposal, a shared exit that calls `deactivateGrounding()` and so also ungrounds the companion in this tab, or no exit at all. Recommended: agree. The page is believed, and the safety stack is untouched.
7. **After the study.** Open Folio to everyone, or keep it an invited, art-piece mode? For the Phase 3 invitations, use an Auth custom claim (recommended) or a uid list in `appConfig`?
8. **Consent.** Does the policy change require re-consent?
9. **Platform.** Polish desktop first or phone first in Phase 1?
10. **Come back.** Offer it right after Keep (as proposed), or only from the trace?
11. **Phase P logistics.**
    - Who moderates?
    - Recruiting nine people.
    - Local only, or a Firebase preview channel for the prototypes (an infrastructure change)? Question 18 depends on it.
12. **False-activation tolerance.** The rate of shore activations per participant-week above which shore counts as "too often" (§8).
13. **The distances.** The five stops start at 0, 1, 3, 7 and 15 line-heights. This is a matter of taste, to be settled after Phase P.
14. **Phase P's rules** *(rewritten in draft 4)*. §4.4 proposes them. Set the final numbers and commit them before the first session. Who is the second coder?
15. **Late checks** *(changed in draft 4)*. Text never checked by L2 is checked when its page is next opened (§5.4). Every result is recorded. What should a risk-2 result do?
    - **Recommended:** start shore if the page was edited within the last 72 hours. On an older page, show the crisis resources card only: no flattening, no shore line, and no `lastGroundingAt`, so nothing reaches SMS or Sessions.
    - **The alternative:** start shore for every result, as draft 3.1 did. It's simpler, and one of Fable's two reviewers preferred it. But it can flatten a page, and change the companion's register in SMS for three days, over a paragraph written weeks ago.
16. **The classifier's own limit** *(new in draft 4)*. Give the chat function's `crisis_check` and `signal_check` routes their own per-user limiter, so classifier traffic and the therapist's never block each other (§5.4)? It's a change to the functions, and it makes Sessions' own safety check safer as well. Recommended: yes.
17. **Who are the nine** *(new)*. Recommended: people who already write for themselves at least weekly (§4.3).
18. **Do participants take the prototype home** for the two weeks, on their own phones? Recommended: yes, with the consent sheet, so D5a measures a real return. If not, D5a becomes "Re-encounter" (§4.4).
19. **Scope** *(new)*. Phase 1 in English for admins, with translation in Phase 3's build; corrected copies after Phase 3; no two-tab handover. Recommended: yes to all three (§7).
20. **Lift into today's page** *(new)*. Build it in Phase 2 only if D5b passes (§3.8, §4.4)? Recommended: yes.

Draft 1's questions about the sound palette, native audio CORS and writing inside Step inside now belong to H1.

## Appendix C: Background for reviewers new to the codebase

- **Sessions.** Opening the app drops the writer into a session with an AI companion, across four surfaces:
  - **Talk:** streamed text.
  - **Journal:** a quiet editor where optional "inner voices" can respond on pause.
  - **Speak:** live voice.
  - **Room:** ambient sound and voice, or quiet company.

  An optional SMS channel carries the same companion between sessions.
- **The crisis stack.**
  - **L1** is a narrow, deterministic regex floor for sincere, present, first-person methods and plans.
  - **L2** is a gpt-5-nano classifier tuned for precision, which runs on every path the user writes. It fails open to risk 0.
  - Either layer activates **grounding**: a global, in-memory state.
    - It changes every prompt to a grounded register and suppresses companion programs.
    - It shows a crisis resources card.
    - It saves a timestamp that the SMS channel reads for 72 hours.
  - The classifier is deliberately tuned against over-calling. A previous version flagged grief, metaphor and gallows humor.
- **Fresh eyes.** A per-session switch that keeps durable memory out of the companion's context.
- **The parts ontology** (decided 2026-07-04). The seven seeded parts belong to the companion. The writer's own parts are a separate, consent-based map. Folio uses neither.
- **Feature flags** live in `appConfig/global.features`. The server rejects unknown keys, so functions deploy before hosting.
- **Deploy order:** indexes, then rules, then functions, then hosting.

### F.1 The L2 classifier fails open to the same value as a real negative ([`src/ai/openrouter.ts:452–516`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/ai/openrouter.ts#L452-L516))

This motivates the additive `checkCrisisRisk` in C4 (§5.4). Every failure path returns `{ risk: 0 }`, which is indistinguishable from a successful "no risk".

```ts
 * Synchronous L2 crisis classifier — multilingual, narrow-by-construction.
 *
 * Calls the chat function with `task: 'crisis_check'`, which routes to a
 * smaller/faster model with structured-output enforcement. Risk tier semantics
 * are documented in crisisClassifierPrompt.ts.
 *
 * Fails open on timeout / network error / parse failure: returns {risk: 0}.
 * The L1 regex layer in sessionOrchestrator already ran by the time we get
 * here, so falling open leaves the existing safety net intact. The
 * alternative — bricking message send on transient API failure — is worse.
 *
 * Used by partOrchestrator for diary-mode crisis classification. In session
 * mode, L2 is folded into the therapist stream as a `<<RISK:N>>` preamble.
 */
export async function classifyCrisisRisk(
  text: string,
  language?: string,
): Promise<CrisisRiskResult> {
  const token = await getAuthToken()
  const controller = new AbortController()
  const timeout = setTimeout(() => controller.abort(), 5000)

  // Recent slice is enough — explicit ideation tends to appear in the most
  // recent paragraph, and a tighter input keeps latency + cost down.
  const sample = text.slice(-1000)
  const userContent = language && language !== 'en'
    ? `Language: ${language}\n\nText:\n${sample}`
    : sample

  try {
    const response = await fetch(apiUrl('/api/chat'), {
      method: 'POST',
      headers: {
        'Authorization': `Bearer ${token}`,
        'Content-Type': 'application/json',
      },
      body: JSON.stringify({
        task: 'crisis_check',
        messages: [
          { role: 'system', content: CRISIS_CLASSIFIER_PROMPT },
          { role: 'user', content: userContent },
        ],
      }),
      signal: controller.signal,
    })

    if (!response.ok) return { risk: 0 }

    const data = await response.json()
    const content = data?.choices?.[0]?.message?.content
    if (typeof content !== 'string') return { risk: 0 }

    const parsed = JSON.parse(content) as { risk?: unknown; reason?: unknown }
    const risk = parsed.risk
    if (risk !== 0 && risk !== 1 && risk !== 2) return { risk: 0 }
    return {
      risk: risk as CrisisRisk,
      reason: typeof parsed.reason === 'string' ? parsed.reason : undefined,
    }
  } catch {
    return { risk: 0 }
  } finally {
    clearTimeout(timeout)
  }
}
```

### F.2 Grounding is a global in-memory state with an auto-exit ([`src/store/groundingState.ts:1–86`](https://github.com/zohoora/undersurface/blob/35f8297/src/store/groundingState.ts#L1-L86))

C5's "Shore" state is exactly this state. Folio subscribes to it and adds nothing to it. The activation sinks are how `lastGroundingAt` (the 72-hour "Recent" overlay) gets persisted.

```ts
import { getGlobalConfig } from './globalConfig'
import { engineLog } from '../services/diagnostics'

// Module-level state
let groundingActive = false
let autoExitTimer: ReturnType<typeof setTimeout> | null = null

const listeners = new Set<() => void>()
// Fired on EVERY activation request (first activation and re-triggers alike),
// regardless of which layer detected distress: L1 floor, raced L2 classifier,
// in-stream <<RISK:N>> preamble, voice/Room transcripts. Used to persist a
// server-readable crisis timestamp. Sinks run best-effort and can never throw
// into the crisis path.
type ActivationSink = (at: number) => void | Promise<unknown>
const activationSinks = new Set<ActivationSink>()

export function onGroundingActivated(sink: ActivationSink): () => void {
  activationSinks.add(sink)
  return () => { activationSinks.delete(sink) }
}

function notifyActivation() {
  const at = Date.now()
  for (const sink of activationSinks) {
    try {
      const result = sink(at)
      if (result && typeof (result as Promise<unknown>).catch === 'function') {
        void (result as Promise<unknown>).catch(() => { /* best-effort */ })
      }
    } catch {
      // A sink failure must never reach the caller that detected distress.
    }
  }
}

function notify() {
  for (const fn of listeners) fn()
}

export function subscribeGrounding(callback: () => void) {
  listeners.add(callback)
  return () => { listeners.delete(callback) }
}

function resetAutoExit() {
  if (autoExitTimer) clearTimeout(autoExitTimer)
  const config = getGlobalConfig()
  const minutes = config?.grounding?.autoExitMinutes ?? 5
  autoExitTimer = setTimeout(() => {
    deactivateGrounding()
  }, minutes * 60 * 1000)
}

export function activateGrounding(trigger: 'auto' | 'manual' = 'auto'): void {
  if (groundingActive) {
    // Re-trigger resets the auto-exit timer
    resetAutoExit()
    notifyActivation()
    return
  }
  groundingActive = true
  document.documentElement.setAttribute('data-grounding', 'true')
  // First-party diagnostics only. Crisis-state signals must never reach
  // Google Analytics: GA is keyed to the user id and shares a property with
  // Ads conversions, so a grounding event there attaches sensitive-category
  // health data to an advertising identifier.
  engineLog('info', 'grounding.activated', 'grounding mode activated', { extra: { trigger } })
  notify()
  resetAutoExit()
  notifyActivation()
}

export function deactivateGrounding(): void {
  if (!groundingActive) return
  groundingActive = false
  document.documentElement.removeAttribute('data-grounding')
  if (autoExitTimer) {
    clearTimeout(autoExitTimer)
    autoExitTimer = null
  }
  notify()
}

export function isGroundingActive(): boolean {
  return groundingActive
}
```

export function writeJournalDraft(
  uid: string,
  sessionId: string,
  draft: { text: string; messageId?: string },
  writerEpoch: number = epoch,

    }

      text: draft.text,
      ...(draft.messageId ? { messageId: draft.messageId } : {}),
      updatedAt: Date.now(),
    }
    localStorage.setItem(key, JSON.stringify(stored))
    return true
  } catch {
    return false
  }
}

### F.9 Why a restore in `appendTransaction` joins the same undo event (prosemirror-history 1.5.0, `dist/index.js`)

This code isn't in the repository; it's the installed dependency. An appended transaction never starts a new undo group (`newGroup` requires `!appended`), so C1's safety net and the edit that triggered it are undone together. Appendix G's step 3 confirms this.

```js
function applyTransaction(history, state, tr, options) {
    let historyTr = tr.getMeta(historyKey), rebased;
    if (historyTr)
        return historyTr.historyState;
    if (tr.getMeta(closeHistoryKey))
        history = new HistoryState(history.done, history.undone, null, 0, -1);
    let appended = tr.getMeta("appendedTransaction");
    if (tr.steps.length == 0) {
        return history;
    }
    else if (appended && appended.getMeta(historyKey)) {
        if (appended.getMeta(historyKey).redo)
            return new HistoryState(history.done.addTransform(tr, undefined, options, mustPreserveItems(state)), history.undone, rangesFor(tr.mapping.maps), history.prevTime, history.prevComposition);
        else
            return new HistoryState(history.done, history.undone.addTransform(tr, undefined, options, mustPreserveItems(state)), null, history.prevTime, history.prevComposition);
    }
    else if (tr.getMeta("addToHistory") !== false && !(appended && appended.getMeta("addToHistory") === false)) {
        // Group transforms that occur in quick succession into one event.
        let composition = tr.getMeta("composition");
        let newGroup = history.prevTime == 0 ||
            (!appended && history.prevComposition != composition &&
                (history.prevTime < (tr.time || 0) - options.newGroupDelay || !isAdjacentTo(tr, history.prevRanges)));
```

### probe.cjs

```js
// Spike S4 probe: does the Firestore JS SDK (as installed) satisfy the two
// assumptions Folio draft 2 relies on, and does it coalesce queued writes?
const fs = require('fs')
const path = require('path')
const REPO = ''   // run from a directory where the packages are installed
const { initializeTestEnvironment } = require(REPO + '@firebase/rules-unit-testing')
const sdkVersion = require(REPO + 'firebase/package.json').version

const page = (id, revId, parentRevId, doc) => ({ id, revId, parentRevId, doc })
const settle = (p, ms = 8000) => Promise.race([
  p.then(() => 'ok', (e) => 'rejected:' + (e && e.code)),
  new Promise((r) => setTimeout(() => r('hung'), ms)),
])
const results = []
const check = (name, actual, expected) => {
  const pass = JSON.stringify(actual) === JSON.stringify(expected)
  results.push({ name, pass, actual, expected })
}

async function main() {
  const env = await initializeTestEnvironment({
    projectId: 'demo-folio-s4',
    firestore: { host: '127.0.0.1', port: 8087, rules: fs.readFileSync(path.join(__dirname, 'folio.rules'), 'utf8') },
  })
  const A = env.authenticatedContext('alice').firestore()
  const B = env.authenticatedContext('alice').firestore()   // a second device, same writer
  const refA = (id) => A.doc(`users/alice/folioPages/${id}`)
  const refB = (id) => B.doc(`users/alice/folioPages/${id}`)
  const server = async (id) => (await refB(id).get({ source: 'server' })).data()

  // 1. Pipelining, no coalescing: two chained offline writes are both accepted.
  await refA('p1').set(page('p1', 'r1', null, 'A'))
  await A.disableNetwork()
  const w2 = settle(refA('p1').set(page('p1', 'r2', 'r1', 'AB')))
  const w3 = settle(refA('p1').set(page('p1', 'r3', 'r2', 'ABC')))
  await A.enableNetwork()
  check('chained offline writes r1→r2→r3 both accepted', [await w2, await w3], ['ok', 'ok'])
  check('server ends at r3', (await server('p1')).revId, 'r3')

  // 2. Rejected chain: device B moves the page on; A's queued chain is rejected
  //    entirely, later writes are still sent, and A's cache rolls back.
  await refA('p2').set(page('p2', 'r1', null, 'A'))
  const seen = []
  const unsub = refA('p2').onSnapshot({ includeMetadataChanges: true }, (s) => seen.push({ rev: s.data()?.revId, pending: s.metadata.hasPendingWrites, cache: s.metadata.fromCache }))
  await A.disableNetwork()
  const x2 = settle(refA('p2').set(page('p2', 'r2', 'r1', 'A-offline-1')))
  const x3 = settle(refA('p2').set(page('p2', 'r3', 'r2', 'A-offline-2')))
  const other = settle(refA('p3').set(page('p3', 'q1', null, 'unrelated')))   // queued after the doomed chain
  const localBefore = (await refA('p2').get({ source: 'cache' })).data().revId
  await refB('p2').set(page('p2', 'r9', 'r1', 'B-online'))                    // B wins the race
  await A.enableNetwork()
  check('A sees its own queued write locally while offline', localBefore, 'r3')
  check('A chain rejected: first write', await x2, 'rejected:permission-denied')
  check('A chain rejected: second write (built on the rejected one)', await x3, 'rejected:permission-denied')
  check('a write queued after a rejected one is still sent (assumption 1)', await other, 'ok')
  await new Promise((r) => setTimeout(r, 300))
  const after = (await refA('p2').get({ source: 'cache' })).data()
  check('A cache rolled back to the server value (assumption 2)', after.revId, 'r9')
  check('last snapshot has no pending writes', seen[seen.length - 1].pending, false)
  check('server still r9', (await server('p2')).revId, 'r9')
  unsub()

  // 3. Edit versus delete: an offline edit after a remote delete is not recreated.
  await refA('p4').set(page('p4', 'r1', null, 'A'))
  await A.disableNetwork()
  const y2 = settle(refA('p4').set(page('p4', 'r2', 'r1', 'A-offline')))
  await refB('p4').delete()
  await A.enableNetwork()
  check('offline edit after remote delete is rejected', await y2, 'rejected:permission-denied')
  check('page stays deleted', (await refB('p4').get({ source: 'server' })).exists, false)

  // 4. permission-denied is ambiguous: a write whose parent matches but which
  //    fails another clause (oversized) returns the same code as a conflict.
  await refA('p5').set(page('p5', 'r1', null, 'A'))
  const big = settle(refA('p5').set(page('p5', 'r2', 'r1', 'x'.repeat(950001))))
  check('oversized write with a matching parent: same code as a conflict', await big, 'rejected:permission-denied')
  check('server unchanged at the parent (so it is NOT a conflict)', (await server('p5')).revId, 'r1')

  await env.cleanup()
  console.log(`firebase JS SDK ${sdkVersion}`)
  for (const r of results) console.log(`${r.pass ? 'PASS' : 'FAIL'}  ${r.name}${r.pass ? '' : `  (got ${JSON.stringify(r.actual)}, expected ${JSON.stringify(r.expected)})`}`)
  process.exit(results.every((r) => r.pass) ? 0 : 1)
}
main().catch((e) => { console.error(e); process.exit(2) })
```

**Reading it.**

- **Check 3** is why §5.2's on-load rule waits for server-confirmed state. While offline, the device's own cache already shows its queued revision.
- **Checks 4 to 9** are S4's outcome 4 and the two assumptions.
- **Checks 10 and 11** are outcome 5.
